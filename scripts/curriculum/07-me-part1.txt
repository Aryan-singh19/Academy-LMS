@ me302 | ME-302 | Thermodynamics | Basic concepts, first and second laws, entropy, properties of steam, gas cycles and availability.
# Basic Concepts & Zeroth Law
- System, Surroundings & Properties | Thermodynamics studies energy interactions of systems. | Closed System = mass fixed, energy may cross the boundary | Open System = both mass and energy cross the boundary (control volume) | Intensive Property = independent of mass, e.g. pressure and temperature
- State, Process & Cycle | Processes change the state of a system. | Quasi-static Process = infinitely slow process passing through equilibrium states | Thermodynamic Cycle = series of processes returning to the initial state | Point Function = property depending only on state, not path
- Zeroth Law & Temperature | Thermal equilibrium defines temperature. | Zeroth Law = two bodies in equilibrium with a third are in equilibrium with each other | Thermometric Property = property that varies with temperature | Absolute Temperature = Kelvin scale, T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15
- Work & Heat | Work and heat are energy in transit. | Displacement Work = W = ∫ p dV | Path Function = quantity depending on the path, e.g. work and heat | Sign Convention = heat added and work done by system are positive
# First Law of Thermodynamics
- First Law for Closed Systems | Energy is conserved in every process. | First Law for a Cycle = net heat equals net work | Internal Energy = energy stored in molecular motion; a property | First Law for a Process = Q − W = ΔU
- Steady Flow Energy Equation | Open systems use the SFEE. | SFEE = h1 + V1²/2 + gz1 + q = h2 + V2²/2 + gz2 + w | Enthalpy = h = u + pv | Steady Flow = properties at each point do not change with time
- Applications of SFEE | Devices are analysed with SFEE. | Nozzle = increases fluid velocity at the expense of enthalpy | Turbine = produces work as fluid expands | Throttling = isenthalpic process, h1 = h2
- PMM1 & Ideal Gas Processes | First law forbids free energy. | PMM1 = perpetual motion machine of first kind, impossible | Isothermal Work = W = p1V1 ln(V2/V1) | Polytropic Process = pV^n = constant
# Second Law & Entropy
- Heat Engines & Heat Pumps | Second law limits conversion of heat into work. | Thermal Efficiency = η = W/Q1 | COP of Refrigerator = Q2/W | COP Relation = COP(HP) = COP(R) + 1
- Kelvin-Planck & Clausius Statements | Two equivalent statements of the second law. | Kelvin-Planck Statement = no engine can convert all heat from a single reservoir into work | Clausius Statement = heat cannot flow from cold to hot without external work | PMM2 = perpetual motion machine of second kind, impossible
- Carnot Cycle | Carnot cycle is the most efficient between two temperatures. | Carnot Efficiency = η = 1 − T2/T1 | Carnot Theorem = no engine is more efficient than a reversible engine | Reversible Process = can be reversed leaving no trace on surroundings
- Entropy | Entropy measures irreversibility. | Clausius Inequality = cyclic integral of dQ/T ≤ 0 | Entropy Change = dS = dQrev/T | Principle of Increase of Entropy = entropy of an isolated system never decreases
# Properties of Pure Substances
- Phase Change of Water | Pure substances change phase at fixed conditions. | Saturation Temperature = boiling temperature at a given pressure | Critical Point = state where liquid and vapour become indistinguishable, 22.06 MPa for water | Triple Point = state where solid, liquid and vapour coexist
- Dryness Fraction & Steam Tables | Wet steam properties use dryness fraction. | Dryness Fraction = x = mass of vapour / total mass | Wet Steam Enthalpy = h = hf + x·hfg | Superheated Steam = steam above saturation temperature
- T-s & h-s Diagrams | Property charts aid analysis. | Mollier Diagram = h-s chart for steam | T-s Diagram = area under curve represents heat transfer | Isentropic Process = vertical line on T-s and h-s diagrams
- Measurement of Dryness Fraction | Calorimeters measure steam quality. | Throttling Calorimeter = throttles steam to superheat to find x | Separating Calorimeter = separates water droplets mechanically | Combined Calorimeter = separating plus throttling for wet steam
# Gas Power Cycles & Availability
- Air Standard Cycles | Engine cycles are idealised with air. | Otto Cycle Efficiency = η = 1 − 1/r^(γ−1) | Diesel Cycle = heat added at constant pressure | Dual Cycle = heat added partly at constant volume and partly at constant pressure
- Brayton Cycle | Gas turbines follow the Brayton cycle. | Brayton Cycle = two isentropic and two isobaric processes | Brayton Efficiency = η = 1 − 1/rp^((γ−1)/γ) | Regeneration = exhaust heats compressed air to improve efficiency
- Mean Effective Pressure | MEP compares engine work output. | Mean Effective Pressure = net work divided by swept volume | Swept Volume = displacement volume of the piston | Compression Ratio = total volume / clearance volume
- Availability & Irreversibility | Exergy is the useful part of energy. | Availability = maximum useful work obtainable relative to the dead state | Irreversibility = I = T0 × entropy generated | Dead State = system in equilibrium with environment

@ me303 | ME-303 | Materials Technology | Crystal structure, imperfections, phase diagrams, heat treatment, ferrous and non-ferrous alloys and material testing.
# Crystal Structure & Imperfections
- Crystal Structures | Metals arrange atoms in repeating lattices. | BCC = body-centred cubic, APF 0.68, e.g. α-iron | FCC = face-centred cubic, APF 0.74, e.g. aluminium | HCP = hexagonal close-packed, APF 0.74, e.g. zinc
- Miller Indices | Planes and directions are labelled by Miller indices. | Miller Indices = reciprocals of intercepts reduced to integers | Slip Plane = close-packed plane on which dislocations move | Coordination Number = number of nearest neighbours of an atom
- Crystal Imperfections | Real crystals contain defects. | Vacancy = missing atom at a lattice site | Dislocation = line defect enabling plastic deformation | Grain Boundary = interface between differently oriented grains
- Deformation & Strengthening | Plastic deformation occurs by slip and twinning. | Slip = shear displacement along slip planes | Work Hardening = strength rises with plastic deformation | Grain Refinement = smaller grains increase strength (Hall-Petch)
# Phase Diagrams
- Solid Solutions & Phase Rule | Alloys form phases governed by rules. | Substitutional Solid Solution = solute replaces solvent atoms | Interstitial Solid Solution = small solute atoms fit in gaps | Gibbs Phase Rule = F = C − P + 2
- Binary Phase Diagrams | Diagrams show phases versus temperature and composition. | Isomorphous System = complete solubility in liquid and solid, e.g. Cu-Ni | Lever Rule = finds phase fractions in a two-phase region | Eutectic Reaction = liquid → two solid phases at one temperature
- Iron-Carbon Diagram | Fe-Fe3C diagram is the basis of steel heat treatment. | Eutectoid Point = 0.8% C at 727 °C forming pearlite | Austenite = FCC γ-iron solid solution of carbon | Cementite = iron carbide Fe3C, hard and brittle
- Microstructures of Steel & Cast Iron | Carbon content sets microstructure. | Pearlite = lamellar ferrite and cementite | Hypoeutectoid Steel = less than 0.8% C; ferrite plus pearlite | Cast Iron = iron with more than 2.11% carbon
# Heat Treatment
- TTT Diagrams | TTT curves predict transformation products. | TTT Diagram = time-temperature-transformation curve for isothermal cooling | Critical Cooling Rate = minimum rate to form martensite | Bainite = product of transformation between pearlite and martensite ranges
- Annealing & Normalising | Softening and refining treatments. | Annealing = heat and slow furnace cool to soften | Normalising = heat and air cool to refine grain | Spheroidising = forms spherical carbides for machinability
- Hardening & Tempering | Quenching hardens; tempering toughens. | Hardening = austenitise and quench to form martensite | Martensite = hard supersaturated BCT phase | Tempering = reheating martensite to improve toughness
- Surface Hardening | Hard case with tough core. | Carburising = adding carbon to the surface | Nitriding = forming hard nitrides at low temperature | Induction Hardening = local heating by induction and quenching
# Engineering Materials
- Plain Carbon & Alloy Steels | Steels are classified by carbon and alloys. | Mild Steel = low carbon steel, below 0.3% C | Stainless Steel = at least 10.5% chromium for corrosion resistance | Tool Steel = high hardness steel for cutting tools
- Cast Irons | Cast irons are classified by carbon form. | Grey Cast Iron = graphite flakes; good damping | Ductile Iron = spheroidal graphite giving ductility | White Cast Iron = cementite, hard and brittle
- Non-ferrous Alloys | Copper and aluminium alloys are widely used. | Brass = copper-zinc alloy | Bronze = copper-tin alloy | Duralumin = aluminium-copper alloy, age hardenable
- Polymers, Ceramics & Composites | Non-metallic materials offer special properties. | Thermoplastic = softens on heating and can be reshaped | Ceramic = inorganic, hard, brittle and heat resistant | Composite = two or more materials combined, e.g. fibre-reinforced plastic
# Material Testing
- Tensile Test | Tensile test gives strength and ductility. | Yield Strength = stress at onset of plastic deformation | Ultimate Tensile Strength = maximum engineering stress | Percentage Elongation = ductility measure after fracture
- Hardness Testing | Hardness is resistance to indentation. | Brinell Hardness = steel ball indenter, BHN = load / indentation area | Rockwell Hardness = depth-based hardness with scales B and C | Vickers Hardness = diamond pyramid indenter
- Impact & Fatigue Testing | Dynamic loads test toughness and endurance. | Charpy Test = impact energy of notched specimen | Fatigue = failure under cyclic stress below UTS | Endurance Limit = stress below which steel survives infinite cycles
- Non-destructive Testing | NDT inspects without damage. | Ultrasonic Testing = sound waves detect internal flaws | Dye Penetrant Test = detects surface cracks | Radiography = X-rays reveal internal defects

@ me304 | ME-304 | Strength of Materials | Stress and strain, SFD and BMD, bending and shear stresses, torsion, deflection, columns and principal stresses.
# Simple Stress & Strain
- Stress, Strain & Hooke's Law | Loads produce internal stress and deformation. | Normal Stress = σ = P/A | Hooke's Law = σ = Eε within the elastic limit | Poisson's Ratio = lateral strain / longitudinal strain
- Elastic Constants | Material elasticity is described by related constants. | Young's Modulus = ratio of normal stress to strain | Modulus of Rigidity = G = shear stress / shear strain | Relation of Constants = E = 2G(1 + ν) = 3K(1 − 2ν)
- Bars of Varying Section & Compound Bars | Composite and stepped bars share loads. | Deformation of Bar = δ = PL/(AE) | Compound Bar = bars of different materials sharing a load with equal strain | Thermal Stress = σ = EαΔT when expansion is prevented
- Strain Energy | Energy is stored in deformed members. | Strain Energy = U = σ²V/(2E) | Resilience = strain energy stored up to elastic limit | Impact Loading = suddenly applied load gives twice the static stress
# Shear Force & Bending Moment
- Types of Beams & Loads | Beams carry transverse loads. | Cantilever = beam fixed at one end, free at the other | Simply Supported Beam = supported on a pin and roller | UDL = uniformly distributed load
- SFD & BMD | Diagrams show internal force variation. | Shear Force = algebraic sum of forces on one side of a section | Bending Moment = algebraic sum of moments on one side | Relation = dM/dx = V
- Point of Contraflexure | Bending moment may change sign. | Point of Contraflexure = location where bending moment is zero and changes sign | Maximum Bending Moment = occurs where shear force is zero | Overhanging Beam = beam extending beyond its support
- Standard Results | Common beams have standard formulae. | SSB Central Load = Mmax = WL/4 | SSB with UDL = Mmax = wL²/8 | Cantilever End Load = Mmax = WL at the fixed end
# Bending & Shear Stresses
- Theory of Simple Bending | Bending stress varies linearly with depth. | Bending Equation = M/I = σ/y = E/R | Neutral Axis = layer with zero bending stress | Section Modulus = Z = I/ymax
- Moment of Inertia | Section shape determines stiffness. | Rectangle I = bd³/12 about centroidal axis | Parallel Axis Theorem = I = Ig + Ah² | Circular Section I = πd⁴/64
- Shear Stress in Beams | Shear stress varies across a section. | Shear Stress Formula = τ = VAȳ/(Ib) | Rectangular Section = maximum shear 1.5 times average | Circular Section = maximum shear 4/3 times average
- Combined Direct & Bending Stress | Eccentric loads produce combined stresses. | Eccentric Load = load offset from centroid | Combined Stress = σ = P/A ± M/Z | Middle Third Rule = no tension if load lies within middle third
# Torsion & Deflection
- Torsion of Shafts | Shafts transmit torque. | Torsion Equation = T/J = τ/r = Gθ/L | Polar Moment of Inertia = J = πd⁴/32 for solid shaft | Power Transmitted = P = 2πNT/60
- Hollow Shafts & Combined Loading | Hollow shafts are lighter for the same strength. | Hollow Shaft J = π(D⁴ − d⁴)/32 | Equivalent Torque = Te = √(M² + T²) | Equivalent Bending Moment = Me = ½(M + √(M² + T²))
- Deflection of Beams | Deflection is found from the elastic curve. | Elastic Curve Equation = EI d²y/dx² = M | Macaulay's Method = single equation for beams with multiple loads | Cantilever End Load Deflection = WL³/(3EI)
- Moment Area & Springs | Other methods for deflection; springs store energy. | Moment Area Method = slope change equals area of M/EI diagram | Close-coiled Helical Spring Stiffness = k = Gd⁴/(8D³n) | Leaf Spring = laminated spring used in vehicles
# Columns, Cylinders & Principal Stresses
- Columns & Struts | Long compression members fail by buckling. | Euler's Formula = Pcr = π²EI/Le² | Effective Length = depends on end conditions; Le = L for both ends hinged | Slenderness Ratio = Le/k
- Rankine's Formula | Empirical formula covers short and long columns. | Rankine's Formula = 1/P = 1/Pc + 1/Pe | Crushing Load = σc·A for short columns | Rankine Constant = α depends on material
- Thin & Thick Cylinders | Pressure vessels carry hoop and longitudinal stress. | Hoop Stress = σh = pd/(2t) for thin cylinder | Longitudinal Stress = σl = pd/(4t) | Lame's Equations = stresses in thick cylinders
- Principal Stresses & Mohr's Circle | Stress transformation finds extreme stresses. | Principal Stress = normal stress on plane with zero shear | Maximum Shear Stress = (σ1 − σ2)/2 | Mohr's Circle = graphical method for stress transformation

@ me305 | ME-305 | Manufacturing Process | Casting, forming, welding, powder metallurgy and plastic processing.
# Casting Processes
- Sand Casting & Patterns | Molten metal is poured into a mould cavity. | Pattern = replica of the casting used to make the mould | Pattern Allowance = extra size for shrinkage, machining and draft | Core = insert forming internal cavities
- Moulding Sand & Gating System | Sand properties and gating control quality. | Permeability = ability of sand to let gases escape | Riser = reservoir feeding molten metal to compensate shrinkage | Sprue = vertical channel through which metal enters
- Special Casting Processes | Special methods give better finish and accuracy. | Investment Casting = wax pattern coated in ceramic, lost wax process | Die Casting = metal forced into metal dies under pressure | Centrifugal Casting = mould rotated to distribute metal, e.g. pipes
- Casting Defects | Defects arise from gas, shrinkage and mould issues. | Blow Hole = cavity caused by trapped gas | Cold Shut = incomplete fusion of two metal streams | Hot Tear = crack from restrained shrinkage
# Metal Forming
- Hot & Cold Working | Forming temperature affects properties. | Hot Working = above recrystallisation temperature; no strain hardening | Cold Working = below recrystallisation; improves strength and finish | Recrystallisation Temperature = about 0.4 of melting point (K)
- Rolling | Rolling reduces thickness between rolls. | Rolling = compressing metal between rotating rolls | Angle of Bite = maximum angle for metal to enter rolls | Neutral Point = where roll and metal velocities are equal
- Forging | Forging shapes metal by compressive forces. | Open Die Forging = metal deformed between flat dies | Closed Die Forging = metal fills a shaped die cavity | Upsetting = increasing cross-section by reducing length
- Extrusion & Drawing | Metal is pushed or pulled through dies. | Direct Extrusion = ram and product move in the same direction | Indirect Extrusion = die moves towards the billet | Wire Drawing = pulling wire through a die to reduce diameter
# Sheet Metal Operations
- Shearing Operations | Shearing cuts sheet metal. | Blanking = punched piece is the product | Punching = punched piece is scrap; hole is the product | Clearance = gap between punch and die
- Bending & Deep Drawing | Sheets are formed into shapes. | Spring Back = elastic recovery after bending | Deep Drawing = forming a cup from a blank | Limiting Draw Ratio = maximum blank to punch diameter ratio
- Press Tools & Presses | Presses drive dies. | Progressive Die = several operations at successive stations | Compound Die = several operations in one stroke at one station | Stripper Plate = removes sheet from the punch
- High Energy Rate Forming | Rapid energy release forms difficult parts. | Explosive Forming = explosive energy forms sheet into a die | Electromagnetic Forming = magnetic pulse forms conductive parts | Electrohydraulic Forming = spark discharge in liquid creates pressure
# Welding & Joining
- Arc Welding | Electric arc melts base and filler metals. | SMAW = shielded metal arc welding with flux-coated electrode | Arc Length = distance between electrode and workpiece | Polarity = DCEN gives deeper penetration
- Gas Welding & Cutting | Oxy-acetylene flame welds and cuts. | Neutral Flame = equal oxygen and acetylene | Oxidising Flame = excess oxygen, used for brass | Oxy-fuel Cutting = oxidises steel in a jet of oxygen
- TIG, MIG & Resistance Welding | Advanced processes improve quality and speed. | TIG = non-consumable tungsten electrode with inert gas | MIG = consumable wire electrode with inert gas shielding | Spot Welding = resistance welding of overlapping sheets
- Welding Defects & Soldering | Defects and lower-temperature joining. | Porosity = gas pockets in weld metal | Heat Affected Zone = base metal altered by welding heat | Brazing = joining with filler above 450 °C without melting base metal
# Powder Metallurgy & Plastics
- Powder Production & Compaction | Metal powders are made and pressed. | Atomisation = molten metal broken into droplets | Compaction = pressing powder into a green compact | Green Strength = strength of compact before sintering
- Sintering & Applications | Heating bonds powder particles. | Sintering = heating below melting point to bond particles | Self-lubricating Bearing = porous sintered bearing filled with oil | Cemented Carbide = tungsten carbide bonded with cobalt
- Plastic Moulding Processes | Polymers are shaped by moulding. | Injection Moulding = molten plastic injected into a mould | Blow Moulding = air inflates a parison into a hollow shape | Compression Moulding = charge pressed in a heated mould
- Extrusion & Thermoforming of Plastics | Continuous and sheet forming of plastics. | Plastic Extrusion = continuous profiles through a die | Thermoforming = heated sheet formed over a mould | Calendering = rolling plastic into sheets

- Torque & Speed Measurement | Shaft output is measured with dynamometers and tachometers. | Dynamometer = device measuring torque and power | Stroboscope = measures speed by apparently freezing motion | Tachogenerator = generator producing voltage proportional to speed
# Pressure & Flow Measurement

- Low Pressure Measurement | Vacuum gauges measure low pressure. | McLeod Gauge = compresses gas to measure vacuum | Pirani Gauge = thermal conductivity vacuum gauge | Ionisation Gauge = measures very high vacuum
- Obstruction Flow Meters | Flow is inferred from pressure drop. | Venturimeter = converging-diverging tube measuring flow | Orifice Meter = plate with hole; cheap but higher loss | Coefficient of Discharge = actual flow / theoretical flow

# Temperature Measurement
- Expansion Thermometers | Expansion of materials indicates temperature. | Bimetallic Strip = two metals bend differently with temperature | Liquid-in-glass Thermometer = liquid expands in a capillary | Pressure Thermometer = gas or liquid pressure in a bulb
- Thermocouples | Thermocouples generate voltage from temperature difference. | Seebeck Effect = emf generated in a junction of dissimilar metals | Law of Intermediate Metals = third metal at same temperature adds no emf | K-type Thermocouple = chromel-alumel, widely used
- Resistance Thermometers | Resistance varies with temperature. | RTD = platinum resistance thermometer | Thermistor = semiconductor with high temperature sensitivity | PT100 = platinum RTD with 100 Ω at 0 °C
- Pyrometers | Non-contact methods measure high temperature. | Radiation Pyrometer = measures emitted thermal radiation | Optical Pyrometer = compares brightness with a calibrated filament | Emissivity = ratio of emitted radiation to black body radiation
# Control Systems
- Open & Closed Loop Control | Feedback improves accuracy. | Open-loop Control = no feedback of output | Closed-loop Control = output compared with set point | Error Signal = set point minus measured value
- Transfer Function & Block Diagrams | Systems are modelled mathematically. | Transfer Function = Laplace output / input | Block Diagram = graphical representation of system | Closed-loop Gain = G/(1 + GH)
- Controller Modes | Controllers act on error in different ways. | On-Off Control = two-position control with differential gap | Proportional Control = output proportional to error, leaves offset | PID Control = proportional, integral and derivative actions combined
- Hydraulic & Pneumatic Control | Fluid power actuates industrial systems. | Hydraulic Actuator = uses pressurised oil for large forces | Pneumatic Control = uses compressed air | Servo Valve = valve controlling flow in proportion to signal

@ me403 | ME-403 | Theory of Machines | Mechanisms, velocity and acceleration analysis, cams, gears, gear trains, friction, belts and governors.
# Mechanisms
- Kinematic Links & Pairs | Mechanisms are made of links joined by pairs. | Kinematic Link = rigid body connecting with others | Lower Pair = surface contact, e.g. turning pair | Higher Pair = point or line contact, e.g. cam and follower
- Degrees of Freedom | Mobility tells how many inputs are needed. | Grubler's Equation = F = 3(n − 1) − 2j − h | Mechanism = kinematic chain with one link fixed and F = 1 | Structure = F = 0, no relative motion
- Four-bar Chain & Inversions | Inversions give different mechanisms. | Grashof's Law = s + l ≤ p + q for continuous rotation | Inversion = fixing different links of a chain | Crank-Rocker = inversion with one link fully rotating
- Slider-Crank & Double Slider | Inversions of slider-crank chains. | Whitworth Quick Return = inversion of slider-crank in shapers | Scotch Yoke = double slider giving simple harmonic motion | Elliptical Trammel = double slider tracing an ellipse
# Velocity & Acceleration Analysis
- Relative Velocity Method | Velocity polygons find link velocities. | Relative Velocity = v = ω × r perpendicular to the link | Velocity Polygon = vector diagram of velocities | Rubbing Velocity = relative velocity at a pin joint
- Instantaneous Centre Method | ICs give velocities quickly. | Instantaneous Centre = point about which a link rotates at an instant | Kennedy's Theorem = three ICs of three bodies lie on a straight line | Number of ICs = n(n − 1)/2
- Acceleration Analysis | Acceleration polygons include centripetal terms. | Centripetal Acceleration = ω²r towards the centre | Tangential Acceleration = αr | Coriolis Component = 2vω for sliding on a rotating link
- Klein's Construction | Graphical method for slider-crank analysis. | Klein's Construction = finds velocity and acceleration of slider-crank | Piston Acceleration = ω²r(cos θ + cos 2θ / n) | Obliquity Ratio = n = connecting rod / crank length
# Cams & Gears
- Cams & Followers | Cams convert rotary into reciprocating motion. | Cam = rotating element imparting motion to follower | Roller Follower = reduces wear compared to knife edge | Pressure Angle = angle between follower motion and normal to the cam profile
- Follower Motions | Motion laws affect smoothness. | Uniform Velocity = infinite acceleration at ends | SHM Follower Motion = smooth harmonic displacement | Cycloidal Motion = zero acceleration at start and end; best for high speed
- Gear Terminology & Law of Gearing | Gears transmit motion at constant velocity ratio. | Law of Gearing = common normal at contact passes through pitch point | Module = m = pitch diameter / number of teeth | Involute Profile = most common tooth profile
- Interference & Contact Ratio | Tooth geometry limits small pinions. | Interference = tip of one tooth digs into flank of another | Minimum Teeth = about 17 for 20° full-depth involute | Contact Ratio = arc of contact / circular pitch
# Gear Trains & Friction Drives
- Simple & Compound Gear Trains | Gear trains change speed and torque. | Simple Gear Train = each shaft has one gear | Compound Gear Train = intermediate shafts carry two gears | Train Value = output speed / input speed
- Epicyclic Gear Trains | Planetary gears give high ratios compactly. | Epicyclic Train = gear axes move around a central axis | Sun and Planet Gear = common epicyclic arrangement | Tabular Method = finds speeds by adding rotations
- Belt & Rope Drives | Belts transmit power by friction. | Tension Ratio = T1/T2 = e^(μθ) | Centrifugal Tension = Tc = mv² | Maximum Power Condition = Tc = Tmax/3
- Friction, Clutches & Brakes | Friction transmits and absorbs power. | Friction Circle = circle of radius μr for journal bearings | Uniform Wear Theory = pr constant; used for clutch design | Band Brake = band around drum providing braking torque
# Governors & Gyroscope
- Watt & Porter Governors | Governors control engine speed. | Governor = regulates mean speed by controlling fuel supply | Watt Governor = simple pendulum governor | Porter Governor = Watt governor with central load
- Hartnell Governor & Characteristics | Spring governors and their qualities. | Hartnell Governor = spring-loaded centrifugal governor | Sensitiveness = range of speed / mean speed | Isochronous Governor = same equilibrium speed at all radii
- Governor Effort & Power | Effort and power quantify governor capability. | Governor Effort = mean force on sleeve for a speed change | Governor Power = effort × sleeve lift | Hunting = continuous fluctuation of speed
- Gyroscopic Effects | Spinning bodies resist changes in axis. | Gyroscopic Couple = C = Iωωp | Precession = rotation of the spin axis | Applications = stability of aeroplanes, ships and two-wheelers

@ me404 | ME-404 | Fluid Mechanics | Fluid properties, statics, kinematics, dynamics, flow measurement, viscous flow, boundary layer and dimensional analysis.
# Fluid Properties & Statics
- Properties of Fluids | Fluids deform continuously under shear. | Viscosity = resistance to shear; τ = μ du/dy | Newtonian Fluid = shear stress proportional to velocity gradient | Surface Tension = force per unit length at a liquid surface
- Pressure & Its Measurement | Pressure acts equally in all directions at a point. | Pascal's Law = pressure at a point is equal in all directions | Hydrostatic Law = dp/dz = −ρg | U-tube Manometer = measures pressure using liquid columns
- Hydrostatic Forces | Submerged surfaces experience pressure forces. | Total Pressure Force = F = ρgAh̄ | Centre of Pressure = depth h* = h̄ + I/(Ah̄) | Curved Surface Force = horizontal and vertical components found separately
- Buoyancy & Stability | Floating bodies obey Archimedes' principle. | Archimedes' Principle = buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid | Metacentric Height = GM; positive for stable floating | Metacentre = point about which a floating body oscillates
# Fluid Kinematics
- Types of Flow | Flows are classified by variation and nature. | Steady Flow = properties at a point do not change with time | Uniform Flow = velocity same at every point | Laminar Flow = fluid moves in smooth layers
- Streamlines & Continuity | Streamlines visualise flow; continuity conserves mass. | Streamline = curve tangent to velocity everywhere | Continuity Equation = A1V1 = A2V2 for incompressible flow | Stream Tube = bundle of streamlines
- Velocity Potential & Stream Function | Potential flow uses φ and ψ. | Velocity Potential = φ with u = −∂φ/∂x | Stream Function = ψ, constant along streamlines | Flow Net = grid of streamlines and equipotential lines
- Rotation & Vorticity | Fluid elements may rotate. | Vorticity = twice the angular velocity | Irrotational Flow = zero vorticity | Circulation = line integral of velocity around a closed curve
# Fluid Dynamics
- Euler's & Bernoulli's Equations | Energy along a streamline is conserved. | Euler's Equation = dp/ρ + V dV + g dz = 0 | Bernoulli's Equation = p/ρg + V²/2g + z = constant | Assumptions = steady, incompressible, inviscid flow along a streamline
- Flow Measurement Devices | Bernoulli explains flow meters. | Venturimeter = measures discharge using pressure difference | Pitot Tube = measures velocity from stagnation pressure | Orifice Meter = simple plate meter with higher losses
- Momentum Equation | Momentum principle gives forces from flow. | Impulse-Momentum Equation = F = ρQ(V2 − V1) | Force on Pipe Bend = found from momentum change | Jet Impact on Plate = F = ρaV² on a fixed flat plate
- Notches & Weirs | Open-channel flow is measured by notches. | Rectangular Notch = Q = (2/3)Cd·L·√(2g)·H^(3/2) | V-notch = Q = (8/15)Cd·tan(θ/2)√(2g)H^(5/2) | Weir = notch on a large scale
# Viscous Flow & Pipe Flow
- Laminar Flow in Pipes | Viscous flow obeys Hagen-Poiseuille law. | Hagen-Poiseuille Equation = Δp = 32μVL/D² | Reynolds Number = Re = ρVD/μ; laminar below about 2000 | Velocity Profile = parabolic in laminar pipe flow
