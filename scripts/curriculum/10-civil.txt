@ ce302 | CE-302 | Construction Materials | Stones, bricks, lime, cement, concrete, timber, metals, polymers and modern construction materials.
# Stones, Bricks & Tiles
- Building Stones | Natural stones are classified and tested for construction use. | Igneous Rock = formed by cooling of magma, e.g. granite; hard and durable | Sedimentary Rock = formed by deposition, e.g. sandstone and limestone | Dressing of Stones = shaping quarried stone to required size and finish
- Tests on Stones | Tests decide whether a stone suits a structure. | Crushing Strength Test = compressive strength of a stone cube | Water Absorption Test = percentage of water absorbed in 24 hours | Attrition Test = resistance of stone to wear by rubbing
- Bricks | Burnt clay bricks are the most common masonry unit. | Standard Brick Size = 190 × 90 × 90 mm (modular) in India | Brick Earth Constituents = silica, alumina, lime, iron oxide and magnesia | Efflorescence = white salt deposits caused by soluble salts in bricks
- Tiles & Clay Products | Burnt clay products include tiles and pipes. | Roofing Tile = clay tile such as Mangalore pattern | Terracotta = burnt clay used for ornamental work | Vitrified Tile = dense low-porosity tile fired at high temperature
# Lime, Cement & Mortar
- Lime | Lime is a traditional binding material. | Fat Lime = high-calcium lime that slakes vigorously | Hydraulic Lime = sets under water due to clay content | Slaking = adding water to quicklime to form hydrated lime
- Portland Cement | Cement hydrates to bind aggregates. | Bogue Compounds = C3S, C2S, C3A and C4AF | C3S = responsible for early strength | Heat of Hydration = heat released when cement reacts with water
- Types & Tests of Cement | Different cements suit different works. | PPC = Portland pozzolana cement with fly ash | Rapid Hardening Cement = gains strength quickly | Vicat Apparatus = finds initial and final setting time
- Mortar | Mortar binds masonry units. | Cement Mortar = cement, sand and water | Mix Proportion = e.g. 1:6 cement to sand for brickwork | Lime Mortar = lime and sand, slower setting
# Concrete
- Constituents of Concrete | Concrete is cement, aggregates and water. | Fine Aggregate = particles passing 4.75 mm sieve | Coarse Aggregate = particles retained on 4.75 mm sieve | Fineness Modulus = index of aggregate coarseness from sieve analysis
- Fresh Concrete Properties | Workability governs placing and compaction. | Workability = ease of mixing, placing and compacting | Slump Test = measures consistency of fresh concrete | Segregation = separation of coarse aggregate from the mix
- Hardened Concrete Properties | Strength and durability of concrete. | Water-Cement Ratio Law = strength falls as w/c ratio rises (Abrams' law) | Curing = maintaining moisture for hydration | Characteristic Strength = strength below which only 5% results fall
- Admixtures & Special Concretes | Admixtures modify concrete properties. | Plasticiser = improves workability at same water content | Accelerator = speeds setting, e.g. calcium chloride | Self-compacting Concrete = flows and compacts under its own weight
# Timber & Metals
- Timber | Timber is a natural building material. | Seasoning = reducing moisture content of timber | Defects in Timber = knots, shakes and decay | Plywood = layers of veneer glued with grain at right angles
- Preservation of Timber | Treatments protect timber. | ASCU Treatment = arsenic-copper preservative | Creosote = oil-based preservative | Fire Proofing = coatings that delay ignition
- Ferrous Metals | Steel is the main structural metal. | Mild Steel = low carbon steel for structures | TMT Bars = thermo-mechanically treated reinforcement bars | HYSD Bars = high yield strength deformed bars
- Non-ferrous Metals | Aluminium and copper are also used. | Aluminium = light, corrosion resistant | Copper = good conductor for wiring and plumbing | Corrosion Protection = painting and galvanising
# Modern & Miscellaneous Materials
- Glass & Plastics | Glass and polymers find wide use. | Float Glass = flat glass of uniform thickness | PVC = polymer for pipes and fittings | FRP = fibre reinforced polymer for strengthening
- Paints & Varnishes | Coatings protect and decorate surfaces. | Paint Constituents = base, vehicle, pigment, drier and thinner | Distemper = water-based coating for interior walls | Varnish = transparent protective coating
- Eco-friendly Materials | Sustainable materials reduce environmental impact. | Fly Ash Brick = brick made with fly ash, lime and gypsum | Bamboo = renewable structural material | Recycled Aggregate = aggregate from demolition waste
- Geosynthetics & Smart Materials | New materials improve performance. | Geotextile = permeable fabric used with soil | Ferrocement = thin mortar with closely spaced mesh | Smart Material = responds to stimuli, e.g. shape memory alloys

@ ce303 | CE-303 | Surveying | Chain, compass, levelling, plane table, theodolite, tacheometry, curves and modern surveying instruments.
# Chain & Compass Surveying
- Principles of Surveying | Surveying fixes relative positions of points. | Plane Surveying = Earth's curvature neglected | Geodetic Surveying = curvature considered for large areas | Working from Whole to Part = limits accumulation of errors
- Chain Surveying | Distances are measured with chain or tape. | Survey Station = point at the end of a chain line | Offset = lateral measurement to locate details | Ranging = establishing intermediate points on a line
- Compass Surveying | Compass measures bearings of lines. | Prismatic Compass = reads whole circle bearing | Magnetic Declination = angle between true and magnetic meridian | Local Attraction = disturbance of compass needle by nearby iron
- Traversing | Traverses link survey stations. | Closed Traverse = starts and ends at same point | Closing Error = misclosure of traverse | Bowditch Rule = distributes closing error in proportion to line lengths
# Levelling & Contouring
- Levelling Fundamentals | Levelling finds difference in elevation. | Benchmark = point of known reduced level | Backsight = first reading after setting up instrument | Foresight = last reading before shifting instrument
- Reduction of Levels | Readings are converted to reduced levels. | Height of Collimation Method = RL = HI − staff reading | Rise and Fall Method = compares consecutive readings | Arithmetic Check = ΣBS − ΣFS = last RL − first RL
- Errors & Special Levelling | Curvature and refraction affect long sights. | Curvature Correction = −0.0785 d² m with d in km | Reciprocal Levelling = eliminates instrument and curvature errors | Profile Levelling = levels along a route for a section
- Contouring | Contours show relief on maps. | Contour Line = line joining points of equal elevation | Contour Interval = vertical distance between contours | Contour Characteristics = closely spaced contours indicate steep slope
# Plane Table & Theodolite
- Plane Table Surveying | Plotting is done in the field. | Radiation Method = locating points from one station | Intersection Method = locating points by rays from two stations | Resection = locating the plane table station itself
- Theodolite | Theodolite measures horizontal and vertical angles. | Transit Theodolite = telescope can be transited | Face Left and Face Right = observations to eliminate errors | Temporary Adjustments = setting up, centring, levelling and focusing
- Measurement of Angles | Methods improve accuracy. | Repetition Method = measures angle several times cumulatively | Reiteration Method = measures several angles from one station | Vertical Angle = angle from horizontal to line of sight
- Traverse Computation | Coordinates are computed from angles and lengths. | Latitude = L cos θ | Departure = L sin θ | Consecutive Coordinates = latitude and departure of each line
# Tacheometry & Curves
- Tacheometry | Distances measured optically. | Tacheometric Constant = multiplying constant usually 100 | Stadia Method = distance from staff intercept | Horizontal Distance = D = ks + C for horizontal sight
- Simple Circular Curves | Curves connect straights on roads. | Degree of Curve = angle subtended by 30 m chord | Tangent Length = R tan(Δ/2) | Rankine's Method = setting out curve by deflection angles
- Transition Curves | Gradual change of curvature improves comfort. | Transition Curve = curvature increases gradually | Clothoid = spiral with curvature proportional to length | Superelevation = raising outer edge on curves
- Vertical Curves | Curves join different gradients. | Summit Curve = vertical curve with convexity upward | Valley Curve = vertical curve with concavity upward | Parabolic Curve = commonly used vertical curve
# Modern Surveying
- Electronic Distance Measurement | EDM uses electromagnetic waves. | EDM = measures distance by phase of modulated wave | Total Station = electronic theodolite with EDM | Prism Reflector = target returning EDM signal
- GPS & GNSS | Satellites give positions. | GNSS = global navigation satellite systems such as GPS and NavIC | DGPS = differential GPS improving accuracy | RTK = real-time kinematic centimetre-level positioning
- Remote Sensing in Surveying | Aerial and satellite imagery map large areas. | Aerial Photogrammetry = measurements from overlapping photographs | Stereoscopic Vision = depth from overlapping images | LiDAR = laser scanning to create elevation models
- GIS Basics | GIS stores and analyses spatial data. | GIS = geographic information system | Vector Data = points, lines and polygons | Raster Data = grid of cells with values

@ ce403 | CE-403 | Structural Analysis-I | Determinacy, deflection methods, energy principles, arches, cables, influence lines and indeterminate beams.
# Fundamentals of Structural Analysis
- Structures & Supports | Analysis predicts internal forces and deflections. | Determinate Structure = solvable by equilibrium alone | Indeterminate Structure = needs compatibility in addition to equilibrium | Degree of Indeterminacy = unknown reactions minus equilibrium equations
- Static & Kinematic Indeterminacy | Two measures characterise structures. | Static Indeterminacy = excess unknown forces | Kinematic Indeterminacy = unknown joint displacements | Stability = structure resists loads without collapse
- Analysis of Trusses | Truss members carry axial forces. | Method of Joints = equilibrium at each joint | Method of Sections = cut through members to find forces | Zero Force Member = member carrying no load under given loading
- Principle of Superposition | Linear structures allow superposition. | Superposition = total response equals sum of individual responses | Linearity = displacements proportional to loads | Small Deflection Assumption = geometry unchanged by loading
# Deflection of Beams
- Double Integration Method | Elastic curve gives deflection. | Elastic Curve = EI d²y/dx² = M | Boundary Conditions = known slopes and deflections at supports | Macaulay's Method = single equation with brackets
- Moment Area Method | Areas of M/EI diagram give slope and deflection. | First Moment Area Theorem = slope change equals area of M/EI | Second Theorem = deflection equals moment of M/EI area | Application = cantilevers and symmetric beams
- Conjugate Beam Method | Fictitious beam loaded with M/EI. | Conjugate Beam = beam loaded by M/EI diagram | Shear in Conjugate Beam = slope in real beam | Moment in Conjugate Beam = deflection in real beam
- Standard Deflections | Common results are memorised. | Cantilever End Load = WL³/(3EI) | SSB Central Load = WL³/(48EI) | SSB UDL = 5wL⁴/(384EI)
# Energy Methods
- Strain Energy | Work done is stored as strain energy. | Strain Energy in Bending = ∫ M²/(2EI) dx | Strain Energy in Axial Load = P²L/(2AE) | Complementary Energy = energy used in non-linear analysis
- Castigliano's Theorems | Derivatives of strain energy give deflections. | Castigliano's First Theorem = ∂U/∂Δ = P | Castigliano's Second Theorem = ∂U/∂P = Δ | Dummy Load Method = apply fictitious load to find deflection
- Unit Load Method | Virtual work finds deflections. | Virtual Work Principle = external virtual work equals internal virtual work | Unit Load Method = δ = ∫ Mm/(EI) dx | Truss Deflection = δ = Σ PuL/(AE)
- Maxwell & Betti Theorems | Reciprocal theorems relate deflections. | Maxwell's Reciprocal Theorem = deflection at A due to load at B equals deflection at B due to load at A | Betti's Theorem = generalised reciprocal work theorem | Application = influence lines by Müller-Breslau
# Arches & Cables
- Three-hinged Arches | Determinate arches carry loads by thrust. | Three-hinged Arch = determinate with hinge at crown | Horizontal Thrust = H = wL²/(8h) for parabolic arch under UDL | Normal Thrust = axial force along arch
- Two-hinged Arches | Indeterminate arches need compatibility. | Two-hinged Arch = one degree indeterminate | Rib Shortening = effect of axial deformation | Temperature Effect = changes horizontal thrust
- Cables | Cables carry loads in tension. | Cable Equation = parabolic shape under uniform horizontal load | Maximum Tension = at supports | Suspension Bridge = cable-supported deck
- Stiffening Girders | Girders distribute loads to cables. | Stiffening Girder = spreads loads in suspension bridges | Two-hinged Stiffening Girder = common arrangement | Tension Coefficient Method = analyses space trusses
# Influence Lines & Rolling Loads
- Influence Lines | ILs show effect of a moving unit load. | Influence Line = response at a point as unit load moves | IL for Reaction = straight line for simply supported beam | Müller-Breslau Principle = IL shape equals deflected shape from released constraint
- Rolling Loads | Moving loads produce maximum effects. | Single Concentrated Load = maximum moment when load is at section | UDL Longer than Span = covers whole span for maximum | Maximum Bending Moment = occurs under a load near mid-span
- Absolute Maximum Moment | Moving load trains create peak effects. | Absolute Maximum Moment = largest moment anywhere in span | Resultant Rule = centre of span bisects distance between resultant and nearest load | Equivalent UDL = uniform load giving same maximum effect
- Indeterminate Beams | Propped and fixed beams. | Propped Cantilever = one degree indeterminate | Fixed Beam = fixing moments at both ends; wL²/12 for UDL | Clapeyron's Three Moment Equation = analyses continuous beams
