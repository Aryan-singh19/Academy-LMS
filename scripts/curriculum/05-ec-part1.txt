@ ec302 | EC-302 | Electronic Measurement & Instrumentation | Measurement errors, analog and digital meters, bridges, oscilloscopes, transducers and data acquisition.
# Measurement Fundamentals
- Characteristics of Instruments | Instruments are judged by static and dynamic characteristics. | Accuracy = closeness of a reading to the true value | Precision = closeness of repeated readings to each other | Resolution = smallest input change an instrument can detect
- Errors in Measurement | Every measurement contains some error. | Gross Error = human mistake in reading or recording | Systematic Error = repeatable error from instrument or environment | Random Error = unpredictable error reduced by averaging
- Standards & Calibration | Instruments are calibrated against standards. | Calibration = comparing an instrument with a known standard | Primary Standard = highest accuracy standard kept by national labs | Traceability = unbroken chain of calibrations to national standards
- Statistical Analysis of Data | Statistics quantify uncertainty in readings. | Arithmetic Mean = best estimate of the true value from many readings | Standard Deviation = spread of readings about the mean | Probable Error = 0.6745 × standard deviation
# Analog & Digital Meters
- PMMC Instruments | Moving-coil meters measure DC current and voltage. | PMMC = permanent magnet moving coil instrument with linear scale | Shunt = low resistance in parallel to extend ammeter range | Multiplier = high series resistance to extend voltmeter range
- Moving Iron & Electrodynamometer Instruments | These meters measure AC and DC quantities. | Moving Iron Instrument = works on AC and DC with a non-linear scale | Electrodynamometer = fixed and moving coils used as a wattmeter | Loading Effect = meter draws current and alters the measured circuit
- Digital Voltmeters | DVMs convert analog voltage to digital readings. | Ramp-type DVM = counts clock pulses during a linear ramp | Dual-slope DVM = integrating converter with good noise rejection | Successive Approximation DVM = binary search conversion
- Digital Multimeter & Frequency Counter | Digital meters measure many quantities. | Digital Multimeter = measures voltage, current and resistance digitally | 3½ Digit Display = maximum reading 1999 | Frequency Counter = counts cycles in a precise time gate
# Bridges
- DC Bridges | Bridges measure resistance by null balance. | Wheatstone Bridge = balance condition R1·R4 = R2·R3 for medium resistance | Kelvin Double Bridge = measures low resistance, removing lead resistance | Null Detection = galvanometer shows zero at balance
- AC Bridges for Inductance | AC bridges balance both magnitude and phase. | Maxwell Bridge = measures medium-Q inductance with a capacitor | Hay's Bridge = suits high-Q coils | Anderson Bridge = accurate inductance measurement using a modified Maxwell bridge
- AC Bridges for Capacitance & Frequency | Other bridges measure capacitance and frequency. | Schering Bridge = measures capacitance and dissipation factor | Wien Bridge = frequency-dependent bridge used in oscillators | De Sauty Bridge = simple capacitance comparison bridge
- Q-meter | The Q-meter measures coil quality factor. | Q-meter = measures Q using series resonance | Quality Factor = Q = ωL/R | Distributed Capacitance = self-capacitance of a coil found with a Q-meter
# Oscilloscopes & Signal Generators
- Cathode Ray Oscilloscope | The CRO displays voltage against time. | CRT = cathode ray tube with electron gun, deflection plates and screen | Time Base = sawtooth sweep moving the beam horizontally | Deflection Sensitivity = beam deflection per volt
- Lissajous Patterns & Measurements | CROs measure phase and frequency. | Lissajous Figure = pattern from two sinusoids on X and Y plates | Frequency Ratio = fy/fx = horizontal tangencies / vertical tangencies | Phase Angle = sin φ = Y-intercept / Y-max for an ellipse
- Digital Storage Oscilloscope | DSOs sample and store waveforms. | DSO = digitises and stores waveforms in memory | Sampling Rate = samples per second taken by the scope | Pre-trigger View = shows events before the trigger point
- Signal Generators & Analysers | Generators supply test signals; analysers study spectra. | Function Generator = produces sine, square and triangular waves | Spectrum Analyser = displays signal amplitude versus frequency | Wave Analyser = measures amplitude of individual harmonics
# Transducers & Data Acquisition
- Transducer Classification | Transducers convert physical quantities to electrical signals. | Active Transducer = generates its own output, e.g. thermocouple | Passive Transducer = needs external excitation, e.g. strain gauge | Inverse Transducer = converts electrical signal into physical quantity
- Strain Gauges & LVDT | Displacement and strain are measured electrically. | Strain Gauge = resistance changes with strain | Gauge Factor = (ΔR/R)/(Δl/l) | LVDT = linear variable differential transformer for displacement
- Temperature & Pressure Transducers | Common sensors measure temperature and pressure. | Thermocouple = Seebeck-effect sensor generating voltage with temperature | RTD = resistance temperature detector, usually platinum | Thermistor = semiconductor with large negative temperature coefficient
- Data Acquisition Systems | DAQ systems condition, convert and record signals. | Signal Conditioning = amplification, filtering and isolation | Sample-and-Hold = holds the input steady during conversion | Telemetry = measuring and transmitting data from remote points

@ ec303 | EC-303 | Digital System Design | Logic minimisation, combinational and sequential design, state machines, memories, PLDs and logic families.
# Logic Simplification
- Boolean Algebra Review | Boolean laws simplify digital expressions. | Consensus Theorem = AB + A'C + BC = AB + A'C | De Morgan's Theorem = complement of a sum is the product of complements | Duality = interchanging AND/OR and 0/1 gives a valid identity
- K-Map Minimisation | K-maps group adjacent minterms. | Karnaugh Map = Gray-ordered grid for grouping 1s | Essential Prime Implicant = prime implicant covering a minterm no other covers | Don't-care Condition = unspecified output usable for larger groups
- Quine-McCluskey Method | Tabulation suits many variables. | Quine-McCluskey = tabular minimisation by combining minterms differing in one bit | Prime Implicant Chart = table selecting implicants that cover all minterms | Petrick's Method = algebraic way to choose minimal cover
- Hazards in Combinational Circuits | Hazards cause glitches during transitions. | Static Hazard = output momentarily changes when it should stay constant | Dynamic Hazard = output changes several times instead of once | Hazard Cover = adding redundant terms to remove static hazards
# Combinational Design
- Arithmetic Circuits | Adders and subtractors build ALUs. | Full Adder = Sum = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin, Cout = AB + Cin(A ⊕ B) | Carry Look-ahead = generate and propagate terms compute carries quickly | BCD Adder = adds BCD digits with correction when sum exceeds 9
- MUX & DEMUX Design | Data selectors implement functions. | Multiplexer = selects one input with select lines | Function Implementation with MUX = n-variable function on 2^(n−1):1 MUX | Demultiplexer = routes one input to one of several outputs
- Decoders & Encoders | Code conversion circuits support addressing. | Decoder = activates one of 2^n outputs | Priority Encoder = encodes the highest-priority active input | 7-Segment Decoder = drives display segments from a BCD input
- Comparators & ALU Design | Comparators and ALUs perform decisions and operations. | Magnitude Comparator = indicates greater, equal or less | 74181 ALU = classic 4-bit arithmetic logic unit IC | Parity Checker = detects odd number of errors
# Sequential Circuits
- Flip-Flops | Bistable elements store state. | JK Flip-Flop = J = K = 1 toggles the state | T Flip-Flop = toggles when T = 1 | Master-Slave Flip-Flop = avoids race-around using two latches
- Counters | Counters cycle through states. | Ripple Counter = asynchronous counter with cumulative delay | Synchronous Counter = all flip-flops clocked together | Johnson Counter = twisted ring counter with 2n states
- Shift Registers | Shift registers move data serially. | SISO = serial-in serial-out | Universal Shift Register = shifts left, right and loads in parallel | Serial-to-parallel Conversion = data shifted in serially and read in parallel
- Timing & Clocking | Clocking constraints govern reliable operation. | Setup Time = input stable before clock edge | Hold Time = input stable after clock edge | Clock Skew = arrival time difference of the clock at different flip-flops
# Finite State Machines
- Mealy & Moore Machines | FSMs model sequential behaviour. | Moore Machine = outputs depend only on present state | Mealy Machine = outputs depend on present state and inputs | State Diagram = graph of states and transitions
- State Reduction & Assignment | Fewer states mean less hardware. | Equivalent States = states with same outputs and equivalent next states | State Assignment = choosing binary codes for states | One-hot Encoding = one flip-flop per state
- Sequence Detector Design | FSMs recognise bit patterns. | Sequence Detector = FSM outputting 1 when a pattern appears | Overlapping Detection = allows a pattern to start within a previous match | Excitation Equations = flip-flop input equations derived from tables
- Asynchronous Sequential Circuits | Circuits without clocks respond to input changes. | Fundamental Mode = only one input changes at a time | Critical Race = race whose outcome depends on delays | Flow Table = table of states for asynchronous circuits
# Memories, PLDs & HDL
- Semiconductor Memories | Memories store binary data in arrays. | ROM = non-volatile read-only memory | SRAM = static RAM using flip-flops | DRAM = dynamic RAM needing periodic refresh
- Programmable Logic Devices | PLDs implement custom logic. | PLA = programmable AND and OR arrays | PAL = programmable AND array with fixed OR array | FPGA = array of configurable logic blocks and interconnects
- Logic Families | Technologies differ in speed and power. | TTL = bipolar logic family, 74 series | CMOS = complementary MOS with very low static power | Fan-out = number of loads an output can drive
- Introduction to Verilog/VHDL | HDLs describe hardware for simulation and synthesis. | HDL = hardware description language such as Verilog or VHDL | Behavioural Modelling = describing what a circuit does | Synthesis = converting HDL into a gate-level netlist

@ ec304 | EC-304 | Electronic Devices | Semiconductor physics, PN junction, BJT, FET and MOSFET, special diodes and optoelectronic devices.
# Semiconductor Material Properties
- Energy Bands & Carriers | Band theory explains conductors, insulators and semiconductors. | Energy Band Gap = energy gap between valence and conduction bands; about 1.1 eV for Si | Intrinsic Semiconductor = pure semiconductor with equal electrons and holes | Fermi Level = energy with 50% occupation probability
- Doping & Extrinsic Semiconductors | Impurities control conductivity. | N-type = doped with pentavalent donors; electrons majority | P-type = doped with trivalent acceptors; holes majority | Mass Action Law = n·p = ni²
- Carrier Transport | Carriers move by drift and diffusion. | Drift Current = carrier flow due to an electric field | Diffusion Current = flow from high to low concentration | Mobility = drift velocity per unit electric field
- Hall Effect & Carrier Lifetime | Measurements reveal carrier type and density. | Hall Effect = transverse voltage when current flows in a magnetic field | Hall Coefficient = RH = 1/(nq) | Recombination = electron filling a hole, ending both carriers
# PN Junction Diode
- PN Junction Formation | A depletion region forms at the junction. | Depletion Region = region empty of mobile carriers near the junction | Barrier Potential = about 0.7 V for Si and 0.3 V for Ge | Built-in Field = field opposing further diffusion
- Diode Characteristics | Forward and reverse bias give rectifying behaviour. | Diode Equation = I = Is(e^(V/ηVT) − 1) | Reverse Saturation Current = small current under reverse bias | Dynamic Resistance = rd = ηVT/I
- Diode Capacitances & Breakdown | Junctions store charge and break down at high reverse voltage. | Transition Capacitance = depletion capacitance under reverse bias | Diffusion Capacitance = dominant capacitance under forward bias | Avalanche Breakdown = carrier multiplication at high reverse voltage
- Rectifiers & Clippers | Diodes convert AC to DC and shape waveforms. | Half-wave Rectifier = ripple factor 1.21, efficiency 40.6% | Bridge Rectifier = full-wave with four diodes; efficiency 81.2% | Clipper = circuit removing part of a waveform
# Bipolar Junction Transistor
- BJT Construction & Operation | A BJT is two back-to-back junctions controlled by base current. | BJT = three-terminal current-controlled device | Current Gain β = IC/IB | Alpha α = IC/IE = β/(1 + β)
- BJT Configurations | Each configuration offers different gains and impedances. | Common Emitter = high voltage and current gain; phase inversion | Common Base = low input impedance; current gain near 1 | Common Collector = emitter follower with voltage gain near 1
- BJT Biasing | Biasing fixes a stable operating point. | Q-point = DC operating point on the load line | Voltage Divider Bias = most stable common biasing method | Stability Factor = S = ∂IC/∂ICO
- BJT Small-signal Models | Models analyse amplifier behaviour. | h-parameter Model = hybrid parameters hie, hfe, hre, hoe | re Model = small-signal model with re = 26 mV/IE | Early Effect = base-width modulation with collector voltage
# Field Effect Transistors
- JFET | Junction FETs are voltage-controlled. | JFET = field effect transistor controlled by gate-junction reverse bias | Pinch-off Voltage = VGS at which the channel closes | Shockley Equation = ID = IDSS(1 − VGS/VP)²
- MOSFET | MOSFETs use an insulated gate. | Enhancement MOSFET = channel induced when VGS exceeds threshold | Depletion MOSFET = channel exists at VGS = 0 | Threshold Voltage = minimum VGS to form an inversion channel
- FET Biasing & Small-signal Model | FET amplifiers are biased and modelled like BJTs. | Self Bias = source resistor sets VGS | Transconductance = gm = ΔID/ΔVGS | Common Source Amplifier = FET amplifier with gain −gm·RD
- CMOS & Comparison of Devices | CMOS pairs NMOS and PMOS devices. | CMOS Inverter = complementary pair with negligible static current | BJT vs FET = BJT current controlled, FET voltage controlled | Channel Length Modulation = ID increases slightly with VDS in saturation
# Special Semiconductor Devices
- Zener Diode & Regulators | Zener breakdown provides voltage regulation. | Zener Diode = heavily doped diode operating in reverse breakdown | Zener Regulator = keeps load voltage constant | Zener Breakdown = tunnelling breakdown below about 5 V
- Tunnel & Varactor Diodes | Special diodes exploit unusual characteristics. | Tunnel Diode = negative resistance region from quantum tunnelling | Varactor Diode = capacitance varies with reverse voltage | Schottky Diode = metal-semiconductor diode with fast switching
- Optoelectronic Devices | Devices convert light and electricity. | LED = emits light when forward biased | Photodiode = generates current proportional to light | Solar Cell = large-area PN junction producing power from light
- Thyristors | PNPN devices switch large power. | SCR = silicon controlled rectifier turned on by a gate pulse | UJT = unijunction transistor used in relaxation oscillators | DIAC and TRIAC = bidirectional switching devices for AC control

@ ec305 | EC-305 | Network Analysis | Circuit laws, network theorems, transient analysis, Laplace methods, two-port networks and filters.
# Circuit Fundamentals
- Circuit Elements & Sources | Networks are built from passive elements and sources. | Active Element = supplies energy, e.g. voltage source | Passive Element = absorbs or stores energy, e.g. R, L, C | Dependent Source = source whose value depends on another circuit quantity
- Kirchhoff's Laws | KCL and KVL are the basis of circuit analysis. | KCL = algebraic sum of currents at a node is zero | KVL = algebraic sum of voltages around a loop is zero | Source Transformation = converting a voltage source with series R to a current source with parallel R
- Mesh & Nodal Analysis | Systematic methods write equations for any network. | Mesh Analysis = KVL around meshes using mesh currents | Nodal Analysis = KCL at nodes using node voltages | Supermesh = mesh formed when a current source lies between meshes
- Graph Theory of Networks | Network topology organises equation writing. | Tree = connected subgraph with all nodes and no loops | Tie-set = loop formed by one link and tree branches | Incidence Matrix = node-branch matrix of a network graph
# Network Theorems
- Superposition & Linearity | Linear networks allow sources to be considered one at a time. | Superposition Theorem = response is the sum of responses to each source alone | Linearity = output proportional to input | Source Deactivation = voltage sources shorted, current sources opened
- Thevenin & Norton Theorems | Any linear network seen from two terminals has a simple equivalent. | Thevenin Equivalent = voltage source Vth in series with Rth | Norton Equivalent = current source IN in parallel with RN | Rth = Vth / IN
- Maximum Power Transfer | Load power peaks at a particular load. | Maximum Power Transfer = load resistance equals source resistance | AC Maximum Power = load impedance is the complex conjugate of source impedance | Efficiency at Maximum Power = 50%
- Reciprocity, Millman & Tellegen | Other theorems simplify specific problems. | Reciprocity Theorem = ratio of response to excitation unchanged when swapped | Millman's Theorem = combines parallel voltage sources into one | Tellegen's Theorem = sum of instantaneous powers in a network is zero
# Transient Analysis
- RL & RC Transients | First-order circuits respond exponentially. | Time Constant RC = τ = RC; capacitor charges to 63.2% in τ | Time Constant RL = τ = L/R | Initial Conditions = capacitor voltage and inductor current cannot change instantly
- RLC Transients | Second-order circuits can oscillate. | Damping Ratio = ζ = (R/2)√(C/L) for a series RLC | Underdamped Response = oscillatory decaying response when ζ < 1 | Critically Damped = fastest non-oscillatory response, ζ = 1
- Laplace Transform in Circuits | Laplace turns differential equations into algebra. | s-domain Impedance = R, sL and 1/(sC) | Initial Value Theorem = f(0+) = lim s·F(s) as s → ∞ | Final Value Theorem = f(∞) = lim s·F(s) as s → 0
- Step & Impulse Response | Standard inputs characterise a network. | Unit Step Response = output to a sudden constant input | Impulse Response = output to a unit impulse; inverse Laplace of H(s) | Convolution = output equals input convolved with impulse response
# Network Functions & Two-port Networks
- Network Functions | Transfer functions relate outputs to inputs. | Driving Point Impedance = ratio of voltage to current at the same port | Transfer Function = output transform divided by input transform | Poles and Zeros = roots of denominator and numerator
- Z & Y Parameters | Two-ports are described by impedance or admittance parameters. | Z-parameters = open-circuit impedance parameters | Y-parameters = short-circuit admittance parameters | Reciprocal Network = Z12 = Z21
- h & ABCD Parameters | Hybrid and transmission parameters suit transistors and cascades. | h-parameters = hybrid parameters used for transistor models | ABCD Parameters = transmission parameters relating input to output | Cascade Connection = overall ABCD is the product of individual matrices
- Interconnection of Two-ports | Two-ports combine in series, parallel and cascade. | Series Connection = Z-parameters add | Parallel Connection = Y-parameters add | Symmetrical Network = Z11 = Z22
# Resonance & Filters
- Series Resonance | Series RLC resonates when reactances cancel. | Resonant Frequency = f0 = 1/(2π√(LC)) | Q-factor = Q = ω0L/R | Bandwidth = f0 / Q
- Parallel Resonance | Parallel circuits show maximum impedance at resonance. | Anti-resonance = parallel resonance with maximum impedance | Dynamic Resistance = L/(CR) at parallel resonance | Selectivity = ability to discriminate between nearby frequencies
- Passive Filters | Filters pass desired frequencies. | Low-pass Filter = passes frequencies below cut-off | Constant-k Filter = filter with Z1·Z2 = k² independent of frequency | m-derived Filter = sharper cut-off than constant-k
- Attenuators & Coupled Circuits | Attenuators reduce signal level; coupled coils transfer energy. | Attenuator = resistive network giving fixed loss | Mutual Inductance = M = k√(L1L2) | Dot Convention = marks coil ends for polarity of mutual voltage

@ ec402 | EC-402 | Signals & Systems | Signal classification, LTI systems, convolution, Fourier series and transforms, Laplace and Z-transforms, sampling.
# Signals & Their Classification
- Continuous & Discrete Signals | Signals are defined in continuous or discrete time. | Continuous-time Signal = defined for every instant t | Discrete-time Signal = defined only at integer n | Digital Signal = discrete in both time and amplitude
- Elementary Signals | Basic signals build more complex ones. | Unit Step = u(t) = 1 for t ≥ 0, else 0 | Unit Impulse = δ(t) with unit area concentrated at t = 0 | Complex Exponential = e^(jωt), the eigenfunction of LTI systems
- Signal Operations | Signals are shifted, scaled and reflected. | Time Shifting = x(t − t0) delays a signal | Time Scaling = x(at) compresses or expands a signal | Time Reversal = x(−t) reflects the signal about t = 0
- Even, Odd, Energy & Power Signals | Signals are decomposed and classified by energy. | Even Signal = x(t) = x(−t) | Odd Signal = x(t) = −x(−t) | Energy Signal = finite energy and zero average power
# LTI Systems
- System Properties | Systems are classified by their input-output behaviour. | Linearity = superposition holds | Time Invariance = a delayed input gives an equally delayed output | Causality = output depends only on present and past inputs
- Convolution | LTI output equals input convolved with impulse response. | Convolution Integral = y(t) = ∫ x(τ) h(t − τ) dτ | Convolution Sum = y[n] = Σ x[k] h[n − k] | Commutative Property = x * h = h * x
- Stability & Impulse Response | Impulse response reveals system properties. | BIBO Stability = bounded input gives bounded output | Stability Condition = impulse response absolutely integrable | Memoryless System = output depends only on current input
- Difference & Differential Equations | LTI systems are described by constant-coefficient equations. | Difference Equation = relates y[n] to past outputs and inputs | FIR System = finite-duration impulse response | IIR System = infinite-duration impulse response with feedback
# Fourier Analysis
- Fourier Series | Periodic signals are sums of harmonics. | Exponential Fourier Series = x(t) = Σ ck e^(jkω0t) | Parseval's Theorem = signal power equals sum of squared coefficient magnitudes | Gibbs Phenomenon = overshoot near discontinuities in partial sums
- Continuous-time Fourier Transform | CTFT gives the spectrum of aperiodic signals. | CTFT = X(jω) = ∫ x(t) e^(−jωt) dt | Duality = time and frequency roles can be swapped | Convolution Property = convolution in time is multiplication in frequency
- Discrete-time Fourier Transform | DTFT is periodic in frequency. | DTFT = X(e^jω) = Σ x[n] e^(−jωn) | Periodicity of DTFT = DTFT repeats every 2π | Frequency Response = DTFT of the impulse response
- DFT & FFT | Computable transforms of finite sequences. | DFT = N-point transform of a finite sequence | FFT = fast algorithm computing DFT in O(N log N) | Circular Convolution = convolution corresponding to DFT multiplication
# Laplace & Z-Transform
- Laplace Transform & ROC | Laplace generalises Fourier analysis. | Bilateral Laplace = X(s) = ∫ x(t) e^(−st) dt over all t | ROC = region of s-plane where the transform converges | Stable Causal System = all poles in the left half s-plane
- Z-Transform | Z-transform is the discrete counterpart of Laplace. | Z-Transform = X(z) = Σ x[n] z^(−n) | ROC of Causal Sequence = exterior of a circle | Stability in z-domain = all poles inside the unit circle
- Inverse Z-Transform | Sequences are recovered from X(z). | Partial Fraction Method = expands X(z)/z into simple terms | Power Series Method = long division gives samples directly | Residue Method = contour integration using residues
- System Analysis with Transforms | Transforms give transfer functions and responses. | Transfer Function H(z) = Y(z)/X(z) | Pole-Zero Plot = locations of poles and zeros in the complex plane | Initial Value Theorem (Z) = x[0] = lim X(z) as z → ∞
# Sampling & Correlation
- Sampling Theorem | Sampling links continuous and discrete signals. | Sampling Theorem = signal recoverable if fs > 2fmax | Aliasing = spectral overlap from undersampling | Nyquist Rate = twice the highest frequency
- Reconstruction | Samples are interpolated back into a continuous signal. | Ideal Reconstruction = sinc interpolation by an ideal low-pass filter | Zero-order Hold = holds each sample until the next | Interpolation = estimating values between samples
- Correlation | Correlation measures similarity between signals. | Cross-correlation = similarity of one signal with a shifted other | Autocorrelation = correlation of a signal with itself | Power Spectral Density = Fourier transform of autocorrelation
- Random Signals | Random processes model noise. | Random Process = ensemble of random signals indexed by time | Wide-sense Stationary = constant mean and shift-invariant autocorrelation | White Noise = flat power spectral density

@ ec403 | EC-403 | Analog Communication | Amplitude, angle and pulse modulation, transmitters, receivers, noise and performance.
# Amplitude Modulation
- AM Fundamentals | AM varies carrier amplitude with the message. | Modulation Index = m = Am/Ac; overmodulation when m > 1 | AM Spectrum = carrier plus upper and lower sidebands | Total Power = Pt = Pc(1 + m²/2)
- AM Generation | AM is produced by linear or square-law methods. | Square-law Modulator = uses non-linear device to produce AM | Collector Modulation = high-level AM in the final stage | Switching Modulator = diode switching to generate AM
- AM Detection | Detectors recover the message from AM. | Envelope Detector = diode-RC circuit following the envelope | Diagonal Clipping = distortion when RC is too large | Square-law Detector = demodulates small-signal AM
- DSB-SC, SSB & VSB | Variants save power or bandwidth. | DSB-SC = carrier suppressed; needs coherent detection | SSB = one sideband; half the AM bandwidth | VSB = vestigial sideband used for TV video
# Angle Modulation
- Frequency Modulation | FM varies carrier frequency with the message. | Frequency Deviation = Δf = kf × Am | FM Modulation Index = β = Δf / fm | Narrowband FM = β much less than 1, bandwidth about 2fm
- FM Spectrum & Bandwidth | FM produces many sidebands given by Bessel functions. | Bessel Function Coefficients = amplitudes of FM sidebands | Carson's Rule = BW ≈ 2(Δf + fm) | Wideband FM = β greater than 1
- FM Generation | FM is generated directly or indirectly. | Direct Method = varactor varies oscillator frequency | Armstrong Method = indirect FM via narrowband PM and frequency multiplication | Frequency Multiplier = multiplies carrier and deviation
- FM Detection | Discriminators convert frequency changes to amplitude. | Foster-Seeley Discriminator = phase-shift discriminator | Ratio Detector = discriminator insensitive to amplitude variations | PLL Demodulator = phase-locked loop tracks frequency changes
# Transmitters & Receivers
- AM & FM Transmitters | Transmitters generate and amplify modulated signals. | Low-level Modulation = modulation before final amplification | High-level Modulation = modulation at the final stage | Pre-emphasis = boosting high audio frequencies before FM transmission
- Tuned Radio Frequency Receiver | Early receivers amplify at RF directly. | TRF Receiver = cascaded tuned RF amplifiers | Selectivity Problem = bandwidth varies with tuning | Sensitivity = minimum input for a specified output
- Superheterodyne Receiver | Converting to a fixed IF gives uniform performance. | Intermediate Frequency = fixed frequency, e.g. 455 kHz for AM | Image Frequency = fs + 2IF, rejected by RF stage | Mixer = combines signal and local oscillator to produce IF
- AGC & Receiver Characteristics | Control circuits keep output steady. | AGC = automatic gain control adjusting gain with signal strength | Fidelity = faithful reproduction of all modulating frequencies | De-emphasis = restores audio balance after pre-emphasis
# Noise in Communication
- Sources of Noise | Noise originates inside and outside the system. | Thermal Noise = Pn = kTB | Shot Noise = random arrival of charge carriers | Atmospheric Noise = noise from lightning and natural sources
- Noise Figure & Temperature | Noise is quantified for amplifiers and receivers. | Noise Figure = ratio of input SNR to output SNR | Friis Formula = overall noise factor of cascaded stages | Equivalent Noise Temperature = Te = (F − 1)T0
- Noise in AM Systems | AM performance depends on detection method. | Figure of Merit = output SNR divided by channel SNR | Threshold Effect = sharp SNR drop in envelope detection at low input | Coherent Detection = synchronous demodulation with a local carrier
- Noise in FM Systems | FM trades bandwidth for noise performance. | FM Noise Advantage = output SNR grows with β² | Capture Effect = stronger FM signal suppresses the weaker | FM Threshold = minimum carrier-to-noise ratio for good FM performance
# Pulse Modulation & Multiplexing
- Sampling & PAM | Pulse modulation starts with sampling. | Natural Sampling = pulse tops follow the signal | Flat-top Sampling = sample-and-hold pulses | Aperture Effect = distortion from flat-top pulse width
- PWM & PPM | Pulse timing carries the message. | PWM = pulse width proportional to sample value | PPM = pulse position varies with sample value | PPM Generation = derived from PWM trailing edges
- Frequency Division Multiplexing | FDM stacks signals in frequency. | FDM = signals placed in separate frequency bands | Guard Band = unused band separating channels | Group and Supergroup = FDM hierarchy in telephony
- Time Division Multiplexing | TDM interleaves samples in time. | TDM = signals share time slots | Frame = one cycle of all channel samples | Synchronisation = aligning receiver timing with transmitter

@ ec404 | EC-404 | Control System | Control system modelling, time response, stability, root locus, frequency response and state-space analysis.
# Introduction & Modelling
- Control System Basics | Control systems regulate outputs using inputs and feedback. | Open-loop System = output has no effect on control action | Closed-loop System = output fed back to correct errors | Feedback = comparison of output with reference
- Transfer Function | Transfer functions model LTI systems in s-domain. | Transfer Function = Laplace output over input with zero initial conditions | Order of System = highest power of s in the denominator | Characteristic Equation = denominator of closed-loop TF set to zero
- Block Diagram Reduction | Block diagrams combine into one transfer function. | Closed-loop TF = G/(1 + GH) for negative feedback | Cascade Blocks = transfer functions multiply | Moving a Summing Point = rules preserve the overall TF
- Signal Flow Graph & Mason's Gain | SFGs and Mason's rule find overall gain. | Signal Flow Graph = nodes and branches representing signals | Mason's Gain Formula = T = Σ Pk Δk / Δ | Non-touching Loops = loops with no common node
# Time Response Analysis
- Standard Test Signals | Systems are tested with standard inputs. | Step Input = sudden constant change | Ramp Input = linearly increasing input | Impulse Response = inverse Laplace of the transfer function
- First & Second Order Response | Order determines response shape. | Time Constant = time to reach 63.2% for a first-order step response | Natural Frequency = ωn, oscillation frequency without damping | Damping Ratio = ζ determines oscillation and overshoot
- Time-domain Specifications | Specifications describe transient performance. | Peak Overshoot = Mp = e^(−πζ/√(1 − ζ²)) | Settling Time = about 4/(ζωn) for 2% band | Rise Time = time from 10% to 90% of final value
- Steady-state Error | Error constants measure steady-state accuracy. | Type of System = number of poles at the origin | Position Error Constant = Kp = lim G(s)H(s) as s → 0 | Velocity Error Constant = Kv = lim s·G(s)H(s) as s → 0
# Stability Analysis
- Concept of Stability | Stable systems return to equilibrium. | BIBO Stability = bounded input gives bounded output | Absolute Stability = whether the system is stable or not | Relative Stability = how close a stable system is to instability
- Routh-Hurwitz Criterion | Routh array checks stability without solving roots. | Routh Array = tabulation from characteristic equation coefficients | Sign Changes = number equals roots in right half-plane | Row of Zeros = indicates symmetric roots; use auxiliary equation
- Root Locus | Root locus tracks closed-loop poles as gain varies. | Root Locus = path of closed-loop poles as K goes from 0 to ∞ | Asymptote Angle = (2q + 1)180° / (P − Z) | Breakaway Point = where loci leave the real axis
- Effect of Adding Poles & Zeros | Pole-zero placement shapes response. | Adding a Zero = tends to increase stability and speed | Adding a Pole = tends to reduce stability | Dominant Poles = poles nearest the jω-axis govern response
# Frequency Response Analysis
