@ es301 | ES-301 | Energy & Environmental Engineering | Energy resources, ecosystems, biodiversity, pollution control and environmental policy.
# Energy Science & Resources
- Energy Systems & Resource Classification | Energy resources are classed by origin and renewability; engineering decisions weigh availability, cost and environmental impact. | Renewable Energy = source replenished naturally on a human timescale, e.g. solar, wind, hydro | Non-renewable Energy = finite stock such as coal, oil, natural gas and nuclear fuel | Primary Energy = energy in its natural form before any conversion
- Solar Energy | Solar radiation is harvested as heat (collectors) or electricity (photovoltaics). | Photovoltaic Cell = semiconductor p-n junction that converts sunlight directly into DC electricity | Flat Plate Collector = absorber plate under glazing that heats water or air for low-temperature use | Solar Constant = about 1367 W/m² of radiation received outside Earth's atmosphere
- Wind, Hydro & Tidal Power | Kinetic and potential energy of moving air and water drive turbines. | Betz Limit = maximum theoretical wind-turbine efficiency of 59.3% | Hydroelectric Power = electricity from water head driving a turbine, P = ρgQH·η | Tidal Energy = power from the rise and fall of sea level due to lunar gravity
- Biomass, Geothermal & Fuel Cells | Alternative sources convert chemical or earth heat energy into usable power. | Biogas = methane-rich gas from anaerobic digestion of organic waste | Geothermal Energy = heat extracted from the Earth's interior via hot water or steam | Fuel Cell = electrochemical device converting hydrogen and oxygen into electricity and water
# Ecosystems
- Structure & Function of an Ecosystem | An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with its physical environment. | Producers = autotrophs such as green plants that fix solar energy by photosynthesis | Consumers = heterotrophs that feed on producers or other consumers | Decomposers = bacteria and fungi that break dead matter into nutrients
- Energy Flow & Food Chains | Energy moves one way through trophic levels while nutrients cycle. | Food Chain = linear sequence of who eats whom in an ecosystem | Food Web = interlocking network of many food chains | 10 Percent Law = only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level
- Ecological Pyramids & Succession | Pyramids summarise number, biomass and energy at each level; succession is gradual community change. | Pyramid of Energy = always upright because energy is lost at every level | Ecological Succession = orderly replacement of communities until a climax community forms | Pioneer Species = first organisms to colonise bare or disturbed land
- Biogeochemical Cycles | Matter such as carbon, nitrogen and water cycles between living and non-living parts. | Carbon Cycle = exchange of carbon via photosynthesis, respiration, combustion and decay | Nitrogen Fixation = conversion of atmospheric N₂ into ammonia by bacteria or lightning | Hydrological Cycle = continuous movement of water by evaporation, condensation and precipitation
# Biodiversity & Its Conservation
- Levels & Value of Biodiversity | Biodiversity is the variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels. | Genetic Diversity = variation of genes within a species | Species Diversity = variety and abundance of species in a region | Ecosystem Diversity = variety of habitats and ecological processes
- Hotspots & India as a Mega-diversity Nation | Some regions hold exceptional endemic species under high threat. | Biodiversity Hotspot = region with at least 1500 endemic plants that has lost 70% of habitat | Endemic Species = species found naturally only in one area | Mega-diversity Nation = country holding a large share of world species; India is one of 17
- Threats to Biodiversity | Habitat loss, poaching and invasive species drive extinction. | Habitat Fragmentation = breaking continuous habitat into isolated patches | Poaching = illegal hunting of wildlife for trade | Man-Wildlife Conflict = clashes when animals and people compete for space and resources
- In-situ & Ex-situ Conservation | Species are protected either in their natural habitat or outside it. | In-situ Conservation = protection in natural habitat, e.g. national parks and biosphere reserves | Ex-situ Conservation = conservation outside habitat, e.g. zoos, seed banks, botanical gardens | Biosphere Reserve = protected area with core, buffer and transition zones
# Environmental Pollution
- Air Pollution | Gases and particulates from combustion and industry degrade air quality. | Particulate Matter = suspended solid or liquid particles such as PM2.5 and PM10 | Electrostatic Precipitator = device that charges particles and collects them on plates | Photochemical Smog = secondary pollution formed when NOx and hydrocarbons react in sunlight
- Water Pollution & Treatment | Sewage and industrial effluent contaminate water; treatment restores quality. | BOD = biochemical oxygen demand, oxygen needed by microbes to decompose organic matter | Eutrophication = excessive nutrient enrichment causing algal blooms and oxygen depletion | Activated Sludge Process = aerobic secondary treatment using microbial floc
- Soil, Noise & Thermal Pollution | Other pollution forms affect land, hearing and aquatic life. | Decibel = logarithmic unit of sound intensity; above about 85 dB risks hearing damage | Thermal Pollution = degradation of water quality by discharge of heated water | Soil Erosion = removal of fertile topsoil by wind or water
- Solid Waste & Hazardous Waste Management | Waste is reduced, segregated, treated and safely disposed. | Sanitary Landfill = engineered disposal site with liner and leachate collection | Composting = aerobic decomposition of organic waste into manure | 3R Principle = reduce, reuse and recycle to minimise waste
# Social Issues & Environmental Policy
- Climate Change & Global Warming | Greenhouse gases trap heat and change the climate system. | Greenhouse Effect = trapping of infrared radiation by gases such as CO₂ and CH₄ | Ozone Depletion = thinning of the stratospheric ozone layer by CFCs | Carbon Footprint = total greenhouse gas emissions caused by an activity
- Sustainable Development | Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations. | Sustainable Development = growth that balances economy, society and environment | Rainwater Harvesting = collecting and storing rainwater for reuse or recharge | Environmental Impact Assessment = study of a project's likely environmental effects before approval
- Environmental Laws in India | Legislation gives powers to prevent and control pollution. | Environment Protection Act 1986 = umbrella law empowering the Centre to protect the environment | Water Act 1974 = law for prevention and control of water pollution | Air Act 1981 = law for prevention and control of air pollution
- International Protocols & Public Awareness | Global agreements coordinate action on shared environmental problems. | Montreal Protocol = 1987 treaty phasing out ozone-depleting substances | Kyoto Protocol = 1997 agreement setting binding emission cuts for developed nations | Paris Agreement = 2015 pact to limit warming well below 2 °C

# Numerical Solution of Equations
- Bisection & Regula-Falsi Methods | Bracketing methods trap a root between two points with opposite function signs. | Bisection Method = halves the interval [a,b] each step; always converges, but linearly | Regula-Falsi = joins f(a) and f(b) with a chord and uses its x-intercept as the next guess | Intermediate Value Theorem = if f(a)·f(b) < 0 a continuous f has a root in (a,b)
- Newton-Raphson Method | An open method using the tangent line for fast convergence. | Newton-Raphson Formula = x(n+1) = x(n) − f(x(n))/f'(x(n)) | Quadratic Convergence = error roughly squares each iteration near the root | Failure Case = method breaks down when f'(x) is zero or near zero
- Solution of Linear Systems | Direct and iterative methods solve AX = B. | Gauss Elimination = reduces the system to upper-triangular form then back-substitutes | Gauss-Seidel Method = iterative method using latest updated values immediately | Diagonal Dominance = each diagonal element's magnitude is at least the sum of the other magnitudes in its row; guarantees iterative convergence
- Errors in Numerical Computation | Every numerical answer carries rounding and truncation error. | Absolute Error = magnitude of (true value − approximate value) | Relative Error = absolute error divided by the true value | Truncation Error = error from cutting off an infinite process such as a series
# Interpolation & Finite Differences
- Finite Difference Operators | Operators relate tabulated values at equal spacing h. | Forward Difference = Δf(x) = f(x+h) − f(x) | Backward Difference = ∇f(x) = f(x) − f(x−h) | Shift Operator = E f(x) = f(x+h), with E = 1 + Δ
- Newton's Interpolation Formulae | Polynomials through equally spaced points estimate intermediate values. | Newton Forward Formula = used to interpolate near the beginning of a table | Newton Backward Formula = used to interpolate near the end of a table | Phase Variable u = (x − x0)/h used inside the formulae
- Lagrange & Divided Differences | Unequally spaced data need different interpolation formulas. | Lagrange Interpolation = weighted sum of y-values using basis polynomials L_i(x) | Divided Difference = [x0,x1] = (y1 − y0)/(x1 − x0), generalised recursively | Newton Divided Difference Formula = interpolation for unequal intervals built from divided differences
- Central Difference Formulae | Central formulas give better accuracy near the middle of a table. | Central Difference = δf(x) = f(x + h/2) − f(x − h/2) | Stirling Formula = average of Gauss forward and backward formulae | Bessel Formula = central formula best when u is near 1/2
# Numerical Differentiation & Integration
- Numerical Differentiation | Derivatives are estimated from interpolating polynomials. | Forward Derivative Formula = dy/dx at x0 ≈ (1/h)(Δy0 − Δ²y0/2 + Δ³y0/3 …) | Backward Derivative Formula = used for derivatives near the end of a table | Step Size h = smaller h lowers truncation error but raises round-off error
- Trapezoidal Rule | Area under a curve approximated by trapezoids. | Trapezoidal Rule = ∫ ≈ (h/2)[y0 + 2(y1 + … + y(n−1)) + yn] | Error Order = global error proportional to h² | Applicability = works for any number of intervals
- Simpson's Rules | Parabolic and cubic fits give higher accuracy. | Simpson 1/3 Rule = (h/3)[y0 + 4(odd ordinates) + 2(even ordinates) + yn]; n must be even | Simpson 3/8 Rule = (3h/8)[y0 + 3y1 + 3y2 + 2y3 + …]; n a multiple of 3 | Accuracy = Simpson 1/3 error is of order h⁴
- Gaussian Quadrature | Optimally placed points integrate polynomials exactly with few evaluations. | Gauss-Legendre Quadrature = uses roots of Legendre polynomials on [−1,1] | Two-Point Rule = exact for polynomials up to degree 3 | Weight Function = coefficient multiplying each sampled function value
# Numerical Solution of ODEs
- Taylor Series & Picard's Method | Series-based methods approximate the solution of y' = f(x,y). | Taylor Series Method = expands y about x0 using successive derivatives | Picard's Method = successive approximation by integrating y(n+1) = y0 + ∫ f(x, y(n)) dx | Initial Value Problem = ODE with y specified at a starting point x0
- Euler & Modified Euler Methods | Step-by-step methods march along small increments h. | Euler Method = y(n+1) = y(n) + h·f(x(n), y(n)) | Modified Euler = averages slopes at both ends of the step for better accuracy | Local Error = Euler error per step is of order h²
- Runge-Kutta Methods | RK methods use weighted slopes inside each step. | RK4 Formula = y(n+1) = y(n) + (k1 + 2k2 + 2k3 + k4)/6 | Fourth-Order Accuracy = global error of order h⁴ | Self-starting = needs only the previous value, not past history
- Predictor-Corrector Methods | Multistep methods predict and then correct each new value. | Milne's Method = predictor-corrector using four previous values | Adams-Bashforth Method = explicit multistep predictor | Corrector = formula that refines the predicted value iteratively
# Transforms & Probability
- Laplace Transform | Converts linear ODEs into algebraic equations. | Laplace Transform = L{f(t)} = ∫0^∞ e^(−st) f(t) dt | First Shifting Theorem = L{e^(at) f(t)} = F(s − a) | Inverse Laplace = recovering f(t) from F(s), often via partial fractions
- Fourier Series & Transform | Periodic and aperiodic signals expressed through sinusoids. | Fourier Series = periodic function as a0/2 + Σ(an cos nx + bn sin nx) | Dirichlet Conditions = sufficient conditions for convergence of a Fourier series | Fourier Transform = F(ω) = ∫ f(t) e^(−iωt) dt for non-periodic signals
- Probability Distributions | Discrete and continuous models describe random variables. | Binomial Distribution = P(X = r) = nCr p^r q^(n−r); mean np | Poisson Distribution = P(X = r) = e^(−λ) λ^r / r!; mean = variance = λ | Normal Distribution = bell-shaped continuous distribution defined by mean μ and σ
- Correlation, Regression & Hypothesis Tests | Statistical tools measure relationships and test claims. | Karl Pearson Coefficient = r = cov(x,y)/(σx σy), between −1 and +1 | Regression Line = best-fit line predicting y from x by least squares | Chi-square Test = tests goodness of fit or independence of attributes
