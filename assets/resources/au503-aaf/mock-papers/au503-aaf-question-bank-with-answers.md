## Unit 2: Alcohols & Biodiesel

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Ethanol*.  
  **Ans.** alcohol fuel from sugarcane or grains
- **Q.** Define *Methanol*.  
  **Ans.** alcohol from natural gas or coal
- **Q.** Define *High Octane*.  
  **Ans.** alcohols resist knock
- **Q.** Define *Flex Fuel Vehicle*.  
  **Ans.** runs on variable ethanol blends
- **Q.** Define *Cold Starting Problem*.  
  **Ans.** high latent heat of alcohols
- **Q.** Define *Material Compatibility*.  
  **Ans.** alcohols corrode some metals and rubbers
- **Q.** Define *Biodiesel*.  
  **Ans.** fatty acid methyl esters from oils
- **Q.** Define *Transesterification*.  
  **Ans.** reaction of oil with alcohol and catalyst
- **Q.** Define *B20*.  
  **Ans.** 20% biodiesel blend with diesel
- **Q.** Define *High Viscosity*.  
  **Ans.** poor atomisation of raw vegetable oil
- **Q.** Define *Cold Flow Properties*.  
  **Ans.** biodiesel gels in cold
- **Q.** Define *Lower NOx Trade-off*.  
  **Ans.** biodiesel may raise NOx

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Alcohol Fuels* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Ethanol and methanol suit SI engines. Ethanol — alcohol fuel from sugarcane or grains; Methanol — alcohol from natural gas or coal; High Octane — alcohols resist knock.
- **Q.** Explain *Alcohol in Engines* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Engines need modification for alcohols. Flex Fuel Vehicle — runs on variable ethanol blends; Cold Starting Problem — high latent heat of alcohols; Material Compatibility — alcohols corrode some metals and rubbers.
- **Q.** Explain *Biodiesel* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Vegetable oils converted for diesel engines. Biodiesel — fatty acid methyl esters from oils; Transesterification — reaction of oil with alcohol and catalyst; B20 — 20% biodiesel blend with diesel.
- **Q.** Explain *Vegetable Oils & Biodiesel Issues* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Straight oils have problems. High Viscosity — poor atomisation of raw vegetable oil; Cold Flow Properties — biodiesel gels in cold; Lower NOx Trade-off — biodiesel may raise NOx.

## Unit 3: Gaseous Fuels

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *CNG*.  
  **Ans.** compressed natural gas, mainly methane, stored at about 200 bar
- **Q.** Define *LPG*.  
  **Ans.** mixture of propane and butane stored as liquid
- **Q.** Define *Bi-fuel Vehicle*.  
  **Ans.** runs on petrol or gas
- **Q.** Define *Hydrogen*.  
  **Ans.** zero-carbon fuel with wide flammability
- **Q.** Define *Hydrogen Storage*.  
  **Ans.** compressed, liquid or metal hydride
- **Q.** Define *Backfire*.  
  **Ans.** flashback problem in hydrogen engines
- **Q.** Define *Biogas*.  
  **Ans.** methane and CO2 from digestion
- **Q.** Define *Producer Gas*.  
  **Ans.** CO-rich gas from biomass gasification
- **Q.** Define *Scrubbing*.  
  **Ans.** removes CO2 and H2S from biogas
- **Q.** Define *PEM Fuel Cell*.  
  **Ans.** proton exchange membrane cell for vehicles
- **Q.** Define *FCEV*.  
  **Ans.** fuel cell electric vehicle
- **Q.** Define *Fuel Cell Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** higher than IC engines

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *CNG & LPG* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Gaseous fuels are cleaner than liquids. CNG — compressed natural gas, mainly methane, stored at about 200 bar; LPG — mixture of propane and butane stored as liquid; Bi-fuel Vehicle — runs on petrol or gas.
- **Q.** Explain *Hydrogen as Fuel* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Hydrogen burns without carbon. Hydrogen — zero-carbon fuel with wide flammability; Hydrogen Storage — compressed, liquid or metal hydride; Backfire — flashback problem in hydrogen engines.
- **Q.** Explain *Biogas & Producer Gas* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Gases from biomass. Biogas — methane and CO2 from digestion; Producer Gas — CO-rich gas from biomass gasification; Scrubbing — removes CO2 and H2S from biogas.
- **Q.** Explain *Fuel Cells for Vehicles* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Fuel cells convert hydrogen directly to electricity. PEM Fuel Cell — proton exchange membrane cell for vehicles; FCEV — fuel cell electric vehicle; Fuel Cell Efficiency — higher than IC engines.

## Unit 4: Emission Formation

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *CO Formation*.  
  **Ans.** incomplete combustion in rich mixtures
- **Q.** Define *HC Formation*.  
  **Ans.** unburned fuel from crevices and wall quenching
- **Q.** Define *NOx Formation*.  
  **Ans.** high-temperature reaction of N2 and O2
- **Q.** Define *Particulate Matter*.  
  **Ans.** carbon soot with adsorbed hydrocarbons
- **Q.** Define *Smoke*.  
  **Ans.** visible particulates
- **Q.** Define *NOx-PM Trade-off*.  
  **Ans.** reducing one tends to increase the other
- **Q.** Define *Evaporative Emissions*.  
  **Ans.** fuel vapour from tank
- **Q.** Define *Blow-by Gases*.  
  **Ans.** gases leaking past rings into crankcase
- **Q.** Define *PCV Valve*.  
  **Ans.** positive crankcase ventilation
- **Q.** Define *CO Toxicity*.  
  **Ans.** binds with haemoglobin
- **Q.** Define *Photochemical Smog*.  
  **Ans.** from NOx and HC in sunlight
- **Q.** Define *Greenhouse Gas*.  
  **Ans.** CO2 contributes to warming

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Pollutants from SI Engines* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** SI engines emit CO, HC and NOx. CO Formation — incomplete combustion in rich mixtures; HC Formation — unburned fuel from crevices and wall quenching; NOx Formation — high-temperature reaction of N2 and O2.
- **Q.** Explain *Pollutants from CI Engines* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Diesel emits soot and NOx. Particulate Matter — carbon soot with adsorbed hydrocarbons; Smoke — visible particulates; NOx-PM Trade-off — reducing one tends to increase the other.
- **Q.** Explain *Evaporative & Crankcase Emissions* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Non-exhaust emissions exist. Evaporative Emissions — fuel vapour from tank; Blow-by Gases — gases leaking past rings into crankcase; PCV Valve — positive crankcase ventilation.
- **Q.** Explain *Effects of Pollutants* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Pollutants harm health and environment. CO Toxicity — binds with haemoglobin; Photochemical Smog — from NOx and HC in sunlight; Greenhouse Gas — CO2 contributes to warming.

## Unit 5: Emission Control & Norms

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Lean Burn*.  
  **Ans.** excess air lowers CO and HC
- **Q.** Define *EGR*.  
  **Ans.** recirculated exhaust lowers combustion temperature
- **Q.** Define *Injection Timing Retard*.  
  **Ans.** reduces NOx in diesel engines
- **Q.** Define *Catalytic Converter*.  
  **Ans.** platinum, palladium and rhodium catalysts
- **Q.** Define *DOC*.  
  **Ans.** diesel oxidation catalyst
- **Q.** Define *SCR with AdBlue*.  
  **Ans.** urea solution reduces NOx
- **Q.** Define *NDIR Analyser*.  
  **Ans.** measures CO and CO2
- **Q.** Define *FID*.  
  **Ans.** flame ionisation detector for hydrocarbons
- **Q.** Define *Chemiluminescence Analyser*.  
  **Ans.** measures NOx
- **Q.** Define *Bharat Stage Norms*.  
  **Ans.** Indian emission standards
- **Q.** Define *BS-VI*.  
  **Ans.** equivalent to Euro VI, effective 2020
- **Q.** Define *Driving Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** standard test cycle, e.g. MIDC

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Engine Modifications* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Design changes reduce emissions. Lean Burn — excess air lowers CO and HC; EGR — recirculated exhaust lowers combustion temperature; Injection Timing Retard — reduces NOx in diesel engines.
- **Q.** Explain *Aftertreatment Devices* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Exhaust treatment removes pollutants. Catalytic Converter — platinum, palladium and rhodium catalysts; DOC — diesel oxidation catalyst; SCR with AdBlue — urea solution reduces NOx.
- **Q.** Explain *Emission Measurement* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Instruments measure exhaust pollutants. NDIR Analyser — measures CO and CO2; FID — flame ionisation detector for hydrocarbons; Chemiluminescence Analyser — measures NOx.
- **Q.** Explain *Emission Norms* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Standards limit vehicle emissions. Bharat Stage Norms — Indian emission standards; BS-VI — equivalent to Euro VI, effective 2020; Driving Cycle — standard test cycle, e.g. MIDC.
