- **Q1(a):** Two-stage Compression: with flash intercooling
- **Q1(b):** Cascade System: high-temperature and low-temperature cycles | Cascade Condenser: links two cycles | Refrigerant Selection: suitable for each temperature range
- **Q2(a):** Low-grade Heat: drives adsorption cycles
- **Q2(b):** Steam Ejector Refrigeration: jet pump compression | Thermoelectric Cooling: Peltier effect | Figure of Merit ZT: thermoelectric material performance
- **Q3(a):** Hydrocarbons: R290 propane, R600a isobutane
- **Q3(b):** Safety Classification: A1 to B3 classes | Leak Detection: sensors and soap tests | Charge Limits: restrict flammable refrigerant quantity
- **Q4(a):** Stirling Cryocooler: regenerative cycle cooler
- **Q4(b):** Cryogenics: science of temperatures below about 123 K | Liquid Nitrogen: boils at 77 K | Liquid Helium: boils at 4.2 K
- **Q5(a):** Vacuum Jacket: eliminates convection
- **Q5(b):** Boil-off: evaporation due to heat leak | Transfer Line: vacuum-insulated piping | Cool-down: bringing equipment to cryogenic temperature
- **Q6(a):** Ejector: recovers expansion work
- **Q6(b):** Two-stage Compression: with flash intercooling | Optimum Intermediate Pressure: √(p1 p2) approximately | Flash Gas Removal: improves efficiency
- **Q7(a):** ODP: ozone depletion potential
- **Q7(b):** Montreal Protocol: phased out CFCs | Kigali Amendment: phase-down of HFCs | HFO Refrigerants: low-GWP alternatives such as R1234yf
- **Q8(a):** Transfer Line: vacuum-insulated piping
- **Q8(b):** Ductile-brittle Transition: carbon steels become brittle | Austenitic Stainless Steel: suitable for cryogenic use | Thermal Contraction: large shrinkage on cooling
