### Question 1: Explain the Disaster Management Cycle with all its phases. Distinguish between Structural and Non-Structural Mitigation with examples.
**Solution:**
The Disaster Management Cycle consists of two cyclical phases divided by the event horizon:
1. **Pre-Disaster Phase (Risk Reduction):**
   - **Prevention & Mitigation:** Structural or non-structural measures undertaken to limit the adverse impacts of hazards.
   - **Preparedness:** Action plans, emergency drills, training of first responders, and Early Warning Systems (EWS) developed before hazard strikes.
2. **Post-Disaster Phase (Recovery):**
   - **Immediate Emergency Response & Relief:** Evacuation, search and rescue (NDRF), medical triage, emergency shelters, food rationing.
   - **Rehabilitation:** Restoring critical lifeline infrastructure (drinking water, electricity grids, transport links).
   - **Reconstruction:** Permanent rebuilding incorporating "Build Back Better" principles to ensure new structures withstand future events.

**Structural vs Non-Structural Mitigation:**
| Dimension | Structural Mitigation | Non-Structural Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Any physical construction to reduce or avoid possible hazard impacts. | Policies, awareness, knowledge development, practices, or agreements. |
| **Technological Basis** | Civil engineering, seismic architecture, hydrological dikes. | Law, urban zoning, building codes, insurance, environmental protection. |
| **Examples** | - Constructing flood embankments along rivers.<br>- Retrofitting buildings with base isolators.<br>- Constructing cyclone shelters on stilts. | - Enforcing Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) rules.<br>- Mandatory earthquake mock drills in schools.<br>- Crop insurance against prolonged droughts. |

### Question 2: A magnitude 7.0 earthquake strikes an urban region. Compare its energy release with a magnitude 5.0 earthquake. Explain why small magnitude differences represent catastrophic increases in destructive power.
**Solution:**
**Mathematical Energy Scaling Formula:**
The Gutenberg-Richter energy-magnitude relation is:
$$\log_{10}(E) = 4.8 + 1.5 M$$
Where $E$ is radiant seismic energy in Joules and $M$ is moment magnitude.

For two earthquakes with magnitudes $M_1 = 7.0$ and $M_2 = 5.0$:
$$\log_{10}\left(\frac{E_1}{E_2}\right) = 1.5(M_1 - M_2) = 1.5(7.0 - 5.0) = 1.5 \times 2 = 3.0$$
Taking the anti-log:
$$\frac{E_1}{E_2} = 10^{3.0} = 1000$$

**Conclusion:**
- A magnitude 7.0 earthquake releases **exactly 1,000 times more seismic energy** than a magnitude 5.0 earthquake ($31.62 \times 31.62 \approx 1000$).
- While ground displacement amplitude increases by $10^{7 - 5} = 100\times$, the explosive kinetic energy released into fault lines scales by $1000\times$. This exponential surge causes catastrophic collapse of unreinforced masonry structures.
