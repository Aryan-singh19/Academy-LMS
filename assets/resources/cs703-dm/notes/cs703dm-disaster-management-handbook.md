## Unit 1: Core Disaster Management Concepts & Paradigms
### 1. The Fundamental Disaster Equation
$$\text{Disaster Risk} = \frac{\text{Hazard} \times \text{Vulnerability} \times \text{Exposure}}{\text{Capacity}}$$
- **Hazard ($H$):** A dangerous physical event, phenomenon, or human activity that may cause loss of life, injury, property damage, or environmental degradation.
- **Vulnerability ($V$):** The conditions determined by physical, social, economic, and environmental factors which increase susceptibility of an individual or community.
- **Exposure ($E$):** The people, infrastructure, housing, and production capacities present in hazard zones.
- **Capacity ($C$):** The combination of all strengths, attributes, and resources available within a community to manage and reduce disaster risks.

### 2. The Disaster Management Life Cycle
```
                      [PRE-DISASTER PHASE]
               Mitigation & Prevention (Hazard reduction)
                              |
                     Preparedness (Drills, EWS)
                              v
------------------------ [EVENT OCCURS] ------------------------

```

## Unit 2: Natural & Technological Hazards
### 1. Earthquakes & Seismic Zoning
- **Elastic Rebound Theory:** Tectonic stresses accumulate until frictional resistance on fault planes is exceeded, violently releasing stored elastic strain energy as seismic waves ($P$-waves, $S$-waves, surface waves).
- **Richter vs Mercalli Scales:**
  - *Richter Scale (Magnitude $M$):* Quantitative measure of total energy released at hypocenter. Logarithmic: each unit increase represents $\approx 31.6\times$ more energy.
  - *Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI):* Qualitative measure of observed surface shaking, destruction, and human reaction ($I$ to $XII$).
- **Indian Seismic Zones (IS 1893:2016):**
  - **Zone II:** Low damage risk ($Z = 0.10$).
  - **Zone III:** Moderate damage risk ($Z = 0.16$).
  - **Zone IV:** Severe damage risk ($Z = 0.24$) - Delhi, parts of Maharashtra, Bihar.
  - **Zone V:** Very severe damage risk ($Z = 0.36$) - Entire Northeast India, Kashmir, Kutch, Andaman & Nicobar.

## Unit 3: Institutional Framework & Sendai Framework
### 1. Disaster Management Act, 2005 (India)
Established a 3-tier statutory institutional hierarchy:
1. **NDMA (National Disaster Management Authority):** Headed by the Prime Minister of India. Formulates apex national policies, guidelines, and plans.
2. **SDMA (State Disaster Management Authority):** Headed by the respective Chief Minister. Coordinates state-level mitigation and budget allocation.
3. **DDMA (District Disaster Management Authority):** Headed by the District Collector / District Magistrate as Chairperson and Zilla Parishad President as Co-Chairperson. The frontline executive arm for disaster operations.
- **NDRF (National Disaster Response Force):** Specialized multi-disciplinary force trained in chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear (CBRN), and structural collapse search and rescue.

### 2. Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (2015–2030)
Adopted at the 3rd UN World Conference in Sendai, Japan. 4 Priorities for Action:
1. **Priority 1:** Understanding disaster risk.
2. **Priority 2:** Strengthening disaster risk governance to manage disaster risk.
3. **Priority 3:** Investing in disaster risk reduction for resilience.
4. **Priority 4:** Enhancing disaster preparedness for effective response and to "Build Back Better" in recovery, rehabilitation, and reconstruction.
