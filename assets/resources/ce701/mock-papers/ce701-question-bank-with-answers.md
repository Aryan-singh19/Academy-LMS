## Unit 5: Earth Pressure & Slopes

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Active Earth Pressure*.  
  **Ans.** Ka = (1 − sin φ)/(1 + sin φ)
- **Q.** Define *Passive Earth Pressure*.  
  **Ans.** Kp = (1 + sin φ)/(1 − sin φ)
- **Q.** Define *Earth Pressure at Rest*.  
  **Ans.** K0 = 1 − sin φ
- **Q.** Define *Rankine's Theory*.  
  **Ans.** smooth vertical wall
- **Q.** Define *Coulomb's Wedge Theory*.  
  **Ans.** considers wall friction
- **Q.** Define *Culmann's Method*.  
  **Ans.** graphical earth pressure
- **Q.** Define *Gravity Retaining Wall*.  
  **Ans.** resists by weight
- **Q.** Define *Cantilever Retaining Wall*.  
  **Ans.** RCC stem and base
- **Q.** Define *Stability Checks*.  
  **Ans.** sliding, overturning and bearing
- **Q.** Define *Infinite Slope*.  
  **Ans.** factor of safety tan φ/tan β for cohesionless soil
- **Q.** Define *Swedish Slip Circle*.  
  **Ans.** method of slices
- **Q.** Define *Taylor's Stability Number*.  
  **Ans.** for cohesive slopes

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Earth Pressure Theories* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Retaining walls resist lateral pressure. Active Earth Pressure — Ka = (1 − sin φ)/(1 + sin φ); Passive Earth Pressure — Kp = (1 + sin φ)/(1 − sin φ); Earth Pressure at Rest — K0 = 1 − sin φ.
- **Q.** Explain *Rankine & Coulomb Theories* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Two classical approaches. Rankine's Theory — smooth vertical wall; Coulomb's Wedge Theory — considers wall friction; Culmann's Method — graphical earth pressure.
- **Q.** Explain *Retaining Walls* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Walls are checked for stability. Gravity Retaining Wall — resists by weight; Cantilever Retaining Wall — RCC stem and base; Stability Checks — sliding, overturning and bearing.
- **Q.** Explain *Slope Stability* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Slopes may fail by sliding. Infinite Slope — factor of safety tan φ/tan β for cohesionless soil; Swedish Slip Circle — method of slices; Taylor's Stability Number — for cohesive slopes.
