# CE-701 Geotechnical Engineering — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Soil properties, classification, permeability, compaction, consolidation, shear strength, earth pressure and slope stability.

## Unit 1: Soil Properties & Classification

### Soil Formation & Phase Relations

Soil is a three-phase system.

- **Void Ratio:** volume of voids / volume of solids
- **Porosity:** volume of voids / total volume
- **Degree of Saturation:** volume of water / volume of voids

### Index Properties

Index properties classify soils.

- **Specific Gravity:** ratio of soil solids density to water
- **Liquid Limit:** water content at transition to liquid
- **Plasticity Index:** liquid limit minus plastic limit

### Particle Size Analysis

Grading influences behaviour.

- **Sieve Analysis:** for coarse-grained soils
- **Hydrometer Analysis:** for fine-grained soils
- **Coefficient of Uniformity:** Cu = D60/D10

### Soil Classification

Systems group soils.

- **IS Classification:** based on particle size and plasticity
- **Plasticity Chart:** A-line separates clays and silts
- **Well-graded Soil:** wide range of sizes

## Unit 2: Permeability & Seepage

### Darcy's Law

Water flows through soil pores.

- **Darcy's Law:** v = ki
- **Coefficient of Permeability:** flow velocity at unit gradient
- **Constant Head Test:** permeability of coarse soils

### Factors & Tests

Permeability varies widely.

- **Falling Head Test:** for fine soils
- **Effect of Grain Size:** Hazen's k = C D10²
- **Stratified Soil:** equivalent permeability

### Seepage & Flow Nets

Flow nets solve seepage.

- **Flow Net:** flow lines and equipotential lines
- **Seepage Discharge:** q = kH Nf/Nd
- **Laplace Equation:** governs 2D seepage

### Effective Stress & Quicksand

Effective stress controls strength.

- **Effective Stress:** σ' = σ − u
- **Quicksand Condition:** effective stress becomes zero
- **Critical Hydraulic Gradient:** ic = (G − 1)/(1 + e)

## Unit 3: Compaction & Consolidation

### Compaction

Compaction densifies soil.

- **Standard Proctor Test:** finds maximum dry density and OMC
- **Optimum Moisture Content:** water content for maximum dry density
- **Zero Air Voids Line:** theoretical maximum density

### Field Compaction

Equipment compacts soil in layers.

- **Sheep Foot Roller:** for clayey soils
- **Vibratory Roller:** for granular soils
- **Field Density Test:** sand replacement or core cutter

### Consolidation

Saturated clays compress slowly.

- **Consolidation:** volume decrease by expulsion of pore water
- **Terzaghi's Theory:** one-dimensional consolidation theory
- **Coefficient of Consolidation:** cv from time-settlement curve

### Settlement

Consolidation causes settlement.

- **Compression Index:** slope of e-log σ' curve
- **Primary Settlement:** due to pore pressure dissipation
- **Overconsolidated Clay:** past pressure exceeds present

## Unit 4: Shear Strength

### Mohr-Coulomb Criterion

Shear strength depends on cohesion and friction.

- **Mohr-Coulomb Equation:** τ = c + σ tan φ
- **Cohesion:** shear strength at zero normal stress
- **Angle of Internal Friction:** frictional resistance parameter

### Shear Tests

Laboratory tests measure strength.

- **Direct Shear Test:** predetermined failure plane
- **Triaxial Test:** controlled drainage and confining pressure
- **Unconfined Compression Test:** for cohesive soils, qu = 2c

### Drainage Conditions

Drainage affects measured strength.

- **UU Test:** unconsolidated undrained
- **CU Test:** consolidated undrained with pore pressure
- **CD Test:** consolidated drained

### Sensitivity & Liquefaction

Some soils lose strength.

- **Sensitivity:** undisturbed / remoulded strength
- **Thixotropy:** regain of strength with time
- **Liquefaction:** loss of strength in saturated sand during earthquakes

## Unit 5: Earth Pressure & Slopes

### Earth Pressure Theories

Retaining walls resist lateral pressure.

- **Active Earth Pressure:** Ka = (1 − sin φ)/(1 + sin φ)
- **Passive Earth Pressure:** Kp = (1 + sin φ)/(1 − sin φ)
- **Earth Pressure at Rest:** K0 = 1 − sin φ

### Rankine & Coulomb Theories

Two classical approaches.

- **Rankine's Theory:** smooth vertical wall
- **Coulomb's Wedge Theory:** considers wall friction
- **Culmann's Method:** graphical earth pressure

### Retaining Walls

Walls are checked for stability.

- **Gravity Retaining Wall:** resists by weight
- **Cantilever Retaining Wall:** RCC stem and base
- **Stability Checks:** sliding, overturning and bearing

### Slope Stability

Slopes may fail by sliding.

- **Infinite Slope:** factor of safety tan φ/tan β for cohesionless soil
- **Swedish Slip Circle:** method of slices
- **Taylor's Stability Number:** for cohesive slopes
