## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Commutator: converts AC in armature to DC
- **Q1(b):** Armature Reaction: effect of armature flux on main field | Interpoles: improve commutation | Compensating Winding: neutralises armature reaction
- **Q2(a):** Four-point Starter: no-volt coil independent of field
- **Q2(b):** Copper Losses: I²R in windings | Iron Losses: hysteresis and eddy current | Maximum Efficiency: when variable losses equal constant losses
- **Q3(a):** ZPF Method: Potier triangle method, accurate
- **Q3(b):** Direct Axis Reactance: Xd | Quadrature Axis Reactance: Xq | Slip Test: determines Xd and Xq
- **Q4(a):** Power Equation: P = (EV/Xs) sin δ
- **Q4(b):** Synchronisation Conditions: equal voltage, frequency and phase sequence | Synchroscope: indicates phase difference | Load Sharing: by governor and excitation control
- **Q5(a):** Not Self-starting: net starting torque is zero
- **Q5(b):** Split-phase Motor: resistance difference creates phase shift | Capacitor Start Motor: high starting torque | Permanent Split Capacitor: capacitor always in circuit
- **Q6(a):** Series Motor: high starting torque, never start without load
- **Q6(b):** Commutator: converts AC in armature to DC | EMF Equation: E = PΦZN/(60A) | Lap vs Wave Winding: A = P for lap, A = 2 for wave
- **Q7(a):** Salient Pole Rotor: for low-speed hydro alternators
- **Q7(b):** Alternator EMF: E = 4.44 f Φ T Kp Kd | Pitch Factor: Kp = cos(α/2) | Distribution Factor: Kd = sin(mβ/2)/(m sin(β/2))
- **Q8(a):** Capacitor Start Motor: high starting torque
- **Q8(b):** Shaded Pole Motor: shading ring creates rotating field | Universal Motor: series motor running on AC and DC | Reluctance Motor: runs at synchronous speed
