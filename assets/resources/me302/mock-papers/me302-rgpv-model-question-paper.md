## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Closed System: mass fixed, energy may cross the boundary
- **Q1(b):** Quasi-static Process: infinitely slow process passing through equilibrium states | Thermodynamic Cycle: series of processes returning to the initial state | Point Function: property depending only on state, not path
- **Q2(a):** Enthalpy: h = u + pv
- **Q2(b):** Nozzle: increases fluid velocity at the expense of enthalpy | Turbine: produces work as fluid expands | Throttling: isenthalpic process, h1 = h2
- **Q3(a):** Reversible Process: can be reversed leaving no trace on surroundings
- **Q3(b):** Clausius Inequality: cyclic integral of dQ/T ≤ 0 | Entropy Change: dS = dQrev/T | Principle of Increase of Entropy: entropy of an isolated system never decreases
- **Q4(a):** Throttling Calorimeter: throttles steam to superheat to find x
- **Q4(b):** Saturation Temperature: boiling temperature at a given pressure | Critical Point: state where liquid and vapour become indistinguishable, 22.06 MPa for water | Triple Point: state where solid, liquid and vapour coexist
- **Q5(a):** Diesel Cycle: heat added at constant pressure
- **Q5(b):** Brayton Cycle: two isentropic and two isobaric processes | Brayton Efficiency: η = 1 − 1/rp^((γ−1)/γ) | Regeneration: exhaust heats compressed air to improve efficiency
- **Q6(a):** Sign Convention: heat added and work done by system are positive
- **Q6(b):** Closed System: mass fixed, energy may cross the boundary | Open System: both mass and energy cross the boundary (control volume) | Intensive Property: independent of mass, e.g. pressure and temperature
- **Q7(a):** Thermal Efficiency: η = W/Q1
- **Q7(b):** Kelvin-Planck Statement: no engine can convert all heat from a single reservoir into work | Clausius Statement: heat cannot flow from cold to hot without external work | PMM2: perpetual motion machine of second kind, impossible
- **Q8(a):** Brayton Efficiency: η = 1 − 1/rp^((γ−1)/γ)
- **Q8(b):** Mean Effective Pressure: net work divided by swept volume | Swept Volume: displacement volume of the piston | Compression Ratio: total volume / clearance volume
