# ME-302 Thermodynamics — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Basic Concepts & Zeroth Law

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Closed System*.  
  **Ans.** mass fixed, energy may cross the boundary
- **Q.** Define *Open System*.  
  **Ans.** both mass and energy cross the boundary (control volume)
- **Q.** Define *Intensive Property*.  
  **Ans.** independent of mass, e.g. pressure and temperature
- **Q.** Define *Quasi-static Process*.  
  **Ans.** infinitely slow process passing through equilibrium states
- **Q.** Define *Thermodynamic Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** series of processes returning to the initial state
- **Q.** Define *Point Function*.  
  **Ans.** property depending only on state, not path
- **Q.** Define *Zeroth Law*.  
  **Ans.** two bodies in equilibrium with a third are in equilibrium with each other
- **Q.** Define *Thermometric Property*.  
  **Ans.** property that varies with temperature
- **Q.** Define *Absolute Temperature*.  
  **Ans.** Kelvin scale, T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15
- **Q.** Define *Displacement Work*.  
  **Ans.** W = ∫ p dV
- **Q.** Define *Path Function*.  
  **Ans.** quantity depending on the path, e.g. work and heat
- **Q.** Define *Sign Convention*.  
  **Ans.** heat added and work done by system are positive

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *System, Surroundings & Properties* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Thermodynamics studies energy interactions of systems. Closed System — mass fixed, energy may cross the boundary; Open System — both mass and energy cross the boundary (control volume); Intensive Property — independent of mass, e.g. pressure and temperature.
- **Q.** Explain *State, Process & Cycle* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Processes change the state of a system. Quasi-static Process — infinitely slow process passing through equilibrium states; Thermodynamic Cycle — series of processes returning to the initial state; Point Function — property depending only on state, not path.
- **Q.** Explain *Zeroth Law & Temperature* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Thermal equilibrium defines temperature. Zeroth Law — two bodies in equilibrium with a third are in equilibrium with each other; Thermometric Property — property that varies with temperature; Absolute Temperature — Kelvin scale, T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15.
- **Q.** Explain *Work & Heat* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Work and heat are energy in transit. Displacement Work — W = ∫ p dV; Path Function — quantity depending on the path, e.g. work and heat; Sign Convention — heat added and work done by system are positive.

## Unit 2: First Law of Thermodynamics

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *First Law for a Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** net heat equals net work
- **Q.** Define *Internal Energy*.  
  **Ans.** energy stored in molecular motion; a property
- **Q.** Define *First Law for a Process*.  
  **Ans.** Q − W = ΔU
- **Q.** Define *SFEE*.  
  **Ans.** h1 + V1²/2 + gz1 + q = h2 + V2²/2 + gz2 + w
- **Q.** Define *Enthalpy*.  
  **Ans.** h = u + pv
- **Q.** Define *Steady Flow*.  
  **Ans.** properties at each point do not change with time
- **Q.** Define *Nozzle*.  
  **Ans.** increases fluid velocity at the expense of enthalpy
- **Q.** Define *Turbine*.  
  **Ans.** produces work as fluid expands
- **Q.** Define *Throttling*.  
  **Ans.** isenthalpic process, h1 = h2
- **Q.** Define *PMM1*.  
  **Ans.** perpetual motion machine of first kind, impossible
- **Q.** Define *Isothermal Work*.  
  **Ans.** W = p1V1 ln(V2/V1)
- **Q.** Define *Polytropic Process*.  
  **Ans.** pV^n = constant

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *First Law for Closed Systems* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Energy is conserved in every process. First Law for a Cycle — net heat equals net work; Internal Energy — energy stored in molecular motion; a property; First Law for a Process — Q − W = ΔU.
- **Q.** Explain *Steady Flow Energy Equation* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Open systems use the SFEE. SFEE — h1 + V1²/2 + gz1 + q = h2 + V2²/2 + gz2 + w; Enthalpy — h = u + pv; Steady Flow — properties at each point do not change with time.
- **Q.** Explain *Applications of SFEE* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Devices are analysed with SFEE. Nozzle — increases fluid velocity at the expense of enthalpy; Turbine — produces work as fluid expands; Throttling — isenthalpic process, h1 = h2.
- **Q.** Explain *PMM1 & Ideal Gas Processes* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** First law forbids free energy. PMM1 — perpetual motion machine of first kind, impossible; Isothermal Work — W = p1V1 ln(V2/V1); Polytropic Process — pV^n = constant.

## Unit 3: Second Law & Entropy

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Thermal Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** η = W/Q1
- **Q.** Define *COP of Refrigerator*.  
  **Ans.** Q2/W
- **Q.** Define *COP Relation*.  
  **Ans.** COP(HP) = COP(R) + 1
- **Q.** Define *Kelvin-Planck Statement*.  
  **Ans.** no engine can convert all heat from a single reservoir into work
- **Q.** Define *Clausius Statement*.  
  **Ans.** heat cannot flow from cold to hot without external work
- **Q.** Define *PMM2*.  
  **Ans.** perpetual motion machine of second kind, impossible
- **Q.** Define *Carnot Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** η = 1 − T2/T1
- **Q.** Define *Carnot Theorem*.  
  **Ans.** no engine is more efficient than a reversible engine
- **Q.** Define *Reversible Process*.  
  **Ans.** can be reversed leaving no trace on surroundings
- **Q.** Define *Clausius Inequality*.  
  **Ans.** cyclic integral of dQ/T ≤ 0
- **Q.** Define *Entropy Change*.  
  **Ans.** dS = dQrev/T
- **Q.** Define *Principle of Increase of Entropy*.  
  **Ans.** entropy of an isolated system never decreases

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Heat Engines & Heat Pumps* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Second law limits conversion of heat into work. Thermal Efficiency — η = W/Q1; COP of Refrigerator — Q2/W; COP Relation — COP(HP) = COP(R) + 1.
- **Q.** Explain *Kelvin-Planck & Clausius Statements* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Two equivalent statements of the second law. Kelvin-Planck Statement — no engine can convert all heat from a single reservoir into work; Clausius Statement — heat cannot flow from cold to hot without external work; PMM2 — perpetual motion machine of second kind, impossible.
- **Q.** Explain *Carnot Cycle* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Carnot cycle is the most efficient between two temperatures. Carnot Efficiency — η = 1 − T2/T1; Carnot Theorem — no engine is more efficient than a reversible engine; Reversible Process — can be reversed leaving no trace on surroundings.
- **Q.** Explain *Entropy* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Entropy measures irreversibility. Clausius Inequality — cyclic integral of dQ/T ≤ 0; Entropy Change — dS = dQrev/T; Principle of Increase of Entropy — entropy of an isolated system never decreases.

## Unit 4: Properties of Pure Substances

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Saturation Temperature*.  
  **Ans.** boiling temperature at a given pressure
- **Q.** Define *Critical Point*.  
  **Ans.** state where liquid and vapour become indistinguishable, 22.06 MPa for water
- **Q.** Define *Triple Point*.  
  **Ans.** state where solid, liquid and vapour coexist
- **Q.** Define *Dryness Fraction*.  
  **Ans.** x = mass of vapour / total mass
- **Q.** Define *Wet Steam Enthalpy*.  
  **Ans.** h = hf + x·hfg
- **Q.** Define *Superheated Steam*.  
  **Ans.** steam above saturation temperature
- **Q.** Define *Mollier Diagram*.  
  **Ans.** h-s chart for steam
- **Q.** Define *T-s Diagram*.  
  **Ans.** area under curve represents heat transfer
- **Q.** Define *Isentropic Process*.  
  **Ans.** vertical line on T-s and h-s diagrams
- **Q.** Define *Throttling Calorimeter*.  
  **Ans.** throttles steam to superheat to find x
- **Q.** Define *Separating Calorimeter*.  
  **Ans.** separates water droplets mechanically
- **Q.** Define *Combined Calorimeter*.  
  **Ans.** separating plus throttling for wet steam

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Phase Change of Water* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Pure substances change phase at fixed conditions. Saturation Temperature — boiling temperature at a given pressure; Critical Point — state where liquid and vapour become indistinguishable, 22.06 MPa for water; Triple Point — state where solid, liquid and vapour coexist.
- **Q.** Explain *Dryness Fraction & Steam Tables* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Wet steam properties use dryness fraction. Dryness Fraction — x = mass of vapour / total mass; Wet Steam Enthalpy — h = hf + x·hfg; Superheated Steam — steam above saturation temperature.
- **Q.** Explain *T-s & h-s Diagrams* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Property charts aid analysis. Mollier Diagram — h-s chart for steam; T-s Diagram — area under curve represents heat transfer; Isentropic Process — vertical line on T-s and h-s diagrams.
- **Q.** Explain *Measurement of Dryness Fraction* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Calorimeters measure steam quality. Throttling Calorimeter — throttles steam to superheat to find x; Separating Calorimeter — separates water droplets mechanically; Combined Calorimeter — separating plus throttling for wet steam.

## Unit 5: Gas Power Cycles & Availability

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Otto Cycle Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** η = 1 − 1/r^(γ−1)
- **Q.** Define *Diesel Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** heat added at constant pressure
- **Q.** Define *Dual Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** heat added partly at constant volume and partly at constant pressure
- **Q.** Define *Brayton Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** two isentropic and two isobaric processes
- **Q.** Define *Brayton Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** η = 1 − 1/rp^((γ−1)/γ)
- **Q.** Define *Regeneration*.  
  **Ans.** exhaust heats compressed air to improve efficiency
- **Q.** Define *Mean Effective Pressure*.  
  **Ans.** net work divided by swept volume
- **Q.** Define *Swept Volume*.  
  **Ans.** displacement volume of the piston
- **Q.** Define *Compression Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** total volume / clearance volume
- **Q.** Define *Availability*.  
  **Ans.** maximum useful work obtainable relative to the dead state
- **Q.** Define *Irreversibility*.  
  **Ans.** I = T0 × entropy generated
- **Q.** Define *Dead State*.  
  **Ans.** system in equilibrium with environment

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Air Standard Cycles* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Engine cycles are idealised with air. Otto Cycle Efficiency — η = 1 − 1/r^(γ−1); Diesel Cycle — heat added at constant pressure; Dual Cycle — heat added partly at constant volume and partly at constant pressure.
- **Q.** Explain *Brayton Cycle* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Gas turbines follow the Brayton cycle. Brayton Cycle — two isentropic and two isobaric processes; Brayton Efficiency — η = 1 − 1/rp^((γ−1)/γ); Regeneration — exhaust heats compressed air to improve efficiency.
- **Q.** Explain *Mean Effective Pressure* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** MEP compares engine work output. Mean Effective Pressure — net work divided by swept volume; Swept Volume — displacement volume of the piston; Compression Ratio — total volume / clearance volume.
- **Q.** Explain *Availability & Irreversibility* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Exergy is the useful part of energy. Availability — maximum useful work obtainable relative to the dead state; Irreversibility — I = T0 × entropy generated; Dead State — system in equilibrium with environment.
