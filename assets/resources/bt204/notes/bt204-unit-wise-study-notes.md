# BT-204 Basic Civil Engineering & Mechanics — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Building materials, surveying, construction, forces, equilibrium, friction and properties of sections.

## Unit 1: Building Materials

### Stones, Bricks & Lime

Traditional materials for construction.

- **First Class Brick:** well burnt, standard size, water absorption below 20%
- **Stone:** natural material for masonry
- **Lime:** binding material from limestone

### Cement & Concrete

Cement binds aggregates into concrete.

- **OPC:** ordinary Portland cement
- **Water-Cement Ratio:** governs concrete strength
- **Concrete Grade:** M20 means 20 N/mm² characteristic strength

### Timber, Steel & Modern Materials

Other construction materials.

- **Seasoning of Timber:** removing moisture to prevent decay
- **Structural Steel:** rolled sections for frames
- **Fly Ash Brick:** eco-friendly brick from fly ash

### Tests on Materials

Tests ensure quality.

- **Slump Test:** measures workability of concrete
- **Compressive Strength Test:** cube test of concrete
- **Setting Time Test:** Vicat apparatus for cement

## Unit 2: Surveying

### Principles of Surveying

Surveying measures positions on Earth.

- **Plane Surveying:** Earth's curvature ignored
- **Working from Whole to Part:** principle to limit error accumulation
- **Chain Surveying:** measuring distances with chain or tape

### Compass Surveying

Compass measures bearings.

- **Bearing:** direction of a line with respect to meridian
- **Whole Circle Bearing:** measured clockwise from north 0-360°
- **Local Attraction:** magnetic disturbance affecting compass

### Levelling

Levelling finds relative elevations.

- **Benchmark:** point of known elevation
- **Reduced Level:** elevation relative to datum
- **Height of Instrument Method:** RL = HI − staff reading

### Modern Surveying Instruments

Electronic instruments improve accuracy.

- **Total Station:** combines theodolite and EDM
- **GPS Surveying:** satellite-based positioning
- **Theodolite:** measures horizontal and vertical angles

## Unit 3: Building Construction

### Foundations

Foundations transfer loads to soil.

- **Shallow Foundation:** depth less than width, e.g. isolated footing
- **Deep Foundation:** piles transferring load to deeper strata
- **Bearing Capacity:** load per unit area soil can safely support

### Masonry & Walls

Walls are built of bricks or stones.

- **English Bond:** alternate courses of headers and stretchers
- **Load-bearing Wall:** carries loads from above
- **Damp Proof Course:** layer preventing moisture rise

### Floors, Roofs & Building Components

Other building elements.

- **Lintel:** beam over openings
- **RCC Slab:** reinforced concrete floor or roof
- **Staircase:** steps connecting floors

### Building Planning & Bye-laws

Planning ensures function and safety.

- **Orientation:** positioning building for sun and wind
- **FAR:** floor area ratio
- **Setback:** open space between building and boundary

## Unit 4: Engineering Mechanics: Forces

### System of Forces

Forces are vectors acting on bodies.

- **Coplanar Forces:** forces in the same plane
- **Resultant:** single force equivalent to a system
- **Parallelogram Law:** resultant of two forces by diagonal

### Lami's Theorem & Equilibrium

Bodies in equilibrium have zero resultant.

- **Lami's Theorem:** P/sin α = Q/sin β = R/sin γ for three concurrent forces
- **Equilibrium Conditions:** ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0, ΣM = 0
- **Free Body Diagram:** isolated body with all forces

### Moments & Couples

Forces cause rotation.

- **Moment:** force × perpendicular distance
- **Varignon's Theorem:** moment of resultant equals sum of moments
- **Couple:** two equal, opposite, parallel forces

### Support Reactions & Trusses

Beams and trusses are analysed for reactions.

- **Simple Support:** provides vertical reaction
- **Method of Joints:** equilibrium at each truss joint
- **Method of Sections:** cutting truss to find member forces

## Unit 5: Friction & Properties of Sections

### Laws of Friction

Friction opposes relative motion.

- **Coefficient of Friction:** μ = F/N
- **Angle of Friction:** tan φ = μ
- **Limiting Friction:** maximum static friction

### Applications of Friction

Friction problems include ladders and wedges.

- **Ladder Friction:** equilibrium of ladder against wall
- **Wedge:** uses friction to lift loads
- **Angle of Repose:** equals angle of friction

### Centroid & Centre of Gravity

Centroid locates the geometric centre.

- **Centroid:** point where area is concentrated
- **Centroid of Triangle:** h/3 from base
- **Composite Area Centroid:** x̄ = ΣAx/ΣA

### Moment of Inertia

Area moment of inertia measures resistance to bending.

- **Moment of Inertia:** ∫ y² dA
- **Rectangle I:** bd³/12
- **Parallel Axis Theorem:** I = Ig + Ah²
