# ME-403 Theory of Machines — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Mechanisms

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Kinematic Link*.  
  **Ans.** rigid body connecting with others
- **Q.** Define *Lower Pair*.  
  **Ans.** surface contact, e.g. turning pair
- **Q.** Define *Higher Pair*.  
  **Ans.** point or line contact, e.g. cam and follower
- **Q.** Define *Grubler's Equation*.  
  **Ans.** F = 3(n − 1) − 2j − h
- **Q.** Define *Mechanism*.  
  **Ans.** kinematic chain with one link fixed and F = 1
- **Q.** Define *Structure*.  
  **Ans.** F = 0, no relative motion
- **Q.** Define *Grashof's Law*.  
  **Ans.** s + l ≤ p + q for continuous rotation
- **Q.** Define *Inversion*.  
  **Ans.** fixing different links of a chain
- **Q.** Define *Crank-Rocker*.  
  **Ans.** inversion with one link fully rotating
- **Q.** Define *Whitworth Quick Return*.  
  **Ans.** inversion of slider-crank in shapers
- **Q.** Define *Scotch Yoke*.  
  **Ans.** double slider giving simple harmonic motion
- **Q.** Define *Elliptical Trammel*.  
  **Ans.** double slider tracing an ellipse

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Kinematic Links & Pairs* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Mechanisms are made of links joined by pairs. Kinematic Link — rigid body connecting with others; Lower Pair — surface contact, e.g. turning pair; Higher Pair — point or line contact, e.g. cam and follower.
- **Q.** Explain *Degrees of Freedom* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Mobility tells how many inputs are needed. Grubler's Equation — F = 3(n − 1) − 2j − h; Mechanism — kinematic chain with one link fixed and F = 1; Structure — F = 0, no relative motion.
- **Q.** Explain *Four-bar Chain & Inversions* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Inversions give different mechanisms. Grashof's Law — s + l ≤ p + q for continuous rotation; Inversion — fixing different links of a chain; Crank-Rocker — inversion with one link fully rotating.
- **Q.** Explain *Slider-Crank & Double Slider* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Inversions of slider-crank chains. Whitworth Quick Return — inversion of slider-crank in shapers; Scotch Yoke — double slider giving simple harmonic motion; Elliptical Trammel — double slider tracing an ellipse.

## Unit 2: Velocity & Acceleration Analysis

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Relative Velocity*.  
  **Ans.** v = ω × r perpendicular to the link
- **Q.** Define *Velocity Polygon*.  
  **Ans.** vector diagram of velocities
- **Q.** Define *Rubbing Velocity*.  
  **Ans.** relative velocity at a pin joint
- **Q.** Define *Instantaneous Centre*.  
  **Ans.** point about which a link rotates at an instant
- **Q.** Define *Kennedy's Theorem*.  
  **Ans.** three ICs of three bodies lie on a straight line
- **Q.** Define *Number of ICs*.  
  **Ans.** n(n − 1)/2
- **Q.** Define *Centripetal Acceleration*.  
  **Ans.** ω²r towards the centre
- **Q.** Define *Tangential Acceleration*.  
  **Ans.** αr
- **Q.** Define *Coriolis Component*.  
  **Ans.** 2vω for sliding on a rotating link
- **Q.** Define *Klein's Construction*.  
  **Ans.** finds velocity and acceleration of slider-crank
- **Q.** Define *Piston Acceleration*.  
  **Ans.** ω²r(cos θ + cos 2θ / n)
- **Q.** Define *Obliquity Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** n = connecting rod / crank length

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Relative Velocity Method* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Velocity polygons find link velocities. Relative Velocity — v = ω × r perpendicular to the link; Velocity Polygon — vector diagram of velocities; Rubbing Velocity — relative velocity at a pin joint.
- **Q.** Explain *Instantaneous Centre Method* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** ICs give velocities quickly. Instantaneous Centre — point about which a link rotates at an instant; Kennedy's Theorem — three ICs of three bodies lie on a straight line; Number of ICs — n(n − 1)/2.
- **Q.** Explain *Acceleration Analysis* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Acceleration polygons include centripetal terms. Centripetal Acceleration — ω²r towards the centre; Tangential Acceleration — αr; Coriolis Component — 2vω for sliding on a rotating link.
- **Q.** Explain *Klein's Construction* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Graphical method for slider-crank analysis. Klein's Construction — finds velocity and acceleration of slider-crank; Piston Acceleration — ω²r(cos θ + cos 2θ / n); Obliquity Ratio — n = connecting rod / crank length.

## Unit 3: Cams & Gears

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Cam*.  
  **Ans.** rotating element imparting motion to follower
- **Q.** Define *Roller Follower*.  
  **Ans.** reduces wear compared to knife edge
- **Q.** Define *Pressure Angle*.  
  **Ans.** angle between follower motion and normal to the cam profile
- **Q.** Define *Uniform Velocity*.  
  **Ans.** infinite acceleration at ends
- **Q.** Define *SHM Follower Motion*.  
  **Ans.** smooth harmonic displacement
- **Q.** Define *Cycloidal Motion*.  
  **Ans.** zero acceleration at start and end; best for high speed
- **Q.** Define *Law of Gearing*.  
  **Ans.** common normal at contact passes through pitch point
- **Q.** Define *Module*.  
  **Ans.** m = pitch diameter / number of teeth
- **Q.** Define *Involute Profile*.  
  **Ans.** most common tooth profile
- **Q.** Define *Interference*.  
  **Ans.** tip of one tooth digs into flank of another
- **Q.** Define *Minimum Teeth*.  
  **Ans.** about 17 for 20° full-depth involute
- **Q.** Define *Contact Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** arc of contact / circular pitch

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Cams & Followers* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Cams convert rotary into reciprocating motion. Cam — rotating element imparting motion to follower; Roller Follower — reduces wear compared to knife edge; Pressure Angle — angle between follower motion and normal to the cam profile.
- **Q.** Explain *Follower Motions* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Motion laws affect smoothness. Uniform Velocity — infinite acceleration at ends; SHM Follower Motion — smooth harmonic displacement; Cycloidal Motion — zero acceleration at start and end; best for high speed.
- **Q.** Explain *Gear Terminology & Law of Gearing* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Gears transmit motion at constant velocity ratio. Law of Gearing — common normal at contact passes through pitch point; Module — m = pitch diameter / number of teeth; Involute Profile — most common tooth profile.
- **Q.** Explain *Interference & Contact Ratio* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Tooth geometry limits small pinions. Interference — tip of one tooth digs into flank of another; Minimum Teeth — about 17 for 20° full-depth involute; Contact Ratio — arc of contact / circular pitch.
