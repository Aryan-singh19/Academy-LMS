# ME-403 Theory of Machines — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Mechanisms, velocity and acceleration analysis, cams, gears, gear trains, friction, belts and governors.

## Unit 1: Mechanisms

### Kinematic Links & Pairs

Mechanisms are made of links joined by pairs.

- **Kinematic Link:** rigid body connecting with others
- **Lower Pair:** surface contact, e.g. turning pair
- **Higher Pair:** point or line contact, e.g. cam and follower

### Degrees of Freedom

Mobility tells how many inputs are needed.

- **Grubler's Equation:** F = 3(n − 1) − 2j − h
- **Mechanism:** kinematic chain with one link fixed and F = 1
- **Structure:** F = 0, no relative motion

### Four-bar Chain & Inversions

Inversions give different mechanisms.

- **Grashof's Law:** s + l ≤ p + q for continuous rotation
- **Inversion:** fixing different links of a chain
- **Crank-Rocker:** inversion with one link fully rotating

### Slider-Crank & Double Slider

Inversions of slider-crank chains.

- **Whitworth Quick Return:** inversion of slider-crank in shapers
- **Scotch Yoke:** double slider giving simple harmonic motion
- **Elliptical Trammel:** double slider tracing an ellipse

## Unit 2: Velocity & Acceleration Analysis

### Relative Velocity Method

Velocity polygons find link velocities.

- **Relative Velocity:** v = ω × r perpendicular to the link
- **Velocity Polygon:** vector diagram of velocities
- **Rubbing Velocity:** relative velocity at a pin joint

### Instantaneous Centre Method

ICs give velocities quickly.

- **Instantaneous Centre:** point about which a link rotates at an instant
- **Kennedy's Theorem:** three ICs of three bodies lie on a straight line
- **Number of ICs:** n(n − 1)/2

### Acceleration Analysis

Acceleration polygons include centripetal terms.

- **Centripetal Acceleration:** ω²r towards the centre
- **Tangential Acceleration:** αr
- **Coriolis Component:** 2vω for sliding on a rotating link

### Klein's Construction

Graphical method for slider-crank analysis.

- **Klein's Construction:** finds velocity and acceleration of slider-crank
- **Piston Acceleration:** ω²r(cos θ + cos 2θ / n)
- **Obliquity Ratio:** n = connecting rod / crank length

## Unit 3: Cams & Gears

### Cams & Followers

Cams convert rotary into reciprocating motion.

- **Cam:** rotating element imparting motion to follower
- **Roller Follower:** reduces wear compared to knife edge
- **Pressure Angle:** angle between follower motion and normal to the cam profile

### Follower Motions

Motion laws affect smoothness.

- **Uniform Velocity:** infinite acceleration at ends
- **SHM Follower Motion:** smooth harmonic displacement
- **Cycloidal Motion:** zero acceleration at start and end; best for high speed

### Gear Terminology & Law of Gearing

Gears transmit motion at constant velocity ratio.

- **Law of Gearing:** common normal at contact passes through pitch point
- **Module:** m = pitch diameter / number of teeth
- **Involute Profile:** most common tooth profile

### Interference & Contact Ratio

Tooth geometry limits small pinions.

- **Interference:** tip of one tooth digs into flank of another
- **Minimum Teeth:** about 17 for 20° full-depth involute
- **Contact Ratio:** arc of contact / circular pitch

## Unit 4: Gear Trains & Friction Drives

### Simple & Compound Gear Trains

Gear trains change speed and torque.

- **Simple Gear Train:** each shaft has one gear
- **Compound Gear Train:** intermediate shafts carry two gears
- **Train Value:** output speed / input speed

### Epicyclic Gear Trains

Planetary gears give high ratios compactly.

- **Epicyclic Train:** gear axes move around a central axis
- **Sun and Planet Gear:** common epicyclic arrangement
- **Tabular Method:** finds speeds by adding rotations

### Belt & Rope Drives

Belts transmit power by friction.

- **Tension Ratio:** T1/T2 = e^(μθ)
- **Centrifugal Tension:** Tc = mv²
- **Maximum Power Condition:** Tc = Tmax/3

### Friction, Clutches & Brakes

Friction transmits and absorbs power.

- **Friction Circle:** circle of radius μr for journal bearings
- **Uniform Wear Theory:** pr constant; used for clutch design
- **Band Brake:** band around drum providing braking torque

## Unit 5: Governors & Gyroscope

### Watt & Porter Governors

Governors control engine speed.

- **Governor:** regulates mean speed by controlling fuel supply
- **Watt Governor:** simple pendulum governor
- **Porter Governor:** Watt governor with central load

### Hartnell Governor & Characteristics

Spring governors and their qualities.

- **Hartnell Governor:** spring-loaded centrifugal governor
- **Sensitiveness:** range of speed / mean speed
- **Isochronous Governor:** same equilibrium speed at all radii

### Governor Effort & Power

Effort and power quantify governor capability.

- **Governor Effort:** mean force on sleeve for a speed change
- **Governor Power:** effort × sleeve lift
- **Hunting:** continuous fluctuation of speed

### Gyroscopic Effects

Spinning bodies resist changes in axis.

- **Gyroscopic Couple:** C = Iωωp
- **Precession:** rotation of the spin axis
- **Applications:** stability of aeroplanes, ships and two-wheelers
