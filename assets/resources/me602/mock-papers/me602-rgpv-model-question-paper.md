## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Machine Design: creating components to satisfy functional requirements
- **Q1(b):** Maximum Principal Stress Theory: Rankine's theory for brittle materials | Maximum Shear Stress Theory: Tresca theory for ductile materials | Distortion Energy Theory: von Mises theory, most accurate for ductile metals
- **Q2(a):** Size Factor: larger parts have lower endurance
- **Q2(b):** ASME Shaft Equation: combines bending and torsion with shock factors | Equivalent Torque: Te = √((KmM)² + (KtT)²) | Critical Speed: shaft speed causing resonance
- **Q3(a):** Equivalent Dynamic Load: P = XFr + YFa
- **Q3(b):** Hydrodynamic Lubrication: pressure generated by relative motion | Sommerfeld Number: dimensionless bearing characteristic | Petroff's Equation: friction in lightly loaded bearings
- **Q4(a):** Worm Gear: high ratio drive between non-intersecting shafts
- **Q4(b):** Lewis Equation: beam strength of gear tooth | Buckingham's Dynamic Load: additional load due to errors and speed | Wear Strength: surface durability against pitting
- **Q5(a):** Multi-plate Clutch: several plates for compact high torque
- **Q5(b):** Block Brake: shoe pressed against drum | Internal Expanding Shoe Brake: used in vehicles | Disc Brake: calipers clamp a rotating disc
- **Q6(a):** Ergonomics: designing for human comfort and use
- **Q6(b):** Machine Design: creating components to satisfy functional requirements | Factor of Safety: failure stress / allowable stress | Standardisation: using preferred sizes and standard parts
- **Q7(a):** Wahl's Factor: K = (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C
- **Q7(b):** Leaf Spring: layered plates with graduated lengths | Nipping: pre-stress by different curvature of leaves | Full-length Leaves: longest leaves including master leaf
- **Q8(a):** Internal Expanding Shoe Brake: used in vehicles
- **Q8(b):** Cylinder Design: wall thickness from hoop stress | Piston Design: crown thickness by Grashoff's formula | Connecting Rod: I-section designed against buckling
