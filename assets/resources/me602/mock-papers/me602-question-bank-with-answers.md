## Unit 1: Design Fundamentals

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Machine Design*.  
  **Ans.** creating components to satisfy functional requirements
- **Q.** Define *Factor of Safety*.  
  **Ans.** failure stress / allowable stress
- **Q.** Define *Standardisation*.  
  **Ans.** using preferred sizes and standard parts
- **Q.** Define *Maximum Principal Stress Theory*.  
  **Ans.** Rankine's theory for brittle materials
- **Q.** Define *Maximum Shear Stress Theory*.  
  **Ans.** Tresca theory for ductile materials
- **Q.** Define *Distortion Energy Theory*.  
  **Ans.** von Mises theory, most accurate for ductile metals
- **Q.** Define *Stress Concentration Factor*.  
  **Ans.** Kt = maximum stress / nominal stress
- **Q.** Define *Notch Sensitivity*.  
  **Ans.** q = (Kf − 1)/(Kt − 1)
- **Q.** Define *Fillet Radius*.  
  **Ans.** larger radius reduces stress concentration
- **Q.** Define *Material Selection*.  
  **Ans.** based on strength, cost and manufacturability
- **Q.** Define *Preferred Numbers*.  
  **Ans.** Renard series for standard sizes
- **Q.** Define *Ergonomics*.  
  **Ans.** designing for human comfort and use

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Design Process & Considerations* in detail with a neat diagram.

  **Key points:** Failure theories predict yielding under combined stresses. Maximum Principal Stress Theory — Rankine's theory for brittle materials; Maximum Shear Stress Theory — Tresca theory for ductile materials; Distortion Energy Theory — von Mises theory, most accurate for ductile metals.

## Unit 2: Fatigue & Shafts

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Endurance Limit*.  
  **Ans.** stress for infinite life, about 0.5 Sut for steels
- **Q.** Define *Goodman Line*.  
  **Ans.** fatigue criterion linking alternating and mean stresses
- **Q.** Define *Soderberg Line*.  
  **Ans.** conservative criterion using yield strength
- **Q.** Define *Surface Finish Factor*.  
  **Ans.** rougher surfaces reduce fatigue strength
- **Q.** Define *Size Factor*.  
  **Ans.** larger parts have lower endurance
- **Q.** Define *Reliability Factor*.  
  **Ans.** adjusts for desired reliability
- **Q.** Define *ASME Shaft Equation*.  
  **Ans.** combines bending and torsion with shock factors
- **Q.** Define *Equivalent Torque*.  
  **Ans.** Te = √((KmM)² + (KtT)²)
- **Q.** Define *Critical Speed*.  
  **Ans.** shaft speed causing resonance
- **Q.** Define *Sunk Key*.  
  **Ans.** rectangular key fitted in keyways
- **Q.** Define *Flange Coupling*.  
  **Ans.** rigid coupling bolted through flanges
- **Q.** Define *Flexible Coupling*.  
  **Ans.** tolerates misalignment, e.g. bushed pin type

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Fatigue Design* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Cyclic loads cause failure below yield. Endurance Limit — stress for infinite life, about 0.5 Sut for steels; Goodman Line — fatigue criterion linking alternating and mean stresses; Soderberg Line — conservative criterion using yield strength.
- **Q.** Explain *Endurance Limit Modifying Factors* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Real components have lower endurance limits. Surface Finish Factor — rougher surfaces reduce fatigue strength; Size Factor — larger parts have lower endurance; Reliability Factor — adjusts for desired reliability.
- **Q.** Explain *Design of Shafts* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Shafts carry torque and bending. ASME Shaft Equation — combines bending and torsion with shock factors; Equivalent Torque — Te = √((KmM)² + (KtT)²); Critical Speed — shaft speed causing resonance.
- **Q.** Explain *Keys & Couplings* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Keys and couplings connect shafts and hubs. Sunk Key — rectangular key fitted in keyways; Flange Coupling — rigid coupling bolted through flanges; Flexible Coupling — tolerates misalignment, e.g. bushed pin type.

## Unit 3: Springs & Bearings

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Wahl's Factor*.  
  **Ans.** K = (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C
- **Q.** Define *Spring Index*.  
  **Ans.** C = D/d
- **Q.** Define *Spring Deflection*.  
  **Ans.** δ = 8PD³n/(Gd⁴)
- **Q.** Define *Leaf Spring*.  
  **Ans.** layered plates with graduated lengths
- **Q.** Define *Nipping*.  
  **Ans.** pre-stress by different curvature of leaves
- **Q.** Define *Full-length Leaves*.  
  **Ans.** longest leaves including master leaf
- **Q.** Define *Dynamic Load Rating*.  
  **Ans.** load for L10 life of one million revolutions
- **Q.** Define *Bearing Life Equation*.  
  **Ans.** L10 = (C/P)^3 for ball bearings
- **Q.** Define *Equivalent Dynamic Load*.  
  **Ans.** P = XFr + YFa
- **Q.** Define *Hydrodynamic Lubrication*.  
  **Ans.** pressure generated by relative motion
- **Q.** Define *Sommerfeld Number*.  
  **Ans.** dimensionless bearing characteristic
- **Q.** Define *Petroff's Equation*.  
  **Ans.** friction in lightly loaded bearings

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Helical Springs* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Springs store energy and absorb shock. Wahl's Factor — K = (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C; Spring Index — C = D/d; Spring Deflection — δ = 8PD³n/(Gd⁴).
- **Q.** Explain *Leaf Springs* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Laminated springs carry vehicle loads. Leaf Spring — layered plates with graduated lengths; Nipping — pre-stress by different curvature of leaves; Full-length Leaves — longest leaves including master leaf.
- **Q.** Explain *Rolling Contact Bearings* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Ball and roller bearings reduce friction. Dynamic Load Rating — load for L10 life of one million revolutions; Bearing Life Equation — L10 = (C/P)^3 for ball bearings; Equivalent Dynamic Load — P = XFr + YFa.
- **Q.** Explain *Sliding Contact Bearings* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Journal bearings use a lubricant film. Hydrodynamic Lubrication — pressure generated by relative motion; Sommerfeld Number — dimensionless bearing characteristic; Petroff's Equation — friction in lightly loaded bearings.

  **Ans.** used in vehicles
- **Q.** Define *Disc Brake*.
