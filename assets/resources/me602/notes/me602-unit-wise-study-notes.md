# ME-602 Machine Component Design — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Design for static and fatigue loads, shafts, keys, couplings, springs, bearings, gears, clutches and brakes.

## Unit 1: Design Fundamentals

### Design Process & Considerations

Machine design converts needs into components.

- **Machine Design:** creating components to satisfy functional requirements
- **Factor of Safety:** failure stress / allowable stress
- **Standardisation:** using preferred sizes and standard parts

### Theories of Failure

Failure theories predict yielding under combined stresses.

- **Maximum Principal Stress Theory:** Rankine's theory for brittle materials
- **Maximum Shear Stress Theory:** Tresca theory for ductile materials
- **Distortion Energy Theory:** von Mises theory, most accurate for ductile metals

### Stress Concentration

Discontinuities raise local stress.

- **Stress Concentration Factor:** Kt = maximum stress / nominal stress
- **Notch Sensitivity:** q = (Kf − 1)/(Kt − 1)
- **Fillet Radius:** larger radius reduces stress concentration

### Material Selection & Preferred Numbers

Materials and sizes are chosen systematically.

- **Material Selection:** based on strength, cost and manufacturability
- **Preferred Numbers:** Renard series for standard sizes
- **Ergonomics:** designing for human comfort and use

## Unit 2: Fatigue & Shafts

### Fatigue Design

Cyclic loads cause failure below yield.

- **Endurance Limit:** stress for infinite life, about 0.5 Sut for steels
- **Goodman Line:** fatigue criterion linking alternating and mean stresses
- **Soderberg Line:** conservative criterion using yield strength

### Endurance Limit Modifying Factors

Real components have lower endurance limits.

- **Surface Finish Factor:** rougher surfaces reduce fatigue strength
- **Size Factor:** larger parts have lower endurance
- **Reliability Factor:** adjusts for desired reliability

### Design of Shafts

Shafts carry torque and bending.

- **ASME Shaft Equation:** combines bending and torsion with shock factors
- **Equivalent Torque:** Te = √((KmM)² + (KtT)²)
- **Critical Speed:** shaft speed causing resonance

### Keys & Couplings

Keys and couplings connect shafts and hubs.

- **Sunk Key:** rectangular key fitted in keyways
- **Flange Coupling:** rigid coupling bolted through flanges
- **Flexible Coupling:** tolerates misalignment, e.g. bushed pin type

## Unit 3: Springs & Bearings

### Helical Springs

Springs store energy and absorb shock.

- **Wahl's Factor:** K = (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C
- **Spring Index:** C = D/d
- **Spring Deflection:** δ = 8PD³n/(Gd⁴)

### Leaf Springs

Laminated springs carry vehicle loads.

- **Leaf Spring:** layered plates with graduated lengths
- **Nipping:** pre-stress by different curvature of leaves
- **Full-length Leaves:** longest leaves including master leaf

### Rolling Contact Bearings

Ball and roller bearings reduce friction.

- **Dynamic Load Rating:** load for L10 life of one million revolutions
- **Bearing Life Equation:** L10 = (C/P)^3 for ball bearings
- **Equivalent Dynamic Load:** P = XFr + YFa

### Sliding Contact Bearings

Journal bearings use a lubricant film.

- **Hydrodynamic Lubrication:** pressure generated by relative motion
- **Sommerfeld Number:** dimensionless bearing characteristic
- **Petroff's Equation:** friction in lightly loaded bearings

## Unit 4: Gears

### Spur Gear Design

Spur gears are designed for strength and wear.

- **Lewis Equation:** beam strength of gear tooth
- **Buckingham's Dynamic Load:** additional load due to errors and speed
- **Wear Strength:** surface durability against pitting

### Helical Gears

Helical gears run smoother.

- **Helix Angle:** angle of teeth to the axis
- **Virtual Number of Teeth:** z/cos³ψ
- **Axial Thrust:** produced by helical teeth

### Bevel Gears

Bevel gears connect intersecting shafts.

- **Bevel Gear:** conical gear for intersecting shafts
- **Pitch Cone Angle:** cone angle of the pitch surface
- **Miter Gears:** equal bevel gears at 90°

### Worm Gears

Worm drives give high reductions.

- **Worm Gear:** high ratio drive between non-intersecting shafts
- **Self-locking:** worm drive cannot be back-driven
- **Efficiency of Worm Drive:** depends on lead angle and friction

## Unit 5: Clutches, Brakes & Engine Parts

### Friction Clutches

Clutches engage and disengage power.

- **Single Plate Clutch:** one friction plate between pressure plates
- **Multi-plate Clutch:** several plates for compact high torque
- **Uniform Wear Assumption:** gives conservative clutch torque

### Brakes

Brakes absorb kinetic energy.

- **Block Brake:** shoe pressed against drum
- **Internal Expanding Shoe Brake:** used in vehicles
- **Disc Brake:** calipers clamp a rotating disc

### Engine Components Design

IC engine parts are designed for gas and inertia loads.

- **Cylinder Design:** wall thickness from hoop stress
- **Piston Design:** crown thickness by Grashoff's formula
- **Connecting Rod:** I-section designed against buckling

### Crankshaft & Flywheel

Rotating parts handle torque fluctuation.

- **Crankshaft:** converts reciprocating to rotary motion
- **Flywheel:** stores energy to smooth speed fluctuation
- **Coefficient of Fluctuation of Speed:** (ωmax − ωmin)/ω
