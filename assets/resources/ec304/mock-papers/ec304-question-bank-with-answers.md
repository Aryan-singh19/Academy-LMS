## Unit 1: Semiconductor Material Properties

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Energy Band Gap*.  
  **Ans.** energy gap between valence and conduction bands; about 1.1 eV for Si
- **Q.** Define *Intrinsic Semiconductor*.  
  **Ans.** pure semiconductor with equal electrons and holes
- **Q.** Define *Fermi Level*.  
  **Ans.** energy with 50% occupation probability
- **Q.** Define *N-type*.  
  **Ans.** doped with pentavalent donors; electrons majority
- **Q.** Define *P-type*.  
  **Ans.** doped with trivalent acceptors; holes majority
- **Q.** Define *Mass Action Law*.  
  **Ans.** n·p = ni²
- **Q.** Define *Drift Current*.  
  **Ans.** carrier flow due to an electric field
- **Q.** Define *Diffusion Current*.  
  **Ans.** flow from high to low concentration
- **Q.** Define *Mobility*.  
  **Ans.** drift velocity per unit electric field
- **Q.** Define *Hall Effect*.  
  **Ans.** transverse voltage when current flows in a magnetic field
- **Q.** Define *Hall Coefficient*.  
  **Ans.** RH = 1/(nq)
- **Q.** Define *Recombination*.  
  **Ans.** electron filling a hole, ending both carriers

## Unit 2: PN Junction Diode

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Depletion Region*.  
  **Ans.** region empty of mobile carriers near the junction
- **Q.** Define *Barrier Potential*.  
  **Ans.** about 0.7 V for Si and 0.3 V for Ge
- **Q.** Define *Built-in Field*.  
  **Ans.** field opposing further diffusion
- **Q.** Define *Diode Equation*.  
  **Ans.** I = Is(e^(V/ηVT) − 1)
- **Q.** Define *Reverse Saturation Current*.  
  **Ans.** small current under reverse bias
- **Q.** Define *Dynamic Resistance*.  
  **Ans.** rd = ηVT/I
- **Q.** Define *Transition Capacitance*.  
  **Ans.** depletion capacitance under reverse bias
- **Q.** Define *Diffusion Capacitance*.  
  **Ans.** dominant capacitance under forward bias
- **Q.** Define *Avalanche Breakdown*.  
  **Ans.** carrier multiplication at high reverse voltage
- **Q.** Define *Half-wave Rectifier*.  
  **Ans.** ripple factor 1.21, efficiency 40.6%
- **Q.** Define *Bridge Rectifier*.  
  **Ans.** full-wave with four diodes; efficiency 81.2%
- **Q.** Define *Clipper*.  
  **Ans.** circuit removing part of a waveform

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *PN Junction Formation* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** A depletion region forms at the junction. Depletion Region — region empty of mobile carriers near the junction; Barrier Potential — about 0.7 V for Si and 0.3 V for Ge; Built-in Field — field opposing further diffusion.
- **Q.** Explain *Diode Characteristics* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Forward and reverse bias give rectifying behaviour. Diode Equation — I = Is(e^(V/ηVT) − 1); Reverse Saturation Current — small current under reverse bias; Dynamic Resistance — rd = ηVT/I.
- **Q.** Explain *Diode Capacitances & Breakdown* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Junctions store charge and break down at high reverse voltage. Transition Capacitance — depletion capacitance under reverse bias; Diffusion Capacitance — dominant capacitance under forward bias; Avalanche Breakdown — carrier multiplication at high reverse voltage.
- **Q.** Explain *Rectifiers & Clippers* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Diodes convert AC to DC and shape waveforms. Half-wave Rectifier — ripple factor 1.21, efficiency 40.6%; Bridge Rectifier — full-wave with four diodes; efficiency 81.2%; Clipper — circuit removing part of a waveform.

## Unit 3: Bipolar Junction Transistor

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *BJT*.  
  **Ans.** three-terminal current-controlled device
- **Q.** Define *Current Gain β*.  
  **Ans.** IC/IB
- **Q.** Define *Alpha α*.  
  **Ans.** IC/IE = β/(1 + β)
- **Q.** Define *Common Emitter*.  
  **Ans.** high voltage and current gain; phase inversion
- **Q.** Define *Common Base*.  
  **Ans.** low input impedance; current gain near 1
- **Q.** Define *Common Collector*.  
  **Ans.** emitter follower with voltage gain near 1
- **Q.** Define *Q-point*.  
  **Ans.** DC operating point on the load line
- **Q.** Define *Voltage Divider Bias*.  
  **Ans.** most stable common biasing method
- **Q.** Define *Stability Factor*.  
  **Ans.** S = ∂IC/∂ICO
- **Q.** Define *h-parameter Model*.  
  **Ans.** hybrid parameters hie, hfe, hre, hoe
- **Q.** Define *re Model*.  
  **Ans.** small-signal model with re = 26 mV/IE
- **Q.** Define *Early Effect*.  
  **Ans.** base-width modulation with collector voltage

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *BJT Construction & Operation* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** A BJT is two back-to-back junctions controlled by base current. BJT — three-terminal current-controlled device; Current Gain β — IC/IB; Alpha α — IC/IE = β/(1 + β).
- **Q.** Explain *BJT Configurations* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Each configuration offers different gains and impedances. Common Emitter — high voltage and current gain; phase inversion; Common Base — low input impedance; current gain near 1; Common Collector — emitter follower with voltage gain near 1.
- **Q.** Explain *BJT Biasing* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Biasing fixes a stable operating point. Q-point — DC operating point on the load line; Voltage Divider Bias — most stable common biasing method; Stability Factor — S = ∂IC/∂ICO.
- **Q.** Explain *BJT Small-signal Models* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Models analyse amplifier behaviour. h-parameter Model — hybrid parameters hie, hfe, hre, hoe; re Model — small-signal model with re = 26 mV/IE; Early Effect — base-width modulation with collector voltage.

## Unit 4: Field Effect Transistors

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *JFET*.  
  **Ans.** field effect transistor controlled by gate-junction reverse bias
- **Q.** Define *Pinch-off Voltage*.  
  **Ans.** VGS at which the channel closes
- **Q.** Define *Shockley Equation*.  
  **Ans.** ID = IDSS(1 − VGS/VP)²
- **Q.** Define *Enhancement MOSFET*.  
  **Ans.** channel induced when VGS exceeds threshold
- **Q.** Define *Depletion MOSFET*.  
  **Ans.** channel exists at VGS = 0
- **Q.** Define *Threshold Voltage*.  
  **Ans.** minimum VGS to form an inversion channel
- **Q.** Define *Self Bias*.  
  **Ans.** source resistor sets VGS
- **Q.** Define *Transconductance*.  
  **Ans.** gm = ΔID/ΔVGS
- **Q.** Define *Common Source Amplifier*.  
  **Ans.** FET amplifier with gain −gm·RD
- **Q.** Define *CMOS Inverter*.  
  **Ans.** complementary pair with negligible static current
- **Q.** Define *BJT vs FET*.  
  **Ans.** BJT current controlled, FET voltage controlled
- **Q.** Define *Channel Length Modulation*.  
  **Ans.** ID increases slightly with VDS in saturation

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *JFET* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Junction FETs are voltage-controlled. JFET — field effect transistor controlled by gate-junction reverse bias; Pinch-off Voltage — VGS at which the channel closes; Shockley Equation — ID = IDSS(1 − VGS/VP)².
- **Q.** Explain *MOSFET* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** MOSFETs use an insulated gate. Enhancement MOSFET — channel induced when VGS exceeds threshold; Depletion MOSFET — channel exists at VGS = 0; Threshold Voltage — minimum VGS to form an inversion channel.
- **Q.** Explain *FET Biasing & Small-signal Model* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** FET amplifiers are biased and modelled like BJTs. Self Bias — source resistor sets VGS; Transconductance — gm = ΔID/ΔVGS; Common Source Amplifier — FET amplifier with gain −gm·RD.
- **Q.** Explain *CMOS & Comparison of Devices* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** CMOS pairs NMOS and PMOS devices. CMOS Inverter — complementary pair with negligible static current; BJT vs FET — BJT current controlled, FET voltage controlled; Channel Length Modulation — ID increases slightly with VDS in saturation.

## Unit 5: Special Semiconductor Devices

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Zener Diode*.  
  **Ans.** heavily doped diode operating in reverse breakdown
- **Q.** Define *Zener Regulator*.  
  **Ans.** keeps load voltage constant
- **Q.** Define *Zener Breakdown*.  
  **Ans.** tunnelling breakdown below about 5 V
- **Q.** Define *Tunnel Diode*.  
  **Ans.** negative resistance region from quantum tunnelling
- **Q.** Define *Varactor Diode*.  
  **Ans.** capacitance varies with reverse voltage
- **Q.** Define *Schottky Diode*.  
  **Ans.** metal-semiconductor diode with fast switching
- **Q.** Define *LED*.  
  **Ans.** emits light when forward biased
- **Q.** Define *Photodiode*.  
  **Ans.** generates current proportional to light
- **Q.** Define *Solar Cell*.  
  **Ans.** large-area PN junction producing power from light
- **Q.** Define *SCR*.  
  **Ans.** silicon controlled rectifier turned on by a gate pulse
- **Q.** Define *UJT*.  
  **Ans.** unijunction transistor used in relaxation oscillators
- **Q.** Define *DIAC and TRIAC*.  
  **Ans.** bidirectional switching devices for AC control

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Zener Diode & Regulators* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Zener breakdown provides voltage regulation. Zener Diode — heavily doped diode operating in reverse breakdown; Zener Regulator — keeps load voltage constant; Zener Breakdown — tunnelling breakdown below about 5 V.
- **Q.** Explain *Tunnel & Varactor Diodes* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Special diodes exploit unusual characteristics. Tunnel Diode — negative resistance region from quantum tunnelling; Varactor Diode — capacitance varies with reverse voltage; Schottky Diode — metal-semiconductor diode with fast switching.
- **Q.** Explain *Optoelectronic Devices* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Devices convert light and electricity. LED — emits light when forward biased; Photodiode — generates current proportional to light; Solar Cell — large-area PN junction producing power from light.
- **Q.** Explain *Thyristors* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** PNPN devices switch large power. SCR — silicon controlled rectifier turned on by a gate pulse; UJT — unijunction transistor used in relaxation oscillators; DIAC and TRIAC — bidirectional switching devices for AC control.
