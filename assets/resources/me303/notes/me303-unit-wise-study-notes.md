# ME-303 Materials Technology — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Crystal structure, imperfections, phase diagrams, heat treatment, ferrous and non-ferrous alloys and material testing.

## Unit 1: Crystal Structure & Imperfections

### Crystal Structures

Metals arrange atoms in repeating lattices.

- **BCC:** body-centred cubic, APF 0.68, e.g. α-iron
- **FCC:** face-centred cubic, APF 0.74, e.g. aluminium
- **HCP:** hexagonal close-packed, APF 0.74, e.g. zinc

### Miller Indices

Planes and directions are labelled by Miller indices.

- **Miller Indices:** reciprocals of intercepts reduced to integers
- **Slip Plane:** close-packed plane on which dislocations move
- **Coordination Number:** number of nearest neighbours of an atom

### Crystal Imperfections

Real crystals contain defects.

- **Vacancy:** missing atom at a lattice site
- **Dislocation:** line defect enabling plastic deformation
- **Grain Boundary:** interface between differently oriented grains

### Deformation & Strengthening

Plastic deformation occurs by slip and twinning.

- **Slip:** shear displacement along slip planes
- **Work Hardening:** strength rises with plastic deformation
- **Grain Refinement:** smaller grains increase strength (Hall-Petch)

## Unit 2: Phase Diagrams

### Solid Solutions & Phase Rule

Alloys form phases governed by rules.

- **Substitutional Solid Solution:** solute replaces solvent atoms
- **Interstitial Solid Solution:** small solute atoms fit in gaps
- **Gibbs Phase Rule:** F = C − P + 2

### Binary Phase Diagrams

Diagrams show phases versus temperature and composition.

- **Isomorphous System:** complete solubility in liquid and solid, e.g. Cu-Ni
- **Lever Rule:** finds phase fractions in a two-phase region
- **Eutectic Reaction:** liquid → two solid phases at one temperature

### Iron-Carbon Diagram

Fe-Fe3C diagram is the basis of steel heat treatment.

- **Eutectoid Point:** 0.8% C at 727 °C forming pearlite
- **Austenite:** FCC γ-iron solid solution of carbon
- **Cementite:** iron carbide Fe3C, hard and brittle

### Microstructures of Steel & Cast Iron

Carbon content sets microstructure.

- **Pearlite:** lamellar ferrite and cementite
- **Hypoeutectoid Steel:** less than 0.8% C; ferrite plus pearlite
- **Cast Iron:** iron with more than 2.11% carbon

## Unit 3: Heat Treatment

### TTT Diagrams

TTT curves predict transformation products.

- **TTT Diagram:** time-temperature-transformation curve for isothermal cooling
- **Critical Cooling Rate:** minimum rate to form martensite
- **Bainite:** product of transformation between pearlite and martensite ranges

### Annealing & Normalising

Softening and refining treatments.

- **Annealing:** heat and slow furnace cool to soften
- **Normalising:** heat and air cool to refine grain
- **Spheroidising:** forms spherical carbides for machinability

### Hardening & Tempering

Quenching hardens; tempering toughens.

- **Hardening:** austenitise and quench to form martensite
- **Martensite:** hard supersaturated BCT phase
- **Tempering:** reheating martensite to improve toughness

### Surface Hardening

Hard case with tough core.

- **Carburising:** adding carbon to the surface
- **Nitriding:** forming hard nitrides at low temperature
- **Induction Hardening:** local heating by induction and quenching

## Unit 4: Engineering Materials

### Plain Carbon & Alloy Steels

Steels are classified by carbon and alloys.

- **Mild Steel:** low carbon steel, below 0.3% C
- **Stainless Steel:** at least 10.5% chromium for corrosion resistance
- **Tool Steel:** high hardness steel for cutting tools

### Cast Irons

Cast irons are classified by carbon form.

- **Grey Cast Iron:** graphite flakes; good damping
- **Ductile Iron:** spheroidal graphite giving ductility
- **White Cast Iron:** cementite, hard and brittle

### Non-ferrous Alloys

Copper and aluminium alloys are widely used.

- **Brass:** copper-zinc alloy
- **Bronze:** copper-tin alloy
- **Duralumin:** aluminium-copper alloy, age hardenable

### Polymers, Ceramics & Composites

Non-metallic materials offer special properties.

- **Thermoplastic:** softens on heating and can be reshaped
- **Ceramic:** inorganic, hard, brittle and heat resistant
- **Composite:** two or more materials combined, e.g. fibre-reinforced plastic

## Unit 5: Material Testing

### Tensile Test

Tensile test gives strength and ductility.

- **Yield Strength:** stress at onset of plastic deformation
- **Ultimate Tensile Strength:** maximum engineering stress
- **Percentage Elongation:** ductility measure after fracture

### Hardness Testing

Hardness is resistance to indentation.

- **Brinell Hardness:** steel ball indenter, BHN = load / indentation area
- **Rockwell Hardness:** depth-based hardness with scales B and C
- **Vickers Hardness:** diamond pyramid indenter

### Impact & Fatigue Testing

Dynamic loads test toughness and endurance.

- **Charpy Test:** impact energy of notched specimen
- **Fatigue:** failure under cyclic stress below UTS
- **Endurance Limit:** stress below which steel survives infinite cycles

### Non-destructive Testing

NDT inspects without damage.

- **Ultrasonic Testing:** sound waves detect internal flaws
- **Dye Penetrant Test:** detects surface cracks
- **Radiography:** X-rays reveal internal defects
