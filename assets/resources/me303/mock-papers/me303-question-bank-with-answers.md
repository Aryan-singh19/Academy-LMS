# ME-303 Materials Technology — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Crystal Structure & Imperfections

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *BCC*.  
  **Ans.** body-centred cubic, APF 0.68, e.g. α-iron
- **Q.** Define *FCC*.  
  **Ans.** face-centred cubic, APF 0.74, e.g. aluminium
- **Q.** Define *HCP*.  
  **Ans.** hexagonal close-packed, APF 0.74, e.g. zinc
- **Q.** Define *Miller Indices*.  
  **Ans.** reciprocals of intercepts reduced to integers
- **Q.** Define *Slip Plane*.  
  **Ans.** close-packed plane on which dislocations move
- **Q.** Define *Coordination Number*.  
  **Ans.** number of nearest neighbours of an atom
- **Q.** Define *Vacancy*.  
  **Ans.** missing atom at a lattice site
- **Q.** Define *Dislocation*.  
  **Ans.** line defect enabling plastic deformation
- **Q.** Define *Grain Boundary*.  
  **Ans.** interface between differently oriented grains
- **Q.** Define *Slip*.  
  **Ans.** shear displacement along slip planes
- **Q.** Define *Work Hardening*.  
  **Ans.** strength rises with plastic deformation
- **Q.** Define *Grain Refinement*.  
  **Ans.** smaller grains increase strength (Hall-Petch)

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Crystal Structures* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Metals arrange atoms in repeating lattices. BCC — body-centred cubic, APF 0.68, e.g. α-iron; FCC — face-centred cubic, APF 0.74, e.g. aluminium; HCP — hexagonal close-packed, APF 0.74, e.g. zinc.
- **Q.** Explain *Miller Indices* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Planes and directions are labelled by Miller indices. Miller Indices — reciprocals of intercepts reduced to integers; Slip Plane — close-packed plane on which dislocations move; Coordination Number — number of nearest neighbours of an atom.
- **Q.** Explain *Crystal Imperfections* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Real crystals contain defects. Vacancy — missing atom at a lattice site; Dislocation — line defect enabling plastic deformation; Grain Boundary — interface between differently oriented grains.
- **Q.** Explain *Deformation & Strengthening* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Plastic deformation occurs by slip and twinning. Slip — shear displacement along slip planes; Work Hardening — strength rises with plastic deformation; Grain Refinement — smaller grains increase strength (Hall-Petch).

## Unit 2: Phase Diagrams

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Substitutional Solid Solution*.  
  **Ans.** solute replaces solvent atoms
- **Q.** Define *Interstitial Solid Solution*.  
  **Ans.** small solute atoms fit in gaps
- **Q.** Define *Gibbs Phase Rule*.  
  **Ans.** F = C − P + 2
- **Q.** Define *Isomorphous System*.  
  **Ans.** complete solubility in liquid and solid, e.g. Cu-Ni
- **Q.** Define *Lever Rule*.  
  **Ans.** finds phase fractions in a two-phase region
- **Q.** Define *Eutectic Reaction*.  
  **Ans.** liquid → two solid phases at one temperature
- **Q.** Define *Eutectoid Point*.  
  **Ans.** 0.8% C at 727 °C forming pearlite
- **Q.** Define *Austenite*.  
  **Ans.** FCC γ-iron solid solution of carbon
- **Q.** Define *Cementite*.  
  **Ans.** iron carbide Fe3C, hard and brittle
- **Q.** Define *Pearlite*.  
  **Ans.** lamellar ferrite and cementite
- **Q.** Define *Hypoeutectoid Steel*.  
  **Ans.** less than 0.8% C; ferrite plus pearlite
- **Q.** Define *Cast Iron*.  
  **Ans.** iron with more than 2.11% carbon

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Solid Solutions & Phase Rule* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Alloys form phases governed by rules. Substitutional Solid Solution — solute replaces solvent atoms; Interstitial Solid Solution — small solute atoms fit in gaps; Gibbs Phase Rule — F = C − P + 2.
- **Q.** Explain *Binary Phase Diagrams* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Diagrams show phases versus temperature and composition. Isomorphous System — complete solubility in liquid and solid, e.g. Cu-Ni; Lever Rule — finds phase fractions in a two-phase region; Eutectic Reaction — liquid → two solid phases at one temperature.
- **Q.** Explain *Iron-Carbon Diagram* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Fe-Fe3C diagram is the basis of steel heat treatment. Eutectoid Point — 0.8% C at 727 °C forming pearlite; Austenite — FCC γ-iron solid solution of carbon; Cementite — iron carbide Fe3C, hard and brittle.
- **Q.** Explain *Microstructures of Steel & Cast Iron* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Carbon content sets microstructure. Pearlite — lamellar ferrite and cementite; Hypoeutectoid Steel — less than 0.8% C; ferrite plus pearlite; Cast Iron — iron with more than 2.11% carbon.

## Unit 3: Heat Treatment

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *TTT Diagram*.  
  **Ans.** time-temperature-transformation curve for isothermal cooling
- **Q.** Define *Critical Cooling Rate*.  
  **Ans.** minimum rate to form martensite
- **Q.** Define *Bainite*.  
  **Ans.** product of transformation between pearlite and martensite ranges
- **Q.** Define *Annealing*.  
  **Ans.** heat and slow furnace cool to soften
- **Q.** Define *Normalising*.  
  **Ans.** heat and air cool to refine grain
- **Q.** Define *Spheroidising*.  
  **Ans.** forms spherical carbides for machinability
- **Q.** Define *Hardening*.  
  **Ans.** austenitise and quench to form martensite
- **Q.** Define *Martensite*.  
  **Ans.** hard supersaturated BCT phase
- **Q.** Define *Tempering*.  
  **Ans.** reheating martensite to improve toughness
- **Q.** Define *Carburising*.  
  **Ans.** adding carbon to the surface
- **Q.** Define *Nitriding*.  
  **Ans.** forming hard nitrides at low temperature
- **Q.** Define *Induction Hardening*.  
  **Ans.** local heating by induction and quenching

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *TTT Diagrams* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** TTT curves predict transformation products. TTT Diagram — time-temperature-transformation curve for isothermal cooling; Critical Cooling Rate — minimum rate to form martensite; Bainite — product of transformation between pearlite and martensite ranges.
- **Q.** Explain *Annealing & Normalising* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Softening and refining treatments. Annealing — heat and slow furnace cool to soften; Normalising — heat and air cool to refine grain; Spheroidising — forms spherical carbides for machinability.
- **Q.** Explain *Hardening & Tempering* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Quenching hardens; tempering toughens. Hardening — austenitise and quench to form martensite; Martensite — hard supersaturated BCT phase; Tempering — reheating martensite to improve toughness.
- **Q.** Explain *Surface Hardening* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Hard case with tough core. Carburising — adding carbon to the surface; Nitriding — forming hard nitrides at low temperature; Induction Hardening — local heating by induction and quenching.

- **Q.** Define *White Cast Iron*.

- **Q.** Define *Bronze*.

- **Q.** Define *Duralumin*.

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Plain Carbon & Alloy Steels* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Steels are classified by carbon and alloys. Mild Steel — low carbon steel, below 0.3% C; Stainless Steel — at least 10.5% chromium for corrosion resistance; Tool Steel — high hardness steel for cutting tools.
- **Q.** Explain *Cast Irons* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Cast irons are classified by carbon form. Grey Cast Iron — graphite flakes; good damping; Ductile Iron — spheroidal graphite giving ductility; White Cast Iron — cementite, hard and brittle.
- **Q.** Explain *Non-ferrous Alloys* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Copper and aluminium alloys are widely used. Brass — copper-zinc alloy; Bronze — copper-tin alloy; Duralumin — aluminium-copper alloy, age hardenable.
- **Q.** Explain *Polymers, Ceramics & Composites* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Non-metallic materials offer special properties. Thermoplastic — softens on heating and can be reshaped; Ceramic — inorganic, hard, brittle and heat resistant; Composite — two or more materials combined, e.g. fibre-reinforced plastic.

## Unit 5: Material Testing

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Yield Strength*.  
  **Ans.** stress at onset of plastic deformation
- **Q.** Define *Ultimate Tensile Strength*.  
  **Ans.** maximum engineering stress
- **Q.** Define *Percentage Elongation*.  
  **Ans.** ductility measure after fracture
- **Q.** Define *Brinell Hardness*.  
  **Ans.** steel ball indenter, BHN = load / indentation area
- **Q.** Define *Rockwell Hardness*.  
  **Ans.** depth-based hardness with scales B and C
- **Q.** Define *Vickers Hardness*.  
  **Ans.** diamond pyramid indenter
- **Q.** Define *Charpy Test*.  
  **Ans.** impact energy of notched specimen
- **Q.** Define *Fatigue*.  
  **Ans.** failure under cyclic stress below UTS
- **Q.** Define *Endurance Limit*.  
  **Ans.** stress below which steel survives infinite cycles
- **Q.** Define *Ultrasonic Testing*.  
  **Ans.** sound waves detect internal flaws
- **Q.** Define *Dye Penetrant Test*.  
  **Ans.** detects surface cracks
- **Q.** Define *Radiography*.  
  **Ans.** X-rays reveal internal defects

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Tensile Test* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Tensile test gives strength and ductility. Yield Strength — stress at onset of plastic deformation; Ultimate Tensile Strength — maximum engineering stress; Percentage Elongation — ductility measure after fracture.
- **Q.** Explain *Hardness Testing* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Hardness is resistance to indentation. Brinell Hardness — steel ball indenter, BHN = load / indentation area; Rockwell Hardness — depth-based hardness with scales B and C; Vickers Hardness — diamond pyramid indenter.
- **Q.** Explain *Impact & Fatigue Testing* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Dynamic loads test toughness and endurance. Charpy Test — impact energy of notched specimen; Fatigue — failure under cyclic stress below UTS; Endurance Limit — stress below which steel survives infinite cycles.
- **Q.** Explain *Non-destructive Testing* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** NDT inspects without damage. Ultrasonic Testing — sound waves detect internal flaws; Dye Penetrant Test — detects surface cracks; Radiography — X-rays reveal internal defects.
