## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** BCC: body-centred cubic, APF 0.68, e.g. α-iron
- **Q1(b):** Miller Indices: reciprocals of intercepts reduced to integers | Slip Plane: close-packed plane on which dislocations move | Coordination Number: number of nearest neighbours of an atom
- **Q2(a):** Lever Rule: finds phase fractions in a two-phase region
- **Q2(b):** Eutectoid Point: 0.8% C at 727 °C forming pearlite | Austenite: FCC γ-iron solid solution of carbon | Cementite: iron carbide Fe3C, hard and brittle
- **Q3(a):** Tempering: reheating martensite to improve toughness
- **Q3(b):** Carburising: adding carbon to the surface | Nitriding: forming hard nitrides at low temperature | Induction Hardening: local heating by induction and quenching
- **Q4(a):** Thermoplastic: softens on heating and can be reshaped
- **Q4(b):** Mild Steel: low carbon steel, below 0.3% C | Stainless Steel: at least 10.5% chromium for corrosion resistance | Tool Steel: high hardness steel for cutting tools
- **Q5(a):** Ultimate Tensile Strength: maximum engineering stress
- **Q5(b):** Brinell Hardness: steel ball indenter, BHN = load / indentation area | Rockwell Hardness: depth-based hardness with scales B and C | Vickers Hardness: diamond pyramid indenter
- **Q6(a):** Grain Refinement: smaller grains increase strength (Hall-Petch)
- **Q6(b):** BCC: body-centred cubic, APF 0.68, e.g. α-iron | FCC: face-centred cubic, APF 0.74, e.g. aluminium | HCP: hexagonal close-packed, APF 0.74, e.g. zinc
- **Q7(a):** TTT Diagram: time-temperature-transformation curve for isothermal cooling
- **Q7(b):** Annealing: heat and slow furnace cool to soften | Normalising: heat and air cool to refine grain | Spheroidising: forms spherical carbides for machinability
- **Q8(a):** Rockwell Hardness: depth-based hardness with scales B and C
- **Q8(b):** Charpy Test: impact energy of notched specimen | Fatigue: failure under cyclic stress below UTS | Endurance Limit: stress below which steel survives infinite cycles
