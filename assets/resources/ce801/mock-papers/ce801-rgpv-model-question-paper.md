## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Structural Steel: Fe 410 grade commonly used
- **Q1(b):** Partial Safety Factor γm0: 1.10 for yielding | γm1: 1.25 for ultimate stress | Load Combinations: dead, live, wind and earthquake
- **Q2(a):** Buckling Class: a, b, c, d based on section
- **Q2(b):** Lacing: diagonal bars connecting components | Battening: plates connecting components | Built-up Column: two or more sections combined
- **Q3(a):** Bearing Stiffener: prevents web failure
- **Q3(b):** Purlin: beam spanning between trusses | Angle Purlin: simple design for light roofs | Channel Purlin: heavier roofs
- **Q4(a):** Gantry Girder: supports crane rails
- **Q4(b):** Plate Girder: built-up I section | Web Plate: carries shear | Flange Plate: resists bending
- **Q5(a):** Pratt Truss: diagonals in tension
- **Q5(b):** Dead Load: sheeting, purlins and self weight | Live Load: as per IS 875 Part 2 | Wind Load: as per IS 875 Part 3
- **Q6(a):** Effective Throat: 0.7 × size of fillet weld
- **Q6(b):** Structural Steel: Fe 410 grade commonly used | IS 800:2007: Indian code for steel design | Rolled Sections: I, channel, angle sections
- **Q7(a):** Plastic Section: develops full plastic moment
- **Q7(b):** Lateral Torsional Buckling: twisting and lateral bending | Effective Length: depends on restraint | Elastic Critical Moment: Mcr for LTB
- **Q8(a):** Live Load: as per IS 875 Part 2
- **Q8(b):** Rafter: top chord in compression | Tie Member: bottom chord in tension | Gusset Plate: connects truss members
