- **Q.** Define *Forced Vibration*.  
  **Ans.** vibration under external periodic force
- **Q.** Define *Degree of Freedom*.  
  **Ans.** number of independent coordinates to describe motion
- **Q.** Define *SHM*.  
  **Ans.** acceleration proportional to displacement and directed towards mean
- **Q.** Define *Amplitude*.

- **Q.** Define *Spring Stiffness*.

- **Q.** Define *Springs in Series*.

- **Q.** Define *Springs in Parallel*.  
  **Ans.** k = k1 + k2
- **Q.** Define *Fourier Analysis*.  
  **Ans.** expresses periodic motion as harmonics
- **Q.** Define *Beats*.  
  **Ans.** periodic amplitude variation from close frequencies
- **Q.** Define *Phase Angle*.  
  **Ans.** lead or lag between motions

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Vibration Basics* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Vibration is oscillatory motion about equilibrium. Free Vibration — vibration after initial disturbance with no external force; Forced Vibration — vibration under external periodic force; Degree of Freedom — number of independent coordinates to describe motion.
- **Q.** Explain *Simple Harmonic Motion* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** SHM is the simplest periodic motion. SHM — acceleration proportional to displacement and directed towards mean; Amplitude — maximum displacement; Time Period — time for one complete cycle.
- **Q.** Explain *Elements of Vibrating Systems* in detail with a neat diagram.

## Unit 2: Undamped Free Vibration

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Natural Frequency*.  
  **Ans.** ωn = √(k/m)
- **Q.** Define *Static Deflection Relation*.  
  **Ans.** fn = (1/2π)√(g/δ)
- **Q.** Define *Equation of Motion*.  
  **Ans.** mẍ + kx = 0
- **Q.** Define *Energy Method*.  
  **Ans.** total energy constant; d/dt(T + U) = 0
- **Q.** Define *Rayleigh's Method*.  
  **Ans.** max kinetic energy equals max potential energy
- **Q.** Define *Effect of Spring Mass*.  
  **Ans.** add one-third of spring mass
- **Q.** Define *Torsional Natural Frequency*.  
  **Ans.** ωn = √(kt/I)
- **Q.** Define *Two-rotor System*.  
  **Ans.** node exists between rotors
- **Q.** Define *Equivalent Shaft*.  
  **Ans.** stepped shaft reduced to uniform equivalent
- **Q.** Define *Dunkerley's Method*.  
  **Ans.** 1/fn² = Σ 1/fni²
- **Q.** Define *Whirling Speed*.  
  **Ans.** critical speed equal to transverse natural frequency
- **Q.** Define *Critical Speed*.  
  **Ans.** speed at which shaft deflection becomes large

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Natural Frequency* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Natural frequency depends on stiffness and mass. Natural Frequency — ωn = √(k/m); Static Deflection Relation — fn = (1/2π)√(g/δ); Equation of Motion — mẍ + kx = 0.
- **Q.** Explain *Energy & Rayleigh's Methods* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Energy methods give natural frequency quickly. Energy Method — total energy constant; d/dt(T + U) = 0; Rayleigh's Method — max kinetic energy equals max potential energy; Effect of Spring Mass — add one-third of spring mass.
- **Q.** Explain *Torsional Vibration* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Shafts with rotors vibrate torsionally. Torsional Natural Frequency — ωn = √(kt/I); Two-rotor System — node exists between rotors; Equivalent Shaft — stepped shaft reduced to uniform equivalent.
- **Q.** Explain *Transverse Vibration & Whirling* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Beams and shafts vibrate transversely. Dunkerley's Method — 1/fn² = Σ 1/fni²; Whirling Speed — critical speed equal to transverse natural frequency; Critical Speed — speed at which shaft deflection becomes large.

## Unit 3: Damped & Forced Vibration

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Damping Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** ζ = c/cc
- **Q.** Define *Critical Damping*.  
  **Ans.** cc = 2√(km)
- **Q.** Define *Logarithmic Decrement*.  
  **Ans.** δ = ln(x1/x2) = 2πζ/√(1 − ζ²)
- **Q.** Define *Magnification Factor*.  
  **Ans.** ratio of dynamic to static amplitude
- **Q.** Define *Resonance*.  
  **Ans.** forcing frequency equals natural frequency
- **Q.** Define *Frequency Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** r = ω/ωn
- **Q.** Define *Rotating Unbalance*.  
  **Ans.** force meω² rotating with the shaft
- **Q.** Define *Support Motion*.  
  **Ans.** vibration excited by base movement
- **Q.** Define *Half-power Bandwidth*.  
  **Ans.** measures damping near resonance
- **Q.** Define *Transmissibility*.  
  **Ans.** transmitted force / applied force
- **Q.** Define *Isolation Condition*.  
  **Ans.** r > √2 for effective isolation
- **Q.** Define *Isolator Materials*.  
  **Ans.** rubber pads, springs and cork

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Damped Free Vibration* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Damping dissipates energy. Damping Ratio — ζ = c/cc; Critical Damping — cc = 2√(km); Logarithmic Decrement — δ = ln(x1/x2) = 2πζ/√(1 − ζ²).
- **Q.** Explain *Forced Vibration & Resonance* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** External forcing at natural frequency causes resonance. Magnification Factor — ratio of dynamic to static amplitude; Resonance — forcing frequency equals natural frequency; Frequency Ratio — r = ω/ωn.
- **Q.** Explain *Rotating & Reciprocating Unbalance* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Unbalanced masses excite vibration. Rotating Unbalance — force meω² rotating with the shaft; Support Motion — vibration excited by base movement; Half-power Bandwidth — measures damping near resonance.
- **Q.** Explain *Vibration Isolation & Transmissibility* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Isolators reduce transmitted force. Transmissibility — transmitted force / applied force; Isolation Condition — r > √2 for effective isolation; Isolator Materials — rubber pads, springs and cork.

## Unit 4: Multi-degree of Freedom Systems

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Principal Modes*.

- **Q.** Define *Mode Shape*.  
  **Ans.** relative amplitudes in a mode
- **Q.** Define *Coupled Coordinates*.  
  **Ans.** coordinates linked through stiffness or mass
- **Q.** Define *Dynamic Vibration Absorber*.  
  **Ans.** tuned auxiliary mass-spring
- **Q.** Define *Tuning Condition*.  
  **Ans.** absorber natural frequency equals forcing frequency
- **Q.** Define *Centrifugal Pendulum Absorber*.  
  **Ans.** absorber for torsional vibration
- **Q.** Define *Holzer's Method*.  
  **Ans.** trial frequencies until residual torque is zero
- **Q.** Define *Multi-rotor System*.  
  **Ans.** shafts with many discs
- **Q.** Define *Node*.  
  **Ans.** point of zero amplitude
- **Q.** Define *Eigenvalue Problem*.  
  **Ans.** [K − ω²M]{X} = 0
- **Q.** Define *Stiffness Matrix*.  
  **Ans.** matrix of spring coefficients
- **Q.** Define *Orthogonality of Modes*.  
  **Ans.** mode shapes are orthogonal w.r.t. M and K

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Two-degree of Freedom Systems* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Two masses give two natural frequencies. Principal Modes — natural modes of vibration; Mode Shape — relative amplitudes in a mode; Coupled Coordinates — coordinates linked through stiffness or mass.
- **Q.** Explain *Vibration Absorber* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Auxiliary mass cancels vibration. Dynamic Vibration Absorber — tuned auxiliary mass-spring; Tuning Condition — absorber natural frequency equals forcing frequency; Centrifugal Pendulum Absorber — absorber for torsional vibration.
- **Q.** Explain *Holzer's Method* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Iterative method for multi-rotor systems. Holzer's Method — trial frequencies until residual torque is zero; Multi-rotor System — shafts with many discs; Node — point of zero amplitude.
- **Q.** Explain *Matrix Methods* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Matrix methods solve many DOF systems. Eigenvalue Problem — [K − ω²M]{X} = 0; Stiffness Matrix — matrix of spring coefficients; Orthogonality of Modes — mode shapes are orthogonal w.r.t. M and K.
