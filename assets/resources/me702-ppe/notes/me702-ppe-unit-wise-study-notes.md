# ME-702(C) Power Plant Engineering — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Thermal, hydro, nuclear, gas turbine and diesel plants, non-conventional power and power plant economics.

## Unit 1: Introduction & Steam Power Plants

### Energy Scenario & Power Plants

Power plants convert primary energy into electricity.

- **Base Load Plant:** runs continuously at near full load
- **Peak Load Plant:** supplies demand peaks
- **Installed Capacity:** total rated capacity of plants

### Layout of Thermal Power Plant

Coal plants have fuel, air, steam and water circuits.

- **Coal and Ash Circuit:** handling coal to boiler and ash to disposal
- **Feed Water Circuit:** condensate pumped back to boiler
- **Cooling Water Circuit:** removes heat from condenser

### Coal Handling & Combustion

Coal is prepared and burned.

- **Pulverised Coal Firing:** finely ground coal burned in suspension
- **Fluidised Bed Combustion:** coal burned in a bed of fluidised particles
- **Stoker Firing:** coal burned on a moving grate

### Draught & Dust Collection

Air and flue gases are moved and cleaned.

- **Induced Draught:** fan pulls gases through boiler
- **Forced Draught:** fan pushes air into furnace
- **Electrostatic Precipitator:** removes fly ash from flue gas

## Unit 2: Hydroelectric Power Plants

### Hydrology & Site Selection

Water availability decides hydro potential.

- **Hydrograph:** discharge versus time
- **Flow Duration Curve:** discharge versus percentage of time exceeded
- **Mass Curve:** cumulative flow used to size storage

### Hydro Plant Layout

Main components of hydro plants.

- **Dam:** raises water level and stores water
- **Penstock:** pipe carrying water to turbines
- **Surge Tank:** protects penstock from water hammer

### Classification of Hydro Plants

Plants are classified by head and storage.

- **Run-of-river Plant:** little or no storage
- **Pumped Storage Plant:** pumps water uphill at off-peak times
- **High Head Plant:** uses Pelton turbines

### Advantages & Limitations

Hydro is clean but site-dependent.

- **Low Running Cost:** no fuel cost
- **Quick Start:** hydro plants pick up load quickly
- **High Initial Cost:** dams and civil works are expensive

## Unit 3: Nuclear & Gas Turbine Plants

### Nuclear Fission & Reactor Components

Fission releases large energy.

- **Nuclear Fission:** splitting of heavy nucleus releasing energy and neutrons
- **Moderator:** slows neutrons, e.g. heavy water or graphite
- **Control Rods:** absorb neutrons to control reaction, e.g. boron or cadmium

### Types of Reactors

Reactors differ in coolant and moderator.

- **PWR:** pressurised water reactor
- **BWR:** boiling water reactor producing steam directly
- **PHWR:** pressurised heavy water reactor used widely in India

### Gas Turbine Power Plant

Gas turbines generate power quickly.

- **Open Cycle Gas Turbine:** exhaust released to atmosphere
- **Closed Cycle:** working fluid recirculated
- **Regenerator:** recovers exhaust heat

### Combined Cycle Plants

Combining cycles raises efficiency.

- **Combined Cycle:** gas turbine exhaust raises steam for steam turbine
- **HRSG:** heat recovery steam generator
- **Combined Cycle Efficiency:** can exceed 55%

## Unit 4: Diesel & Non-conventional Plants

### Diesel Power Plant

Diesel plants suit small and standby loads.

- **Diesel Power Plant:** diesel engines driving generators
- **Applications:** standby, peak load and remote locations
- **Fuel System:** storage, filters and injection

### Solar Power Plants

Solar energy generates electricity.

- **Solar PV Plant:** photovoltaic modules produce DC
- **Concentrated Solar Power:** mirrors concentrate sunlight to produce steam
- **Solar Tracker:** keeps panels facing the sun

### Wind Power Plants

Wind turbines convert wind energy.

- **Horizontal Axis Wind Turbine:** most common type
- **Power in Wind:** P = ½ρAV³
- **Capacity Factor:** actual energy / rated energy

### Geothermal, Tidal & Biomass

Other renewable sources supply power.

- **Geothermal Plant:** uses Earth's heat
- **Tidal Plant:** uses tidal range
- **Biomass Plant:** burns organic matter or biogas

## Unit 5: Power Plant Economics

### Load Curves

Load curves describe demand.

- **Load Curve:** load versus time
- **Load Duration Curve:** loads arranged in descending order
- **Peak Load:** maximum demand

### Load Factors

Factors measure utilisation.

- **Load Factor:** average load / peak load
- **Demand Factor:** maximum demand / connected load
- **Diversity Factor:** sum of individual maximum demands / maximum demand of system

### Cost of Power Generation

Costs include fixed and operating components.

- **Fixed Cost:** interest, depreciation and insurance
- **Operating Cost:** fuel, labour and maintenance
- **Depreciation:** loss of value over time

### Tariffs & Environmental Aspects

Tariffs recover cost; plants affect environment.

- **Two-part Tariff:** demand charge plus energy charge
- **Pollution Control:** reducing SOx, NOx and particulates
- **Fly Ash Utilisation:** using ash in cement and bricks
