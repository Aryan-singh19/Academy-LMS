## Unit 2: Hydroelectric Power Plants

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Hydrograph*.  
  **Ans.** discharge versus time
- **Q.** Define *Flow Duration Curve*.  
  **Ans.** discharge versus percentage of time exceeded
- **Q.** Define *Mass Curve*.  
  **Ans.** cumulative flow used to size storage
- **Q.** Define *Dam*.  
  **Ans.** raises water level and stores water
- **Q.** Define *Penstock*.  
  **Ans.** pipe carrying water to turbines
- **Q.** Define *Surge Tank*.  
  **Ans.** protects penstock from water hammer
- **Q.** Define *Run-of-river Plant*.  
  **Ans.** little or no storage
- **Q.** Define *Pumped Storage Plant*.  
  **Ans.** pumps water uphill at off-peak times
- **Q.** Define *High Head Plant*.  
  **Ans.** uses Pelton turbines
- **Q.** Define *Low Running Cost*.  
  **Ans.** no fuel cost
- **Q.** Define *Quick Start*.  
  **Ans.** hydro plants pick up load quickly
- **Q.** Define *High Initial Cost*.  
  **Ans.** dams and civil works are expensive

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Hydrology & Site Selection* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Water availability decides hydro potential. Hydrograph — discharge versus time; Flow Duration Curve — discharge versus percentage of time exceeded; Mass Curve — cumulative flow used to size storage.
- **Q.** Explain *Hydro Plant Layout* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Main components of hydro plants. Dam — raises water level and stores water; Penstock — pipe carrying water to turbines; Surge Tank — protects penstock from water hammer.
- **Q.** Explain *Classification of Hydro Plants* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Plants are classified by head and storage. Run-of-river Plant — little or no storage; Pumped Storage Plant — pumps water uphill at off-peak times; High Head Plant — uses Pelton turbines.
- **Q.** Explain *Advantages & Limitations* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Hydro is clean but site-dependent. Low Running Cost — no fuel cost; Quick Start — hydro plants pick up load quickly; High Initial Cost — dams and civil works are expensive.

## Unit 3: Nuclear & Gas Turbine Plants

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Nuclear Fission*.  
  **Ans.** splitting of heavy nucleus releasing energy and neutrons
- **Q.** Define *Moderator*.  
  **Ans.** slows neutrons, e.g. heavy water or graphite
- **Q.** Define *Control Rods*.  
  **Ans.** absorb neutrons to control reaction, e.g. boron or cadmium
- **Q.** Define *PWR*.  
  **Ans.** pressurised water reactor
- **Q.** Define *BWR*.  
  **Ans.** boiling water reactor producing steam directly
- **Q.** Define *PHWR*.  
  **Ans.** pressurised heavy water reactor used widely in India
- **Q.** Define *Open Cycle Gas Turbine*.  
  **Ans.** exhaust released to atmosphere
- **Q.** Define *Closed Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** working fluid recirculated
- **Q.** Define *Regenerator*.  
  **Ans.** recovers exhaust heat
- **Q.** Define *Combined Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** gas turbine exhaust raises steam for steam turbine
- **Q.** Define *HRSG*.  
  **Ans.** heat recovery steam generator
- **Q.** Define *Combined Cycle Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** can exceed 55%

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Nuclear Fission & Reactor Components* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Fission releases large energy. Nuclear Fission — splitting of heavy nucleus releasing energy and neutrons; Moderator — slows neutrons, e.g. heavy water or graphite; Control Rods — absorb neutrons to control reaction, e.g. boron or cadmium.
- **Q.** Explain *Types of Reactors* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Reactors differ in coolant and moderator. PWR — pressurised water reactor; BWR — boiling water reactor producing steam directly; PHWR — pressurised heavy water reactor used widely in India.
- **Q.** Explain *Gas Turbine Power Plant* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Gas turbines generate power quickly. Open Cycle Gas Turbine — exhaust released to atmosphere; Closed Cycle — working fluid recirculated; Regenerator — recovers exhaust heat.
- **Q.** Explain *Combined Cycle Plants* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Combining cycles raises efficiency. Combined Cycle — gas turbine exhaust raises steam for steam turbine; HRSG — heat recovery steam generator; Combined Cycle Efficiency — can exceed 55%.

## Unit 5: Power Plant Economics

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Load Curve*.  
  **Ans.** load versus time
- **Q.** Define *Load Duration Curve*.  
  **Ans.** loads arranged in descending order
- **Q.** Define *Peak Load*.  
  **Ans.** maximum demand
- **Q.** Define *Load Factor*.  
  **Ans.** average load / peak load
- **Q.** Define *Demand Factor*.  
  **Ans.** maximum demand / connected load
- **Q.** Define *Diversity Factor*.  
  **Ans.** sum of individual maximum demands / maximum demand of system
- **Q.** Define *Fixed Cost*.  
  **Ans.** interest, depreciation and insurance
- **Q.** Define *Operating Cost*.  
  **Ans.** fuel, labour and maintenance
- **Q.** Define *Depreciation*.  
  **Ans.** loss of value over time
- **Q.** Define *Two-part Tariff*.  
  **Ans.** demand charge plus energy charge
- **Q.** Define *Pollution Control*.  
  **Ans.** reducing SOx, NOx and particulates
- **Q.** Define *Fly Ash Utilisation*.  
  **Ans.** using ash in cement and bricks

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Load Curves* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Load curves describe demand. Load Curve — load versus time; Load Duration Curve — loads arranged in descending order; Peak Load — maximum demand.
- **Q.** Explain *Load Factors* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Factors measure utilisation. Load Factor — average load / peak load; Demand Factor — maximum demand / connected load; Diversity Factor — sum of individual maximum demands / maximum demand of system.
- **Q.** Explain *Cost of Power Generation* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Costs include fixed and operating components. Fixed Cost — interest, depreciation and insurance; Operating Cost — fuel, labour and maintenance; Depreciation — loss of value over time.
