# CS-302 Discrete Structure — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Sets, relations, logic, algebraic structures, graphs, posets and combinatorics.

## Unit 1: Set Theory, Relations & Functions

### Sets & Set Operations

Sets are the basic language for collections in computing and proofs.

- **Power Set:** set of all subsets of A; it has 2^n elements
- **De Morgan's Law:** complement of (A ∪ B) equals A' ∩ B'
- **Cartesian Product:** A × B, the set of all ordered pairs (a, b)

### Relations & Their Properties

A relation on A is a subset of A × A classified by its properties.

- **Reflexive Relation:** every element is related to itself, (a, a) ∈ R
- **Symmetric Relation:** (a, b) ∈ R implies (b, a) ∈ R
- **Transitive Relation:** (a, b) and (b, c) in R imply (a, c) in R

### Equivalence Relations & Partitions

Equivalence relations split a set into disjoint classes.

- **Equivalence Relation:** relation that is reflexive, symmetric and transitive
- **Equivalence Class:** set of all elements related to a given element
- **Partition:** collection of disjoint non-empty subsets whose union is the whole set

### Functions & Pigeonhole Principle

Functions map each input to exactly one output.

- **Bijective Function:** both one-one and onto; has an inverse
- **Composition of Functions:** (g ∘ f)(x) = g(f(x))
- **Pigeonhole Principle:** n + 1 objects in n boxes force some box to hold two

## Unit 2: Algebraic Structures

### Groups & Semigroups

Algebraic structures are sets with operations obeying axioms.

- **Semigroup:** set with an associative binary operation
- **Monoid:** semigroup with an identity element
- **Group:** monoid in which every element has an inverse

### Abelian, Cyclic Groups & Subgroups

Special groups have extra structure useful in coding and cryptography.

- **Abelian Group:** group whose operation is commutative
- **Cyclic Group:** group generated by a single element
- **Subgroup:** subset that is itself a group under the same operation

### Cosets & Lagrange's Theorem

Cosets partition a group and fix the possible orders of subgroups.

- **Left Coset:** aH = {ah : h ∈ H} for subgroup H
- **Lagrange's Theorem:** the order of a subgroup divides the order of a finite group
- **Normal Subgroup:** subgroup whose left and right cosets coincide

### Rings, Fields & Homomorphisms

Rings and fields carry two operations, the basis of modular arithmetic.

- **Ring:** abelian group under + with an associative × distributing over +
- **Field:** commutative ring where every non-zero element has a multiplicative inverse
- **Homomorphism:** structure-preserving map f(a * b) = f(a) * f(b)

## Unit 3: Propositional Logic & Proofs

### Propositions & Connectives

Logic formalises statements and how they combine.

- **Proposition:** declarative statement that is either true or false
- **Conditional p → q:** false only when p is true and q is false
- **Biconditional p ↔ q:** true when p and q have the same truth value

### Tautology, Contradiction & Normal Forms

Formulas are classified by truth tables and rewritten into standard forms.

- **Tautology:** formula true for every truth assignment
- **Contradiction:** formula false for every truth assignment
- **CNF:** conjunction of clauses, each a disjunction of literals

### Predicates & Quantifiers

Predicate logic adds variables and quantifiers.

- **Universal Quantifier:** ∀x P(x), P holds for every x in the domain
- **Existential Quantifier:** ∃x P(x), P holds for at least one x
- **Negation Rule:** ¬∀x P(x) is equivalent to ∃x ¬P(x)

### Rules of Inference & Mathematical Induction

Valid arguments derive conclusions; induction proves statements for all n.

- **Modus Ponens:** from p and p → q conclude q
- **Modus Tollens:** from ¬q and p → q conclude ¬p
- **Mathematical Induction:** prove base case and P(k) ⇒ P(k+1) to prove P(n) for all n

## Unit 4: Graph Theory & Trees

### Graph Terminology & Representation

Graphs model networks of vertices and edges.

- **Handshaking Lemma:** sum of vertex degrees equals twice the number of edges
- **Adjacency Matrix:** n × n matrix with 1 where an edge joins two vertices
- **Isomorphic Graphs:** graphs with an edge-preserving bijection between vertex sets

### Euler & Hamiltonian Paths

Special walks cover every edge or every vertex exactly once.

- **Euler Circuit:** closed walk using every edge once; exists iff all degrees are even
- **Hamiltonian Cycle:** cycle visiting every vertex exactly once
- **Konigsberg Problem:** classic bridge puzzle proved impossible by Euler

### Planar Graphs & Graph Colouring

Planarity and colouring model layout and scheduling problems.

- **Euler's Formula:** for a connected planar graph v − e + f = 2
- **Chromatic Number:** minimum colours to colour vertices with no adjacent pair alike
- **Four Colour Theorem:** every planar map can be coloured with four colours

### Trees & Spanning Trees

Trees are connected acyclic graphs used in search and networks.

- **Tree Property:** a tree with n vertices has exactly n − 1 edges
- **Spanning Tree:** subgraph that is a tree containing all vertices
- **Kruskal's Algorithm:** builds a minimum spanning tree by adding cheapest safe edges

## Unit 5: Posets, Lattices & Combinatorics

### Partial Order & Hasse Diagrams

Posets capture ordering where not every pair is comparable.

- **Partial Order:** reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive relation
- **Hasse Diagram:** drawing of a poset omitting loops and transitive edges
- **Total Order:** partial order where every pair of elements is comparable

### Lattices & Boolean Algebra

Lattices have a least upper bound and greatest lower bound for every pair.

- **Lattice:** poset where every pair has a join and a meet
- **Distributive Lattice:** lattice where meet distributes over join
- **Boolean Algebra:** complemented distributive lattice

### Permutations & Combinations

Counting principles count arrangements and selections.

- **Permutation:** ordered arrangement, nPr = n!/(n − r)!
- **Combination:** unordered selection, nCr = n!/(r!(n − r)!)
- **Inclusion-Exclusion:** size of A ∪ B = size of A + size of B − size of A ∩ B

### Recurrence Relations & Generating Functions

Recurrences define sequences from earlier terms.

- **Recurrence Relation:** equation expressing a(n) in terms of earlier terms
- **Characteristic Equation:** polynomial whose roots give the homogeneous solution
- **Generating Function:** power series Σ a(n) x^n encoding a sequence
