# AU-603 Vehicle Dynamics — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Tyre Mechanics

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Rolling Resistance Coefficient*.  
  **Ans.** rolling resistance / normal load
- **Q.** Define *Slip Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** (wheel speed − vehicle speed) / vehicle speed
- **Q.** Define *Contact Patch*.  
  **Ans.** tyre area touching the road
- **Q.** Define *Slip Angle*.  
  **Ans.** angle between wheel heading and travel direction
- **Q.** Define *Cornering Stiffness*.  
  **Ans.** lateral force per degree of slip angle
- **Q.** Define *Self-aligning Torque*.  
  **Ans.** moment returning wheel to straight
- **Q.** Define *Magic Formula*.  
  **Ans.** Pacejka empirical tyre model
- **Q.** Define *Friction Circle*.  
  **Ans.** combined longitudinal and lateral limit
- **Q.** Define *Camber Thrust*.  
  **Ans.** lateral force from camber
- **Q.** Define *Radial Tyre Behaviour*.  
  **Ans.** lower rolling resistance
- **Q.** Define *Inflation Pressure Effect*.  
  **Ans.** changes stiffness and contact patch
- **Q.** Define *Aquaplaning*.  
  **Ans.** tyre rides on water film

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Tyre Forces* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Tyres generate all road forces. Rolling Resistance Coefficient — rolling resistance / normal load; Slip Ratio — (wheel speed − vehicle speed) / vehicle speed; Contact Patch — tyre area touching the road.
- **Q.** Explain *Cornering Force & Slip Angle* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Lateral force comes from slip angle. Slip Angle — angle between wheel heading and travel direction; Cornering Stiffness — lateral force per degree of slip angle; Self-aligning Torque — moment returning wheel to straight.
- **Q.** Explain *Tyre Models* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Models predict tyre behaviour. Magic Formula — Pacejka empirical tyre model; Friction Circle — combined longitudinal and lateral limit; Camber Thrust — lateral force from camber.
- **Q.** Explain *Tyre Characteristics* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Construction affects behaviour. Radial Tyre Behaviour — lower rolling resistance; Inflation Pressure Effect — changes stiffness and contact patch; Aquaplaning — tyre rides on water film.

## Unit 2: Longitudinal Dynamics

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Tractive Force*.  
  **Ans.** engine torque × gear ratio × efficiency / wheel radius
- **Q.** Define *Traction Limit*.  
  **Ans.** μ × normal load on driven wheels
- **Q.** Define *Power-limited Acceleration*.  
  **Ans.** governed by engine power
- **Q.** Define *Load Transfer in Braking*.  
  **Ans.** W·h·a/(g·L)
- **Q.** Define *Brake Force Distribution*.  
  **Ans.** ideal front-rear proportion
- **Q.** Define *Wheel Lock*.  
  **Ans.** loss of steering when front wheels lock
- **Q.** Define *Drag Coefficient*.  
  **Ans.** Cd, dimensionless drag measure
- **Q.** Define *Frontal Area*.  
  **Ans.** projected front area
- **Q.** Define *Downforce*.  
  **Ans.** negative lift increasing grip
- **Q.** Define *Road Load*.  
  **Ans.** sum of rolling, aero and grade resistance
- **Q.** Define *Acceleration Time*.  
  **Ans.** 0-100 km/h time
- **Q.** Define *Top Speed*.  
  **Ans.** when tractive force equals resistance

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Tractive Performance* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Acceleration depends on tractive force. Tractive Force — engine torque × gear ratio × efficiency / wheel radius; Traction Limit — μ × normal load on driven wheels; Power-limited Acceleration — governed by engine power.
- **Q.** Explain *Braking Dynamics* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Braking involves load transfer. Load Transfer in Braking — W·h·a/(g·L); Brake Force Distribution — ideal front-rear proportion; Wheel Lock — loss of steering when front wheels lock.
- **Q.** Explain *Aerodynamic Resistance* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Drag dominates at high speed. Drag Coefficient — Cd, dimensionless drag measure; Frontal Area — projected front area; Downforce — negative lift increasing grip.
- **Q.** Explain *Fuel Economy & Performance* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Resistances determine efficiency. Road Load — sum of rolling, aero and grade resistance; Acceleration Time — 0-100 km/h time; Top Speed — when tractive force equals resistance.

## Unit 3: Lateral Dynamics & Handling

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Lateral Acceleration*.  
  **Ans.** V²/R
- **Q.** Define *Ackermann Angle*.  
  **Ans.** L/R steering angle at low speed
- **Q.** Define *Bicycle Model*.  
  **Ans.** two-wheel model for lateral dynamics
- **Q.** Define *Understeer*.  
  **Ans.** vehicle turns less than steering demands
- **Q.** Define *Oversteer*.  
  **Ans.** vehicle turns more than demanded
- **Q.** Define *Understeer Gradient*.  
  **Ans.** K, positive for understeer
- **Q.** Define *Critical Speed*.  
  **Ans.** oversteer vehicle becomes unstable
- **Q.** Define *Characteristic Speed*.  
  **Ans.** understeer vehicle needs double Ackermann steer
- **Q.** Define *Neutral Steer*.  
  **Ans.** K = 0
- **Q.** Define *Lateral Load Transfer*.  
  **Ans.** W·h·ay/(g·t)
- **Q.** Define *Static Stability Factor*.  
  **Ans.** t/(2h)
- **Q.** Define *Roll Centre*.  
  **Ans.** point about which body rolls

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Steady-state Cornering* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Vehicles corner with lateral acceleration. Lateral Acceleration — V²/R; Ackermann Angle — L/R steering angle at low speed; Bicycle Model — two-wheel model for lateral dynamics.
- **Q.** Explain *Understeer & Oversteer* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Handling balance depends on tyres and weight. Understeer — vehicle turns less than steering demands; Oversteer — vehicle turns more than demanded; Understeer Gradient — K, positive for understeer.
- **Q.** Explain *Critical & Characteristic Speed* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Speeds define stability limits. Critical Speed — oversteer vehicle becomes unstable; Characteristic Speed — understeer vehicle needs double Ackermann steer; Neutral Steer — K = 0.
- **Q.** Explain *Rollover & Load Transfer* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Lateral load transfer can cause rollover. Lateral Load Transfer — W·h·ay/(g·t); Static Stability Factor — t/(2h); Roll Centre — point about which body rolls.
