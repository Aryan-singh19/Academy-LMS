## Unit 4: Pavement Design

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Flexible Pavement*.  
  **Ans.** layered system transmitting load by grain contact
- **Q.** Define *Rigid Pavement*.  
  **Ans.** concrete slab acting by flexure
- **Q.** Define *Composite Pavement*.  
  **Ans.** combination of both
- **Q.** Define *Design Traffic*.  
  **Ans.** cumulative standard axles in msa
- **Q.** Define *Standard Axle*.  
  **Ans.** 80 kN single axle
- **Q.** Define *Layer Thickness*.  
  **Ans.** determined from design charts
- **Q.** Define *Westergaard Stress*.  
  **Ans.** wheel load stresses at interior, edge and corner
- **Q.** Define *Radius of Relative Stiffness*.  
  **Ans.** l = (Eh³/(12(1 − μ²)k))^0.25
- **Q.** Define *Dowel Bar*.  
  **Ans.** transfers load across joints
- **Q.** Define *Rutting*.  
  **Ans.** longitudinal depressions in wheel paths
- **Q.** Define *Fatigue Cracking*.  
  **Ans.** alligator cracks from repeated loading
- **Q.** Define *Pumping*.  
  **Ans.** ejection of fines at rigid pavement joints

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Types of Pavements* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Pavements are flexible or rigid. Flexible Pavement — layered system transmitting load by grain contact; Rigid Pavement — concrete slab acting by flexure; Composite Pavement — combination of both.
- **Q.** Explain *Flexible Pavement Design* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** IRC:37 uses traffic and CBR. Design Traffic — cumulative standard axles in msa; Standard Axle — 80 kN single axle; Layer Thickness — determined from design charts.
- **Q.** Explain *Rigid Pavement Design* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** IRC:58 uses Westergaard theory. Westergaard Stress — wheel load stresses at interior, edge and corner; Radius of Relative Stiffness — l = (Eh³/(12(1 − μ²)k))^0.25; Dowel Bar — transfers load across joints.
- **Q.** Explain *Pavement Failures* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Distresses indicate failure modes. Rutting — longitudinal depressions in wheel paths; Fatigue Cracking — alligator cracks from repeated loading; Pumping — ejection of fines at rigid pavement joints.
