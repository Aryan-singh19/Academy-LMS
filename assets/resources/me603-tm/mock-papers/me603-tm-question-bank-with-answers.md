## Unit 1: Energy Transfer in Turbomachines

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Turbomachine*.  
  **Ans.** device transferring energy between rotor and fluid
- **Q.** Define *Power-absorbing Machine*.  
  **Ans.** pump or compressor
- **Q.** Define *Power-producing Machine*.  
  **Ans.** turbine
- **Q.** Define *Euler's Equation*.  
  **Ans.** E = (U1Vw1 − U2Vw2)/g
- **Q.** Define *Velocity Triangle*.  
  **Ans.** vectors of blade, absolute and relative velocity
- **Q.** Define *Whirl Velocity*.  
  **Ans.** tangential component of absolute velocity
- **Q.** Define *Degree of Reaction*.  
  **Ans.** static enthalpy change in rotor / total change
- **Q.** Define *Impulse Stage*.  
  **Ans.** zero degree of reaction
- **Q.** Define *50% Reaction*.  
  **Ans.** symmetric velocity triangles
- **Q.** Define *Specific Speed*.  
  **Ans.** Ns = N√Q/H^(3/4) for pumps
- **Q.** Define *Unit Speed*.  
  **Ans.** speed under unit head
- **Q.** Define *Affinity Laws*.  
  **Ans.** Q ∝ N, H ∝ N², P ∝ N³

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Classification of Turbomachines* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Turbomachines exchange energy with a flowing fluid. Turbomachine — device transferring energy between rotor and fluid; Power-absorbing Machine — pump or compressor; Power-producing Machine — turbine.
- **Q.** Explain *Euler's Turbine Equation* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Euler's equation gives energy transfer. Euler's Equation — E = (U1Vw1 − U2Vw2)/g; Velocity Triangle — vectors of blade, absolute and relative velocity; Whirl Velocity — tangential component of absolute velocity.
- **Q.** Explain *Degree of Reaction* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Reaction tells where pressure change occurs. Degree of Reaction — static enthalpy change in rotor / total change; Impulse Stage — zero degree of reaction; 50% Reaction — symmetric velocity triangles.
- **Q.** Explain *Dimensionless Parameters* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Similarity laws compare machines. Specific Speed — Ns = N√Q/H^(3/4) for pumps; Unit Speed — speed under unit head; Affinity Laws — Q ∝ N, H ∝ N², P ∝ N³.

## Unit 2: Centrifugal & Reciprocating Pumps

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Centrifugal Pump*.  
  **Ans.** impeller and casing raise liquid pressure
- **Q.** Define *Priming*.  
  **Ans.** filling the pump with liquid before starting
- **Q.** Define *Volute Casing*.  
  **Ans.** gradually increasing area converts velocity to pressure
- **Q.** Define *Manometric Head*.  
  **Ans.** head measured across pump
- **Q.** Define *Manometric Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** manometric head / Euler head
- **Q.** Define *Overall Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** water power / shaft power
- **Q.** Define *Cavitation*.  
  **Ans.** formation and collapse of vapour bubbles
- **Q.** Define *NPSH*.  
  **Ans.** net positive suction head
- **Q.** Define *Thoma Cavitation Factor*.  
  **Ans.** σ = NPSH / H
- **Q.** Define *Slip*.  
  **Ans.** theoretical minus actual discharge
- **Q.** Define *Air Vessel*.  
  **Ans.** smooths flow and reduces acceleration head
- **Q.** Define *Indicator Diagram*.  
  **Ans.** pressure versus stroke diagram

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Centrifugal Pump Working* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Impellers add energy to liquid. Centrifugal Pump — impeller and casing raise liquid pressure; Priming — filling the pump with liquid before starting; Volute Casing — gradually increasing area converts velocity to pressure.
- **Q.** Explain *Pump Heads & Efficiencies* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Heads and efficiencies define performance. Manometric Head — head measured across pump; Manometric Efficiency — manometric head / Euler head; Overall Efficiency — water power / shaft power.
- **Q.** Explain *Cavitation & NPSH* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Low pressure causes vapour bubbles. Cavitation — formation and collapse of vapour bubbles; NPSH — net positive suction head; Thoma Cavitation Factor — σ = NPSH / H.
- **Q.** Explain *Reciprocating Pumps* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Positive displacement pumps deliver fixed volume per stroke. Slip — theoretical minus actual discharge; Air Vessel — smooths flow and reduces acceleration head; Indicator Diagram — pressure versus stroke diagram.

## Unit 4: Compressors, Fans & Blowers

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Centrifugal Compressor*.  
  **Ans.** radial flow compressor with diffuser
- **Q.** Define *Slip Factor*.  
  **Ans.** accounts for flow deviation at impeller exit
- **Q.** Define *Pre-whirl*.  
  **Ans.** guide vanes reduce inlet relative Mach number
- **Q.** Define *Axial Compressor*.  
  **Ans.** multiple stages of rotor and stator rows
- **Q.** Define *Stage Pressure Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** small per stage, so many stages used
- **Q.** Define *Work Done Factor*.  
  **Ans.** accounts for non-uniform axial velocity
- **Q.** Define *Fan*.  
  **Ans.** low pressure rise air mover
- **Q.** Define *Blower*.  
  **Ans.** moderate pressure rise
- **Q.** Define *Backward-curved Blades*.  
  **Ans.** stable characteristic and high efficiency
- **Q.** Define *Surge*.  
  **Ans.** flow reversal oscillation in compressors
- **Q.** Define *Rotating Stall*.  
  **Ans.** stalled cells moving around the annulus
- **Q.** Define *Surge Line*.  
  **Ans.** boundary on compressor map

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Centrifugal Compressors* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Rotating impellers compress gases. Centrifugal Compressor — radial flow compressor with diffuser; Slip Factor — accounts for flow deviation at impeller exit; Pre-whirl — guide vanes reduce inlet relative Mach number.
- **Q.** Explain *Axial Flow Compressors* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Axial machines handle large flows. Axial Compressor — multiple stages of rotor and stator rows; Stage Pressure Ratio — small per stage, so many stages used; Work Done Factor — accounts for non-uniform axial velocity.
- **Q.** Explain *Fans & Blowers* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Low-pressure machines move air. Fan — low pressure rise air mover; Blower — moderate pressure rise; Backward-curved Blades — stable characteristic and high efficiency.
- **Q.** Explain *Surge & Stall* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Instabilities limit operating range. Surge — flow reversal oscillation in compressors; Rotating Stall — stalled cells moving around the annulus; Surge Line — boundary on compressor map.
