# ME-603(A) Turbo Machinery — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Energy transfer in turbomachines, centrifugal pumps, hydraulic turbines, compressors, fans and hydrostatic systems.

## Unit 1: Energy Transfer in Turbomachines

### Classification of Turbomachines

Turbomachines exchange energy with a flowing fluid.

- **Turbomachine:** device transferring energy between rotor and fluid
- **Power-absorbing Machine:** pump or compressor
- **Power-producing Machine:** turbine

### Euler's Turbine Equation

Euler's equation gives energy transfer.

- **Euler's Equation:** E = (U1Vw1 − U2Vw2)/g
- **Velocity Triangle:** vectors of blade, absolute and relative velocity
- **Whirl Velocity:** tangential component of absolute velocity

### Degree of Reaction

Reaction tells where pressure change occurs.

- **Degree of Reaction:** static enthalpy change in rotor / total change
- **Impulse Stage:** zero degree of reaction
- **50% Reaction:** symmetric velocity triangles

### Dimensionless Parameters

Similarity laws compare machines.

- **Specific Speed:** Ns = N√Q/H^(3/4) for pumps
- **Unit Speed:** speed under unit head
- **Affinity Laws:** Q ∝ N, H ∝ N², P ∝ N³

## Unit 2: Centrifugal & Reciprocating Pumps

### Centrifugal Pump Working

Impellers add energy to liquid.

- **Centrifugal Pump:** impeller and casing raise liquid pressure
- **Priming:** filling the pump with liquid before starting
- **Volute Casing:** gradually increasing area converts velocity to pressure

### Pump Heads & Efficiencies

Heads and efficiencies define performance.

- **Manometric Head:** head measured across pump
- **Manometric Efficiency:** manometric head / Euler head
- **Overall Efficiency:** water power / shaft power

### Cavitation & NPSH

Low pressure causes vapour bubbles.

- **Cavitation:** formation and collapse of vapour bubbles
- **NPSH:** net positive suction head
- **Thoma Cavitation Factor:** σ = NPSH / H

### Reciprocating Pumps

Positive displacement pumps deliver fixed volume per stroke.

- **Slip:** theoretical minus actual discharge
- **Air Vessel:** smooths flow and reduces acceleration head
- **Indicator Diagram:** pressure versus stroke diagram

## Unit 3: Hydraulic Turbines

### Pelton Wheel

Impulse turbine for high heads.

- **Pelton Wheel:** impulse turbine with buckets struck by jets
- **Jet Ratio:** wheel diameter / jet diameter
- **Maximum Efficiency Condition:** bucket speed about half jet speed

### Francis Turbine

Mixed flow reaction turbine for medium heads.

- **Francis Turbine:** inward mixed flow reaction turbine
- **Guide Vanes:** control flow and direction into runner
- **Draft Tube:** recovers kinetic energy at exit

### Kaplan Turbine

Axial flow turbine for low heads.

- **Kaplan Turbine:** axial flow turbine with adjustable blades
- **Propeller Turbine:** Kaplan with fixed blades
- **High Specific Speed:** Kaplan suits large flows at low heads

### Turbine Performance & Governing

Characteristics and governing maintain speed.

- **Unit Quantities:** performance at unit head
- **Main Characteristic Curves:** performance at constant head
- **Governing:** adjusting flow to keep speed constant

## Unit 4: Compressors, Fans & Blowers

### Centrifugal Compressors

Rotating impellers compress gases.

- **Centrifugal Compressor:** radial flow compressor with diffuser
- **Slip Factor:** accounts for flow deviation at impeller exit
- **Pre-whirl:** guide vanes reduce inlet relative Mach number

### Axial Flow Compressors

Axial machines handle large flows.

- **Axial Compressor:** multiple stages of rotor and stator rows
- **Stage Pressure Ratio:** small per stage, so many stages used
- **Work Done Factor:** accounts for non-uniform axial velocity

### Fans & Blowers

Low-pressure machines move air.

- **Fan:** low pressure rise air mover
- **Blower:** moderate pressure rise
- **Backward-curved Blades:** stable characteristic and high efficiency

### Surge & Stall

Instabilities limit operating range.

- **Surge:** flow reversal oscillation in compressors
- **Rotating Stall:** stalled cells moving around the annulus
- **Surge Line:** boundary on compressor map

## Unit 5: Hydraulic Systems

### Hydrostatic Systems

Fluid power transmits force by pressure.

- **Positive Displacement Pump:** fixed volume per revolution
- **Variable Displacement Pump:** output adjustable at constant speed
- **Hydraulic Motor:** converts fluid power into rotation

### Hydraulic Devices

Classic devices use pressure.

- **Hydraulic Intensifier:** increases fluid pressure
- **Hydraulic Accumulator:** stores energy in pressurised fluid
- **Hydraulic Press:** multiplies force by area ratio

### Hydraulic Cranes & Lifts

Lifting devices use hydraulics.

- **Hydraulic Crane:** lifts loads using hydraulic cylinders
- **Hydraulic Lift:** elevator powered by hydraulics
- **Hydraulic Ram:** uses water hammer to lift water

### Fluid Couplings & Torque Converters

Hydrodynamic drives transmit torque smoothly.

- **Fluid Coupling:** pump and turbine transmitting torque via fluid
- **Torque Converter:** coupling with stator multiplying torque
- **Slip in Coupling:** speed difference between pump and turbine
