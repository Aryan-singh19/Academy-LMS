## Unit 1: Theory of Metal Cutting

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Orthogonal Cutting*.  
  **Ans.** cutting edge perpendicular to cutting velocity
- **Q.** Define *Oblique Cutting*.  
  **Ans.** cutting edge inclined to velocity
- **Q.** Define *Chip Thickness Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** r = t / tc
- **Q.** Define *Continuous Chip*.  
  **Ans.** ductile material at high speed
- **Q.** Define *Discontinuous Chip*.  
  **Ans.** brittle materials like cast iron
- **Q.** Define *Built-up Edge*.  
  **Ans.** welded material on tool tip at low speed
- **Q.** Define *Merchant's Circle*.  
  **Ans.** force diagram relating cutting forces
- **Q.** Define *Shear Angle Relation*.  
  **Ans.** 2φ + β − α = 90°
- **Q.** Define *Cutting Force*.  
  **Ans.** force component in direction of cutting velocity
- **Q.** Define *Taylor's Tool Life Equation*.  
  **Ans.** VT^n = C
- **Q.** Define *Flank Wear*.  
  **Ans.** wear on clearance face
- **Q.** Define *Crater Wear*.  
  **Ans.** wear on rake face

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Orthogonal & Oblique Cutting* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Cutting geometry affects forces and chips. Orthogonal Cutting — cutting edge perpendicular to cutting velocity; Oblique Cutting — cutting edge inclined to velocity; Chip Thickness Ratio — r = t / tc.
- **Q.** Explain *Chip Formation* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Chip type depends on material and conditions. Continuous Chip — ductile material at high speed; Discontinuous Chip — brittle materials like cast iron; Built-up Edge — welded material on tool tip at low speed.
- **Q.** Explain *Merchant's Circle* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Force analysis of orthogonal cutting. Merchant's Circle — force diagram relating cutting forces; Shear Angle Relation — 2φ + β − α = 90°; Cutting Force — force component in direction of cutting velocity.
- **Q.** Explain *Tool Life & Wear* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Tools wear and must be replaced. Taylor's Tool Life Equation — VT^n = C; Flank Wear — wear on clearance face; Crater Wear — wear on rake face.

## Unit 2: Machine Tools

- **Q.** Define *Taper Turning*.  
  **Ans.** by compound rest, tailstock offset or attachment
- **Q.** Define *Thread Cutting*.  
  **Ans.** lead screw synchronises carriage with spindle
- **Q.** Define *Drilling*.  
  **Ans.** making holes with a twist drill
- **Q.** Define *Reaming*.  
  **Ans.** finishing a drilled hole to accurate size
- **Q.** Define *Boring*.  
  **Ans.** enlarging an existing hole with a single-point tool
- **Q.** Define *Shaper*.  
  **Ans.** tool reciprocates over stationary work
- **Q.** Define *Planer*.  
  **Ans.** work reciprocates under stationary tool
- **Q.** Define *Quick Return Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** cutting stroke time / return stroke time
- **Q.** Define *Up Milling*.  
  **Ans.** cutter rotates against feed direction
- **Q.** Define *Down Milling*.  
  **Ans.** cutter rotates with feed direction
- **Q.** Define *Indexing*.  
  **Ans.** dividing head for equally spaced features

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Lathe* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Lathe produces cylindrical surfaces. Lathe — machine rotating work against a single-point tool; Taper Turning — by compound rest, tailstock offset or attachment; Thread Cutting — lead screw synchronises carriage with spindle.
- **Q.** Explain *Drilling & Boring* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Holes are made and enlarged. Drilling — making holes with a twist drill; Reaming — finishing a drilled hole to accurate size; Boring — enlarging an existing hole with a single-point tool.
- **Q.** Explain *Shaper, Planer & Slotter* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Reciprocating tools make flat surfaces. Shaper — tool reciprocates over stationary work; Planer — work reciprocates under stationary tool; Quick Return Ratio — cutting stroke time / return stroke time.
- **Q.** Explain *Milling* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Multi-tooth cutters machine many shapes. Up Milling — cutter rotates against feed direction; Down Milling — cutter rotates with feed direction; Indexing — dividing head for equally spaced features.

## Unit 4: Non-conventional Machining

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Unconventional Machining*.  
  **Ans.** removes material without traditional chips
- **Q.** Define *Hard Materials*.  
  **Ans.** carbides and ceramics difficult to cut
- **Q.** Define *Energy Classification*.  
  **Ans.** mechanical, thermal, chemical and electrochemical
- **Q.** Define *EDM*.  
  **Ans.** electric discharge machining using sparks in dielectric
- **Q.** Define *Dielectric Fluid*.  
  **Ans.** kerosene or deionised water
- **Q.** Define *Wire EDM*.  
  **Ans.** travelling wire cuts complex profiles
- **Q.** Define *ECM*.  
  **Ans.** electrochemical machining, reverse of electroplating
- **Q.** Define *Electrolyte*.  
  **Ans.** NaCl solution commonly used
- **Q.** Define *Chemical Milling*.  
  **Ans.** selective etching with masks
- **Q.** Define *USM*.  
  **Ans.** ultrasonic machining with abrasive slurry
- **Q.** Define *LBM*.  
  **Ans.** laser beam machining by melting and vaporising
- **Q.** Define *AJM*.  
  **Ans.** abrasive jet machining with high-velocity particles

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Need for Unconventional Processes* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Hard materials and complex shapes need new methods. Unconventional Machining — removes material without traditional chips; Hard Materials — carbides and ceramics difficult to cut; Energy Classification — mechanical, thermal, chemical and electrochemical.
- **Q.** Explain *EDM & Wire EDM* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Sparks erode conductive materials. EDM — electric discharge machining using sparks in dielectric; Dielectric Fluid — kerosene or deionised water; Wire EDM — travelling wire cuts complex profiles.
- **Q.** Explain *ECM & Chemical Machining* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Electrochemical dissolution removes metal. ECM — electrochemical machining, reverse of electroplating; Electrolyte — NaCl solution commonly used; Chemical Milling — selective etching with masks.
- **Q.** Explain *USM, LBM & AJM* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Other unconventional processes. USM — ultrasonic machining with abrasive slurry; LBM — laser beam machining by melting and vaporising; AJM — abrasive jet machining with high-velocity particles.
