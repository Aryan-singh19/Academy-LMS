# ME-501 Internal Combustion Engines — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: IC Engine Fundamentals

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *SI Engine*.  
  **Ans.** spark ignition engine working on Otto cycle
- **Q.** Define *CI Engine*.  
  **Ans.** compression ignition engine working on Diesel cycle
- **Q.** Define *Two-stroke Engine*.  
  **Ans.** one power stroke per revolution
- **Q.** Define *Bore*.  
  **Ans.** cylinder diameter
- **Q.** Define *Stroke*.  
  **Ans.** distance piston travels between dead centres
- **Q.** Define *Compression Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** (Vs + Vc)/Vc
- **Q.** Define *Valve Overlap*.  
  **Ans.** both valves open near TDC
- **Q.** Define *Inlet Valve Opening*.  
  **Ans.** before TDC for better filling
- **Q.** Define *Port Timing*.  
  **Ans.** timing of ports in two-stroke engines
- **Q.** Define *Fuel-air Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** accounts for variable specific heat and dissociation
- **Q.** Define *Time Loss*.  
  **Ans.** due to finite combustion time
- **Q.** Define *Exhaust Blowdown Loss*.  
  **Ans.** loss when exhaust valve opens early

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Classification of IC Engines* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Engines are classified by cycle, fuel and ignition. SI Engine — spark ignition engine working on Otto cycle; CI Engine — compression ignition engine working on Diesel cycle; Two-stroke Engine — one power stroke per revolution.
- **Q.** Explain *Engine Components & Terminology* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Basic parts and dimensions. Bore — cylinder diameter; Stroke — distance piston travels between dead centres; Compression Ratio — (Vs + Vc)/Vc.
- **Q.** Explain *Valve Timing Diagram* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Valves open and close around dead centres. Valve Overlap — both valves open near TDC; Inlet Valve Opening — before TDC for better filling; Port Timing — timing of ports in two-stroke engines.
- **Q.** Explain *Fuel-air & Actual Cycles* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Real cycles deviate from air-standard cycles. Fuel-air Cycle — accounts for variable specific heat and dissociation; Time Loss — due to finite combustion time; Exhaust Blowdown Loss — loss when exhaust valve opens early.

## Unit 2: Fuel Supply Systems

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Simple Carburettor*.  
  **Ans.** venturi draws fuel from float chamber
- **Q.** Define *Air-fuel Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** stoichiometric about 14.7:1 for petrol
- **Q.** Define *Choke*.  
  **Ans.** enriches mixture for cold starting
- **Q.** Define *MPFI*.  
  **Ans.** multi-point fuel injection near each intake valve
- **Q.** Define *GDI*.  
  **Ans.** gasoline direct injection into cylinder
- **Q.** Define *ECU*.  
  **Ans.** electronic control unit managing injection
- **Q.** Define *Fuel Injection Pump*.  
  **Ans.** delivers metered high-pressure fuel
- **Q.** Define *Common Rail*.  
  **Ans.** high-pressure accumulator feeding injectors
- **Q.** Define *Injector Nozzle*.  
  **Ans.** atomises fuel into spray
- **Q.** Define *Battery Ignition*.  
  **Ans.** coil-based system with contact breaker
- **Q.** Define *Electronic Ignition*.  
  **Ans.** transistor-switched ignition
- **Q.** Define *Spark Advance*.  
  **Ans.** igniting before TDC at higher speeds

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Carburetion* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Carburettors mix air and fuel in SI engines. Simple Carburettor — venturi draws fuel from float chamber; Air-fuel Ratio — stoichiometric about 14.7:1 for petrol; Choke — enriches mixture for cold starting.
- **Q.** Explain *Petrol Injection* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Injection replaces carburettors. MPFI — multi-point fuel injection near each intake valve; GDI — gasoline direct injection into cylinder; ECU — electronic control unit managing injection.
- **Q.** Explain *Diesel Injection* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** CI engines inject fuel at high pressure. Fuel Injection Pump — delivers metered high-pressure fuel; Common Rail — high-pressure accumulator feeding injectors; Injector Nozzle — atomises fuel into spray.
- **Q.** Explain *Ignition Systems* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** SI engines need a spark at the right time. Battery Ignition — coil-based system with contact breaker; Electronic Ignition — transistor-switched ignition; Spark Advance — igniting before TDC at higher speeds.

## Unit 3: Combustion

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Ignition Lag*.  
  **Ans.** delay between spark and flame development
- **Q.** Define *Flame Propagation*.  
  **Ans.** flame front travelling across chamber
- **Q.** Define *Knock*.  
  **Ans.** autoignition of end gas causing pressure waves
- **Q.** Define *Ignition Delay*.  
  **Ans.** time between injection and start of combustion
- **Q.** Define *Diesel Knock*.  
  **Ans.** sudden combustion of accumulated fuel
- **Q.** Define *Cetane Number*.  
  **Ans.** ignition quality of diesel fuel
- **Q.** Define *Octane Number*.  
  **Ans.** knock resistance of petrol versus iso-octane
- **Q.** Define *Anti-knock Agent*.  
  **Ans.** additive increasing octane number
- **Q.** Define *Higher Octane*.  
  **Ans.** allows higher compression ratio
- **Q.** Define *Squish*.  
  **Ans.** radial inward air motion at TDC
- **Q.** Define *Swirl*.  
  **Ans.** rotational air motion improving mixing
- **Q.** Define *Direct Injection Chamber*.  
  **Ans.** open chamber for CI engines

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Combustion in SI Engines* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Flame propagates from the spark plug. Ignition Lag — delay between spark and flame development; Flame Propagation — flame front travelling across chamber; Knock — autoignition of end gas causing pressure waves.
- **Q.** Explain *Combustion in CI Engines* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Fuel self-ignites after injection. Ignition Delay — time between injection and start of combustion; Diesel Knock — sudden combustion of accumulated fuel; Cetane Number — ignition quality of diesel fuel.
- **Q.** Explain *Fuel Ratings* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Ratings quantify knock resistance. Octane Number — knock resistance of petrol versus iso-octane; Anti-knock Agent — additive increasing octane number; Higher Octane — allows higher compression ratio.
- **Q.** Explain *Combustion Chamber Design* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Chamber shape affects combustion. Squish — radial inward air motion at TDC; Swirl — rotational air motion improving mixing; Direct Injection Chamber — open chamber for CI engines.

- **Q.** Define *Volumetric Efficiency*.

  **Ans.** exhaust-driven turbine powering a compressor
- **Q.** Define *Intercooler*.  
  **Ans.** cools compressed air to increase density

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Performance Parameters* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Engine output and efficiency are measured. Indicated Power — power developed in cylinder; Brake Power — power at the crankshaft; Mechanical Efficiency — BP / IP.
- **Q.** Explain *Fuel Consumption & Efficiency* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Fuel use is compared across engines. BSFC — brake specific fuel consumption, kg/kWh; Brake Thermal Efficiency — BP / (fuel energy input); Volumetric Efficiency — actual air inducted / swept volume.
- **Q.** Explain *Engine Testing* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Tests determine friction and heat balance. Morse Test — finds IP of multi-cylinder engine by cutting cylinders; Willan's Line — extrapolates fuel line to find friction power; Heat Balance Sheet — distribution of fuel energy into BP, cooling, exhaust.

  **Key points:** Forced induction raises power. Supercharging — compressing intake air with engine-driven compressor; Turbocharger — exhaust-driven turbine powering a compressor; Intercooler — cools compressed air to increase density.

## Unit 5: Lubrication, Cooling & Emissions

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Splash Lubrication*.  
  **Ans.** crank dips into oil
- **Q.** Define *Pressure Lubrication*.  
  **Ans.** oil pump forces oil to bearings
- **Q.** Define *Viscosity Index*.  
  **Ans.** change of viscosity with temperature
- **Q.** Define *Air Cooling*.  
  **Ans.** fins dissipate heat
- **Q.** Define *Water Cooling*.  
  **Ans.** coolant circulated through jackets and radiator
- **Q.** Define *Thermostat*.  
  **Ans.** controls coolant flow to maintain temperature
- **Q.** Define *CO*.  
  **Ans.** carbon monoxide from rich mixtures
- **Q.** Define *NOx*.  
  **Ans.** oxides of nitrogen at high temperatures
- **Q.** Define *Particulate Matter*.  
  **Ans.** soot mainly from diesel engines
- **Q.** Define *Catalytic Converter*.  
  **Ans.** three-way catalyst converts CO, HC and NOx
- **Q.** Define *EGR*.  
  **Ans.** exhaust gas recirculation reduces NOx
- **Q.** Define *Bharat Stage VI*.  
  **Ans.** Indian emission norms equivalent to Euro VI

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Lubrication Systems* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Lubrication reduces friction and wear. Splash Lubrication — crank dips into oil; Pressure Lubrication — oil pump forces oil to bearings; Viscosity Index — change of viscosity with temperature.
- **Q.** Explain *Cooling Systems* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Excess heat must be removed. Air Cooling — fins dissipate heat; Water Cooling — coolant circulated through jackets and radiator; Thermostat — controls coolant flow to maintain temperature.
- **Q.** Explain *Engine Emissions* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Combustion produces pollutants. CO — carbon monoxide from rich mixtures; NOx — oxides of nitrogen at high temperatures; Particulate Matter — soot mainly from diesel engines.
- **Q.** Explain *Emission Control* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Devices and norms reduce emissions. Catalytic Converter — three-way catalyst converts CO, HC and NOx; EGR — exhaust gas recirculation reduces NOx; Bharat Stage VI — Indian emission norms equivalent to Euro VI.
