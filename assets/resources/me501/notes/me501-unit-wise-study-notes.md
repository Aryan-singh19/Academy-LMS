# ME-501 Internal Combustion Engines — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Engine classification, fuel-air cycles, carburetion and injection, combustion, performance testing and emissions.

## Unit 1: IC Engine Fundamentals

### Classification of IC Engines

Engines are classified by cycle, fuel and ignition.

- **SI Engine:** spark ignition engine working on Otto cycle
- **CI Engine:** compression ignition engine working on Diesel cycle
- **Two-stroke Engine:** one power stroke per revolution

### Engine Components & Terminology

Basic parts and dimensions.

- **Bore:** cylinder diameter
- **Stroke:** distance piston travels between dead centres
- **Compression Ratio:** (Vs + Vc)/Vc

### Valve Timing Diagram

Valves open and close around dead centres.

- **Valve Overlap:** both valves open near TDC
- **Inlet Valve Opening:** before TDC for better filling
- **Port Timing:** timing of ports in two-stroke engines

### Fuel-air & Actual Cycles

Real cycles deviate from air-standard cycles.

- **Fuel-air Cycle:** accounts for variable specific heat and dissociation
- **Time Loss:** due to finite combustion time
- **Exhaust Blowdown Loss:** loss when exhaust valve opens early

## Unit 2: Fuel Supply Systems

### Carburetion

Carburettors mix air and fuel in SI engines.

- **Simple Carburettor:** venturi draws fuel from float chamber
- **Air-fuel Ratio:** stoichiometric about 14.7:1 for petrol
- **Choke:** enriches mixture for cold starting

### Petrol Injection

Injection replaces carburettors.

- **MPFI:** multi-point fuel injection near each intake valve
- **GDI:** gasoline direct injection into cylinder
- **ECU:** electronic control unit managing injection

### Diesel Injection

CI engines inject fuel at high pressure.

- **Fuel Injection Pump:** delivers metered high-pressure fuel
- **Common Rail:** high-pressure accumulator feeding injectors
- **Injector Nozzle:** atomises fuel into spray

### Ignition Systems

SI engines need a spark at the right time.

- **Battery Ignition:** coil-based system with contact breaker
- **Electronic Ignition:** transistor-switched ignition
- **Spark Advance:** igniting before TDC at higher speeds

## Unit 3: Combustion

### Combustion in SI Engines

Flame propagates from the spark plug.

- **Ignition Lag:** delay between spark and flame development
- **Flame Propagation:** flame front travelling across chamber
- **Knock:** autoignition of end gas causing pressure waves

### Combustion in CI Engines

Fuel self-ignites after injection.

- **Ignition Delay:** time between injection and start of combustion
- **Diesel Knock:** sudden combustion of accumulated fuel
- **Cetane Number:** ignition quality of diesel fuel

### Fuel Ratings

Ratings quantify knock resistance.

- **Octane Number:** knock resistance of petrol versus iso-octane
- **Anti-knock Agent:** additive increasing octane number
- **Higher Octane:** allows higher compression ratio

### Combustion Chamber Design

Chamber shape affects combustion.

- **Squish:** radial inward air motion at TDC
- **Swirl:** rotational air motion improving mixing
- **Direct Injection Chamber:** open chamber for CI engines

## Unit 4: Engine Performance & Testing

### Performance Parameters

Engine output and efficiency are measured.

- **Indicated Power:** power developed in cylinder
- **Brake Power:** power at the crankshaft
- **Mechanical Efficiency:** BP / IP

### Fuel Consumption & Efficiency

Fuel use is compared across engines.

- **BSFC:** brake specific fuel consumption, kg/kWh
- **Brake Thermal Efficiency:** BP / (fuel energy input)
- **Volumetric Efficiency:** actual air inducted / swept volume

### Engine Testing

Tests determine friction and heat balance.

- **Morse Test:** finds IP of multi-cylinder engine by cutting cylinders
- **Willan's Line:** extrapolates fuel line to find friction power
- **Heat Balance Sheet:** distribution of fuel energy into BP, cooling, exhaust

### Supercharging & Turbocharging

Forced induction raises power.

- **Supercharging:** compressing intake air with engine-driven compressor
- **Turbocharger:** exhaust-driven turbine powering a compressor
- **Intercooler:** cools compressed air to increase density

## Unit 5: Lubrication, Cooling & Emissions

### Lubrication Systems

Lubrication reduces friction and wear.

- **Splash Lubrication:** crank dips into oil
- **Pressure Lubrication:** oil pump forces oil to bearings
- **Viscosity Index:** change of viscosity with temperature

### Cooling Systems

Excess heat must be removed.

- **Air Cooling:** fins dissipate heat
- **Water Cooling:** coolant circulated through jackets and radiator
- **Thermostat:** controls coolant flow to maintain temperature

### Engine Emissions

Combustion produces pollutants.

- **CO:** carbon monoxide from rich mixtures
- **NOx:** oxides of nitrogen at high temperatures
- **Particulate Matter:** soot mainly from diesel engines

### Emission Control

Devices and norms reduce emissions.

- **Catalytic Converter:** three-way catalyst converts CO, HC and NOx
- **EGR:** exhaust gas recirculation reduces NOx
- **Bharat Stage VI:** Indian emission norms equivalent to Euro VI
