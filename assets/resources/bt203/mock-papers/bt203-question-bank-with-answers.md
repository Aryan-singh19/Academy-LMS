## Unit 1: Engineering Materials

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Ferrous Metal*.  
  **Ans.** contains iron, e.g. steel
- **Q.** Define *Non-ferrous Metal*.  
  **Ans.** no iron, e.g. copper and aluminium
- **Q.** Define *Composite*.  
  **Ans.** combination of two or more materials
- **Q.** Define *Ductility*.  
  **Ans.** ability to be drawn into wire
- **Q.** Define *Malleability*.  
  **Ans.** ability to be hammered into sheets
- **Q.** Define *Toughness*.  
  **Ans.** energy absorbed before fracture
- **Q.** Define *Elastic Limit*.  
  **Ans.** stress up to which material regains shape
- **Q.** Define *Yield Point*.  
  **Ans.** stress where plastic deformation begins
- **Q.** Define *Ultimate Stress*.  
  **Ans.** maximum stress in the test
- **Q.** Define *Annealing*.  
  **Ans.** softening by slow cooling
- **Q.** Define *Hardening*.  
  **Ans.** quenching to increase hardness
- **Q.** Define *Tempering*.  
  **Ans.** reheating to reduce brittleness

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Classification of Materials* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Materials are metals, non-metals and composites. Ferrous Metal — contains iron, e.g. steel; Non-ferrous Metal — no iron, e.g. copper and aluminium; Composite — combination of two or more materials.
- **Q.** Explain *Mechanical Properties* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Properties describe response to loads. Ductility — ability to be drawn into wire; Malleability — ability to be hammered into sheets; Toughness — energy absorbed before fracture.
- **Q.** Explain *Stress-Strain Diagram* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Tensile behaviour of mild steel. Elastic Limit — stress up to which material regains shape; Yield Point — stress where plastic deformation begins; Ultimate Stress — maximum stress in the test.
- **Q.** Explain *Heat Treatment Basics* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Heating and cooling change properties. Annealing — softening by slow cooling; Hardening — quenching to increase hardness; Tempering — reheating to reduce brittleness.

  **Ans.** no temperature difference
- **Q.** Define *Zeroth Law*.

  **Ans.** energy conservation, Q − W = ΔU
- **Q.** Define *Second Law*.  
  **Ans.** heat cannot fully convert into work in a cycle
- **Q.** Define *Ideal Gas Equation*.  
  **Ans.** pV = mRT
- **Q.** Define *Isothermal Process*.  
  **Ans.** constant temperature
- **Q.** Define *Adiabatic Process*.  
  **Ans.** no heat transfer; pV^γ = constant
- **Q.** Define *Heat Engine*.  
  **Ans.** device converting heat into work
- **Q.** Define *Carnot Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** 1 − T2/T1
- **Q.** Define *Refrigerator*.  
  **Ans.** transfers heat from cold to hot using work

### Long answer (7 marks)

  **Ans.** spark ignition versus compression ignition
- **Q.** Define *Indicated Power*.  
  **Ans.** power in cylinder
- **Q.** Define *Brake Power*.

- **Q.** Define *Mechanical Efficiency*.  
  **Ans.** BP/IP

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Properties of Steam* in detail with a neat diagram.

  **Key points:** Boilers produce steam. Fire-tube Boiler — hot gases in tubes, e.g. Cochran; Water-tube Boiler — water in tubes, e.g. Babcock and Wilcox; Boiler Mountings — safety valves and gauges.
