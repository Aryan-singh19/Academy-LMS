# EC-603(C) Satellite Communication — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Orbital Mechanics

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Kepler's First Law*.  
  **Ans.** orbit is an ellipse with Earth at one focus
- **Q.** Define *Kepler's Third Law*.  
  **Ans.** T² proportional to a³
- **Q.** Define *Orbital Period*.  
  **Ans.** T = 2π√(a³/μ)
- **Q.** Define *GEO*.  
  **Ans.** geostationary orbit at about 35,786 km
- **Q.** Define *LEO*.  
  **Ans.** low Earth orbit, 500-2000 km
- **Q.** Define *MEO*.  
  **Ans.** medium Earth orbit used by GPS
- **Q.** Define *Apogee*.  
  **Ans.** farthest point from Earth
- **Q.** Define *Perigee*.  
  **Ans.** closest point to Earth
- **Q.** Define *Orbital Perturbation*.  
  **Ans.** drift due to Earth oblateness, Sun and Moon
- **Q.** Define *Azimuth*.  
  **Ans.** horizontal pointing angle
- **Q.** Define *Elevation Angle*.  
  **Ans.** angle above the horizon
- **Q.** Define *Launch Vehicle*.  
  **Ans.** rocket placing satellite into orbit, e.g. PSLV, GSLV

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Kepler's Laws* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Satellite motion obeys Kepler's laws. Kepler's First Law — orbit is an ellipse with Earth at one focus; Kepler's Third Law — T² proportional to a³; Orbital Period — T = 2π√(a³/μ).
- **Q.** Explain *Types of Orbits* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Orbits suit different missions. GEO — geostationary orbit at about 35,786 km; LEO — low Earth orbit, 500-2000 km; MEO — medium Earth orbit used by GPS.
- **Q.** Explain *Orbital Elements & Perturbations* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Parameters fix an orbit; forces disturb it. Apogee — farthest point from Earth; Perigee — closest point to Earth; Orbital Perturbation — drift due to Earth oblateness, Sun and Moon.
- **Q.** Explain *Look Angles & Launch* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Ground antennas point using azimuth and elevation. Azimuth — horizontal pointing angle; Elevation Angle — angle above the horizon; Launch Vehicle — rocket placing satellite into orbit, e.g. PSLV, GSLV.

## Unit 3: Link Design

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *C-band*.  
  **Ans.** 6 GHz uplink, 4 GHz downlink
- **Q.** Define *Ku-band*.  
  **Ans.** 14 GHz uplink, 12 GHz downlink
- **Q.** Define *Ka-band*.  
  **Ans.** 30/20 GHz with high capacity but rain fade
- **Q.** Define *EIRP*.  
  **Ans.** Pt + Gt in dBW
- **Q.** Define *Free Space Loss*.  
  **Ans.** 20 log(4πd/λ) dB
- **Q.** Define *Carrier-to-Noise Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** C/N = EIRP + Gr − losses − N
- **Q.** Define *G/T*.  
  **Ans.** figure of merit of receiving station
- **Q.** Define *System Noise Temperature*.  
  **Ans.** antenna plus receiver noise temperature
- **Q.** Define *Uplink vs Downlink*.  
  **Ans.** overall C/N combines both links
- **Q.** Define *Rain Attenuation*.  
  **Ans.** signal loss from rain, severe above 10 GHz
- **Q.** Define *Faraday Rotation*.  
  **Ans.** rotation of polarisation in ionosphere
- **Q.** Define *Scintillation*.  
  **Ans.** rapid fluctuation due to atmospheric turbulence

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Frequency Bands* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Satellite services use designated bands. C-band — 6 GHz uplink, 4 GHz downlink; Ku-band — 14 GHz uplink, 12 GHz downlink; Ka-band — 30/20 GHz with high capacity but rain fade.
- **Q.** Explain *Link Budget* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Link budget accounts for all gains and losses. EIRP — Pt + Gt in dBW; Free Space Loss — 20 log(4πd/λ) dB; Carrier-to-Noise Ratio — C/N = EIRP + Gr − losses − N.
- **Q.** Explain *G/T & System Noise* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Receive performance uses G/T. G/T — figure of merit of receiving station; System Noise Temperature — antenna plus receiver noise temperature; Uplink vs Downlink — overall C/N combines both links.
- **Q.** Explain *Atmospheric Effects* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Propagation impairs higher bands. Rain Attenuation — signal loss from rain, severe above 10 GHz; Faraday Rotation — rotation of polarisation in ionosphere; Scintillation — rapid fluctuation due to atmospheric turbulence.

## Unit 4: Multiple Access

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *FDMA*.  
  **Ans.** each earth station gets a frequency slot
- **Q.** Define *Intermodulation*.  
  **Ans.** products generated in non-linear amplifiers
- **Q.** Define *Back-off*.  
  **Ans.** reducing drive to keep amplifier linear
- **Q.** Define *TDMA*.  
  **Ans.** stations transmit in assigned time slots
- **Q.** Define *Reference Burst*.  
  **Ans.** timing reference for the frame
- **Q.** Define *Preamble*.  
  **Ans.** synchronisation bits at start of each burst
- **Q.** Define *CDMA*.  
  **Ans.** stations separated by spreading codes
- **Q.** Define *DAMA*.  
  **Ans.** demand assigned multiple access
- **Q.** Define *SPADE*.  
  **Ans.** early DAMA system for voice channels
- **Q.** Define *VSAT*.  
  **Ans.** very small aperture terminal
- **Q.** Define *Star Topology*.  
  **Ans.** all VSATs communicate via a hub
- **Q.** Define *Mesh Topology*.  
  **Ans.** VSATs communicate directly

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *FDMA in Satellites* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Carriers share transponder by frequency. FDMA — each earth station gets a frequency slot; Intermodulation — products generated in non-linear amplifiers; Back-off — reducing drive to keep amplifier linear.
- **Q.** Explain *TDMA in Satellites* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Stations share by time bursts. TDMA — stations transmit in assigned time slots; Reference Burst — timing reference for the frame; Preamble — synchronisation bits at start of each burst.
- **Q.** Explain *CDMA & DAMA* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Codes or demand assignment improve efficiency. CDMA — stations separated by spreading codes; DAMA — demand assigned multiple access; SPADE — early DAMA system for voice channels.
- **Q.** Explain *VSAT Networks* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Small terminals connect to a hub. VSAT — very small aperture terminal; Star Topology — all VSATs communicate via a hub; Mesh Topology — VSATs communicate directly.
