## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Kepler's First Law: orbit is an ellipse with Earth at one focus
- **Q1(b):** GEO: geostationary orbit at about 35,786 km | LEO: low Earth orbit, 500-2000 km | MEO: medium Earth orbit used by GPS
- **Q2(a):** Spin Stabilisation: spinning body gives gyroscopic stability
- **Q2(b):** Solar Panels: primary power source | Batteries: power during eclipse | Thermal Control: keeps components within temperature limits
- **Q3(a):** Uplink vs Downlink: overall C/N combines both links
- **Q3(b):** Rain Attenuation: signal loss from rain, severe above 10 GHz | Faraday Rotation: rotation of polarisation in ionosphere | Scintillation: rapid fluctuation due to atmospheric turbulence
- **Q4(a):** VSAT: very small aperture terminal
- **Q4(b):** FDMA: each earth station gets a frequency slot | Intermodulation: products generated in non-linear amplifiers | Back-off: reducing drive to keep amplifier linear
- **Q5(a):** LNA: low-noise amplifier at the receiver front end
- **Q5(b):** DTH: direct-to-home television | DVB-S2: digital video broadcasting standard for satellite | Footprint: coverage area on Earth
- **Q6(a):** Launch Vehicle: rocket placing satellite into orbit, e.g. PSLV, GSLV
- **Q6(b):** Kepler's First Law: orbit is an ellipse with Earth at one focus | Kepler's Third Law: T² proportional to a³ | Orbital Period: T = 2π√(a³/μ)
- **Q7(a):** C-band: 6 GHz uplink, 4 GHz downlink
- **Q7(b):** EIRP: Pt + Gt in dBW | Free Space Loss: 20 log(4πd/λ) dB | Carrier-to-Noise Ratio: C/N = EIRP + Gr − losses − N
- **Q8(a):** DVB-S2: digital video broadcasting standard for satellite
- **Q8(b):** GPS: US global positioning system | NavIC: Indian regional navigation satellite system | Trilateration: position from distances to several satellites
