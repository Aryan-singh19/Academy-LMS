## Unit 2: Engineering Curves

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Ellipse*.  
  **Ans.** eccentricity less than 1
- **Q.** Define *Parabola*.  
  **Ans.** eccentricity equal to 1
- **Q.** Define *Hyperbola*.  
  **Ans.** eccentricity greater than 1
- **Q.** Define *Eccentricity Method*.  
  **Ans.** distance from focus / distance from directrix
- **Q.** Define *Concentric Circle Method*.  
  **Ans.** draws ellipse from two circles
- **Q.** Define *Rectangle Method*.  
  **Ans.** draws ellipse or parabola in a rectangle
- **Q.** Define *Cycloid*.  
  **Ans.** locus of a point on a circle rolling on a straight line
- **Q.** Define *Epicycloid*.  
  **Ans.** circle rolls outside another circle
- **Q.** Define *Hypocycloid*.  
  **Ans.** circle rolls inside another circle
- **Q.** Define *Involute*.  
  **Ans.** locus of end of a string unwound from a circle
- **Q.** Define *Archimedean Spiral*.  
  **Ans.** radius increases uniformly with angle
- **Q.** Define *Helix*.  
  **Ans.** curve on a cylinder with uniform pitch

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Conic Sections* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Conics are sections of a cone. Ellipse — eccentricity less than 1; Parabola — eccentricity equal to 1; Hyperbola — eccentricity greater than 1.
- **Q.** Explain *Construction of Conics* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Conics are drawn by standard methods. Eccentricity Method — distance from focus / distance from directrix; Concentric Circle Method — draws ellipse from two circles; Rectangle Method — draws ellipse or parabola in a rectangle.
- **Q.** Explain *Cycloidal Curves* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Rolling circles generate cycloids. Cycloid — locus of a point on a circle rolling on a straight line; Epicycloid — circle rolls outside another circle; Hypocycloid — circle rolls inside another circle.
- **Q.** Explain *Involutes & Spirals* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Involutes are used in gear teeth. Involute — locus of end of a string unwound from a circle; Archimedean Spiral — radius increases uniformly with angle; Helix — curve on a cylinder with uniform pitch.

- **Q.** Define *Apparent Inclination*.

  **Ans.** appears as a line in that view
- **Q.** Define *Inclined Plane*.

- **Q.** Define *True Shape*.

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Orthographic Projection* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** 3D objects are shown in 2D views. First Angle Projection — object between observer and plane; used in India; Third Angle Projection — plane between observer and object; Reference Line — XY line separating views.
- **Q.** Explain *Projection of Points* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Points are located by distances from planes. Front View — projection on vertical plane; Top View — projection on horizontal plane; Quadrant — position of point relative to HP and VP.
- **Q.** Explain *Projection of Lines* in detail with a neat diagram.

- **Q.** Explain *Projection of Planes* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Planes are drawn in stages of inclination. Plane Perpendicular to One Plane — appears as a line in that view; Inclined Plane — drawn in two or three stages; True Shape — seen when plane is parallel to projection plane.

## Unit 4: Projections & Sections of Solids

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Prism*.  
  **Ans.** two equal end faces joined by rectangles
- **Q.** Define *Pyramid*.  
  **Ans.** polygon base with triangular faces meeting at apex
- **Q.** Define *Cone*.  
  **Ans.** circular base tapering to apex
- **Q.** Define *Axis Perpendicular to HP*.  
  **Ans.** base seen as true shape in top view
- **Q.** Define *Axis Inclined*.  
  **Ans.** drawn in two stages
- **Q.** Define *Generator*.  
  **Ans.** line from apex to base edge of cone
- **Q.** Define *Section Plane*.  
  **Ans.** imaginary cutting plane
- **Q.** Define *True Shape of Section*.  
  **Ans.** seen on auxiliary plane parallel to section
- **Q.** Define *Hatching*.  
  **Ans.** thin lines at 45° indicating cut surface
- **Q.** Define *Parallel Line Development*.  
  **Ans.** used for prisms and cylinders
- **Q.** Define *Radial Line Development*.  
  **Ans.** used for pyramids and cones
- **Q.** Define *Application*.  
  **Ans.** sheet metal work such as ducts and funnels

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Types of Solids* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Solids are polyhedra or solids of revolution. Prism — two equal end faces joined by rectangles; Pyramid — polygon base with triangular faces meeting at apex; Cone — circular base tapering to apex.
- **Q.** Explain *Projection of Solids* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Solids are projected in simple and inclined positions. Axis Perpendicular to HP — base seen as true shape in top view; Axis Inclined — drawn in two stages; Generator — line from apex to base edge of cone.
- **Q.** Explain *Sections of Solids* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Cutting planes reveal interior shape. Section Plane — imaginary cutting plane; True Shape of Section — seen on auxiliary plane parallel to section; Hatching — thin lines at 45° indicating cut surface.
- **Q.** Explain *Development of Surfaces* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Surfaces are unfolded into flat patterns. Parallel Line Development — used for prisms and cylinders; Radial Line Development — used for pyramids and cones; Application — sheet metal work such as ducts and funnels.
