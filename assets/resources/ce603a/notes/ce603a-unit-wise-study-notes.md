# CE-603(A) Water Resource Engineering — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Hydrology, irrigation, canals, dams, spillways, hydraulic structures and water management.

## Unit 1: Hydrology

### Hydrological Cycle

Water circulates among atmosphere, land and sea.

- **Precipitation:** rain, snow and hail
- **Infiltration:** water entering soil
- **Runoff:** water flowing over surface to streams

### Rainfall Measurement

Rain gauges record precipitation.

- **Symons Rain Gauge:** non-recording gauge
- **Average Rainfall:** arithmetic, Thiessen polygon or isohyetal method
- **Double Mass Curve:** checks consistency of records

### Runoff & Hydrographs

Hydrographs show discharge over time.

- **Hydrograph:** discharge versus time
- **Unit Hydrograph:** runoff from 1 cm effective rain
- **Rational Formula:** Q = CIA/360

### Floods

Flood estimation guides design.

- **Design Flood:** flood adopted for structure design
- **Return Period:** average interval of occurrence
- **Gumbel's Method:** statistical flood frequency analysis

## Unit 2: Irrigation Engineering

### Irrigation & Its Types

Irrigation supplies water to crops.

- **Flow Irrigation:** by gravity from canals
- **Lift Irrigation:** water lifted by pumps
- **Drip Irrigation:** water applied at root zone

### Water Requirement of Crops

Crops need specific water.

- **Duty:** area irrigated per unit discharge
- **Delta:** total depth of water for crop
- **Relation:** Δ = 8.64B/D

### Soil-Water Relations

Soil stores water for plants.

- **Field Capacity:** water held after drainage
- **Permanent Wilting Point:** moisture where plants wilt
- **Available Water:** field capacity minus wilting point

### Irrigation Efficiencies

Efficiencies measure water use.

- **Water Conveyance Efficiency:** water delivered / diverted
- **Application Efficiency:** water stored in root zone / delivered
- **Consumptive Use:** evapotranspiration of crop

## Unit 3: Canals

### Canal Classification

Canals distribute irrigation water.

- **Main Canal:** takes water from headworks
- **Distributary:** supplies water to fields
- **Alignment:** contour, watershed or side slope canal

### Canal Design

Silt theories design stable canals.

- **Kennedy's Theory:** critical velocity V0 = 0.55 m D^0.64
- **Lacey's Theory:** regime channel theory
- **Silt Factor:** f = 1.76√d

### Canal Lining

Lining reduces seepage losses.

- **Lined Canal:** concrete or brick lining
- **Seepage Loss:** water lost through bed and banks
- **Benefits of Lining:** reduces waterlogging

### Waterlogging & Drainage

Excess water harms land.

- **Waterlogging:** saturated root zone
- **Tile Drain:** subsurface drainage
- **Salinity:** salt accumulation in soil

## Unit 4: Dams & Spillways

### Types of Dams

Dams store and divert water.

- **Gravity Dam:** resists by own weight
- **Earth Dam:** embankment of soil
- **Arch Dam:** transfers load to abutments

### Gravity Dam Design

Stability checks ensure safety.

- **Forces on Gravity Dam:** water pressure, uplift, silt, wave and earthquake
- **Elementary Profile:** triangular section
- **Factor of Safety Against Sliding:** shear friction factor

### Earth Dams

Earth dams need seepage control.

- **Phreatic Line:** top seepage line in dam
- **Filter:** prevents piping
- **Slope Stability:** analysed by slip circle method

### Spillways & Energy Dissipation

Spillways pass flood water safely.

- **Ogee Spillway:** shaped overflow spillway
- **Hydraulic Jump:** dissipates energy
- **Stilling Basin:** structure for energy dissipation

## Unit 5: Hydraulic Structures & Management

### Diversion Headworks

Weirs and barrages divert flow.

- **Weir:** raises water level for diversion
- **Barrage:** gated structure across river
- **Bligh's Creep Theory:** seepage path design

### Canal Structures

Structures regulate canal flow.

- **Canal Fall:** drop structure on steep ground
- **Aqueduct:** carries canal over drainage
- **Cross Regulator:** controls canal levels

### Watershed Management

Watersheds are managed as units.

- **Watershed:** area draining to a point
- **Check Dam:** small dam reducing erosion
- **Contour Bunding:** embankments along contours

### Integrated Water Management

Resources are managed holistically.

- **IWRM:** integrated water resources management
- **Groundwater Recharge:** artificial replenishment
- **Water Policy:** national guidelines on water use
