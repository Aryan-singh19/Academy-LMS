## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Refrigeration: process of removing heat from a space below ambient
- **Q1(b):** Reversed Carnot COP: T2/(T1 − T2) | Limitations: Carnot cycle is impractical due to wet compression | Heat Pump COP: T1/(T1 − T2)
- **Q2(a):** Throttling Process: constant enthalpy expansion
- **Q2(b):** Subcooling: cooling liquid below saturation increases refrigerating effect | Superheating: vapour heated above saturation at evaporator exit | Volumetric Efficiency: affects compressor capacity
- **Q3(a):** Vortex Tube: separates compressed air into hot and cold streams
- **Q3(b):** Reciprocating Compressor: common in small systems | Thermostatic Expansion Valve: maintains constant superheat | Evaporator: coil where refrigerant absorbs heat
- **Q4(a):** Bypass Factor: fraction of air not contacting the coil
- **Q4(b):** Specific Humidity: mass of water vapour per kg of dry air | Relative Humidity: vapour pressure / saturation vapour pressure | Dew Point: temperature at which condensation starts
- **Q5(a):** Comfort Zone: conditions where most people feel comfortable
- **Q5(b):** Sensible Heat Load: load from temperature difference | Latent Heat Load: load from moisture | Solar Heat Gain: heat through windows and walls
- **Q6(a):** GWP: global warming potential
- **Q6(b):** Refrigeration: process of removing heat from a space below ambient | Ton of Refrigeration: 3.5 kW, heat to freeze 1 ton of water in 24 h | COP: refrigerating effect / work input
- **Q7(a):** Vapour Absorption System: absorber, generator and pump replace compressor
- **Q7(b):** Electrolux Refrigerator: uses ammonia, water and hydrogen | No Moving Parts: silent operation | Partial Pressure Principle: hydrogen allows evaporation at low partial pressure
- **Q8(a):** Latent Heat Load: load from moisture
- **Q8(b):** Window AC: unitary system for single rooms | Split AC: indoor and outdoor units connected by piping | Central AC: chilled water or ducted systems for buildings
