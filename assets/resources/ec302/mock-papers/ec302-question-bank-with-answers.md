# EC-302 Electronic Measurement & Instrumentation — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Measurement Fundamentals

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Accuracy*.  
  **Ans.** closeness of a reading to the true value
- **Q.** Define *Precision*.  
  **Ans.** closeness of repeated readings to each other
- **Q.** Define *Resolution*.  
  **Ans.** smallest input change an instrument can detect
- **Q.** Define *Gross Error*.  
  **Ans.** human mistake in reading or recording
- **Q.** Define *Systematic Error*.  
  **Ans.** repeatable error from instrument or environment
- **Q.** Define *Random Error*.  
  **Ans.** unpredictable error reduced by averaging
- **Q.** Define *Calibration*.  
  **Ans.** comparing an instrument with a known standard
- **Q.** Define *Primary Standard*.  
  **Ans.** highest accuracy standard kept by national labs
- **Q.** Define *Traceability*.  
  **Ans.** unbroken chain of calibrations to national standards
- **Q.** Define *Arithmetic Mean*.  
  **Ans.** best estimate of the true value from many readings
- **Q.** Define *Standard Deviation*.  
  **Ans.** spread of readings about the mean
- **Q.** Define *Probable Error*.  
  **Ans.** 0.6745 × standard deviation

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Characteristics of Instruments* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Instruments are judged by static and dynamic characteristics. Accuracy — closeness of a reading to the true value; Precision — closeness of repeated readings to each other; Resolution — smallest input change an instrument can detect.
- **Q.** Explain *Errors in Measurement* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Every measurement contains some error. Gross Error — human mistake in reading or recording; Systematic Error — repeatable error from instrument or environment; Random Error — unpredictable error reduced by averaging.
- **Q.** Explain *Standards & Calibration* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Instruments are calibrated against standards. Calibration — comparing an instrument with a known standard; Primary Standard — highest accuracy standard kept by national labs; Traceability — unbroken chain of calibrations to national standards.
- **Q.** Explain *Statistical Analysis of Data* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Statistics quantify uncertainty in readings. Arithmetic Mean — best estimate of the true value from many readings; Standard Deviation — spread of readings about the mean; Probable Error — 0.6745 × standard deviation.

## Unit 2: Analog & Digital Meters

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *PMMC*.  
  **Ans.** permanent magnet moving coil instrument with linear scale
- **Q.** Define *Shunt*.  
  **Ans.** low resistance in parallel to extend ammeter range
- **Q.** Define *Multiplier*.  
  **Ans.** high series resistance to extend voltmeter range
- **Q.** Define *Moving Iron Instrument*.  
  **Ans.** works on AC and DC with a non-linear scale
- **Q.** Define *Electrodynamometer*.  
  **Ans.** fixed and moving coils used as a wattmeter
- **Q.** Define *Loading Effect*.  
  **Ans.** meter draws current and alters the measured circuit
- **Q.** Define *Ramp-type DVM*.  
  **Ans.** counts clock pulses during a linear ramp
- **Q.** Define *Dual-slope DVM*.  
  **Ans.** integrating converter with good noise rejection
- **Q.** Define *Successive Approximation DVM*.  
  **Ans.** binary search conversion
- **Q.** Define *Digital Multimeter*.  
  **Ans.** measures voltage, current and resistance digitally
- **Q.** Define *3½ Digit Display*.  
  **Ans.** maximum reading 1999
- **Q.** Define *Frequency Counter*.  
  **Ans.** counts cycles in a precise time gate

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *PMMC Instruments* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Moving-coil meters measure DC current and voltage. PMMC — permanent magnet moving coil instrument with linear scale; Shunt — low resistance in parallel to extend ammeter range; Multiplier — high series resistance to extend voltmeter range.
- **Q.** Explain *Moving Iron & Electrodynamometer Instruments* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** These meters measure AC and DC quantities. Moving Iron Instrument — works on AC and DC with a non-linear scale; Electrodynamometer — fixed and moving coils used as a wattmeter; Loading Effect — meter draws current and alters the measured circuit.
- **Q.** Explain *Digital Voltmeters* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** DVMs convert analog voltage to digital readings. Ramp-type DVM — counts clock pulses during a linear ramp; Dual-slope DVM — integrating converter with good noise rejection; Successive Approximation DVM — binary search conversion.
- **Q.** Explain *Digital Multimeter & Frequency Counter* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Digital meters measure many quantities. Digital Multimeter — measures voltage, current and resistance digitally; 3½ Digit Display — maximum reading 1999; Frequency Counter — counts cycles in a precise time gate.

## Unit 3: Bridges

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Wheatstone Bridge*.  
  **Ans.** balance condition R1·R4 = R2·R3 for medium resistance
- **Q.** Define *Kelvin Double Bridge*.  
  **Ans.** measures low resistance, removing lead resistance
- **Q.** Define *Null Detection*.  
  **Ans.** galvanometer shows zero at balance
- **Q.** Define *Maxwell Bridge*.  
  **Ans.** measures medium-Q inductance with a capacitor
- **Q.** Define *Hay's Bridge*.  
  **Ans.** suits high-Q coils
- **Q.** Define *Anderson Bridge*.  
  **Ans.** accurate inductance measurement using a modified Maxwell bridge
- **Q.** Define *Schering Bridge*.  
  **Ans.** measures capacitance and dissipation factor
- **Q.** Define *Wien Bridge*.  
  **Ans.** frequency-dependent bridge used in oscillators
- **Q.** Define *De Sauty Bridge*.  
  **Ans.** simple capacitance comparison bridge
- **Q.** Define *Q-meter*.  
  **Ans.** measures Q using series resonance
- **Q.** Define *Quality Factor*.  
  **Ans.** Q = ωL/R
- **Q.** Define *Distributed Capacitance*.  
  **Ans.** self-capacitance of a coil found with a Q-meter

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *DC Bridges* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Bridges measure resistance by null balance. Wheatstone Bridge — balance condition R1·R4 = R2·R3 for medium resistance; Kelvin Double Bridge — measures low resistance, removing lead resistance; Null Detection — galvanometer shows zero at balance.
- **Q.** Explain *AC Bridges for Inductance* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** AC bridges balance both magnitude and phase. Maxwell Bridge — measures medium-Q inductance with a capacitor; Hay's Bridge — suits high-Q coils; Anderson Bridge — accurate inductance measurement using a modified Maxwell bridge.
- **Q.** Explain *AC Bridges for Capacitance & Frequency* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Other bridges measure capacitance and frequency. Schering Bridge — measures capacitance and dissipation factor; Wien Bridge — frequency-dependent bridge used in oscillators; De Sauty Bridge — simple capacitance comparison bridge.
- **Q.** Explain *Q-meter* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** The Q-meter measures coil quality factor. Q-meter — measures Q using series resonance; Quality Factor — Q = ωL/R; Distributed Capacitance — self-capacitance of a coil found with a Q-meter.

## Unit 4: Oscilloscopes & Signal Generators

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *CRT*.  
  **Ans.** cathode ray tube with electron gun, deflection plates and screen
- **Q.** Define *Time Base*.  
  **Ans.** sawtooth sweep moving the beam horizontally
- **Q.** Define *Deflection Sensitivity*.  
  **Ans.** beam deflection per volt
- **Q.** Define *Lissajous Figure*.  
  **Ans.** pattern from two sinusoids on X and Y plates
- **Q.** Define *Frequency Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** fy/fx = horizontal tangencies / vertical tangencies
- **Q.** Define *Phase Angle*.  
  **Ans.** sin φ = Y-intercept / Y-max for an ellipse
- **Q.** Define *DSO*.  
  **Ans.** digitises and stores waveforms in memory
- **Q.** Define *Sampling Rate*.  
  **Ans.** samples per second taken by the scope
- **Q.** Define *Pre-trigger View*.  
  **Ans.** shows events before the trigger point
- **Q.** Define *Function Generator*.  
  **Ans.** produces sine, square and triangular waves
- **Q.** Define *Spectrum Analyser*.  
  **Ans.** displays signal amplitude versus frequency
- **Q.** Define *Wave Analyser*.  
  **Ans.** measures amplitude of individual harmonics

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Cathode Ray Oscilloscope* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** The CRO displays voltage against time. CRT — cathode ray tube with electron gun, deflection plates and screen; Time Base — sawtooth sweep moving the beam horizontally; Deflection Sensitivity — beam deflection per volt.
- **Q.** Explain *Lissajous Patterns & Measurements* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** CROs measure phase and frequency. Lissajous Figure — pattern from two sinusoids on X and Y plates; Frequency Ratio — fy/fx = horizontal tangencies / vertical tangencies; Phase Angle — sin φ = Y-intercept / Y-max for an ellipse.
- **Q.** Explain *Digital Storage Oscilloscope* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** DSOs sample and store waveforms. DSO — digitises and stores waveforms in memory; Sampling Rate — samples per second taken by the scope; Pre-trigger View — shows events before the trigger point.
- **Q.** Explain *Signal Generators & Analysers* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Generators supply test signals; analysers study spectra. Function Generator — produces sine, square and triangular waves; Spectrum Analyser — displays signal amplitude versus frequency; Wave Analyser — measures amplitude of individual harmonics.

## Unit 5: Transducers & Data Acquisition

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Active Transducer*.  
  **Ans.** generates its own output, e.g. thermocouple
- **Q.** Define *Passive Transducer*.  
  **Ans.** needs external excitation, e.g. strain gauge
- **Q.** Define *Inverse Transducer*.  
  **Ans.** converts electrical signal into physical quantity
- **Q.** Define *Strain Gauge*.  
  **Ans.** resistance changes with strain
- **Q.** Define *Gauge Factor*.  
  **Ans.** (ΔR/R)/(Δl/l)
- **Q.** Define *LVDT*.  
  **Ans.** linear variable differential transformer for displacement
- **Q.** Define *Thermocouple*.  
  **Ans.** Seebeck-effect sensor generating voltage with temperature
- **Q.** Define *RTD*.  
  **Ans.** resistance temperature detector, usually platinum
- **Q.** Define *Thermistor*.  
  **Ans.** semiconductor with large negative temperature coefficient
- **Q.** Define *Signal Conditioning*.  
  **Ans.** amplification, filtering and isolation
- **Q.** Define *Sample-and-Hold*.  
  **Ans.** holds the input steady during conversion
- **Q.** Define *Telemetry*.  
  **Ans.** measuring and transmitting data from remote points

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Transducer Classification* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Transducers convert physical quantities to electrical signals. Active Transducer — generates its own output, e.g. thermocouple; Passive Transducer — needs external excitation, e.g. strain gauge; Inverse Transducer — converts electrical signal into physical quantity.
- **Q.** Explain *Strain Gauges & LVDT* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Displacement and strain are measured electrically. Strain Gauge — resistance changes with strain; Gauge Factor — (ΔR/R)/(Δl/l); LVDT — linear variable differential transformer for displacement.
- **Q.** Explain *Temperature & Pressure Transducers* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Common sensors measure temperature and pressure. Thermocouple — Seebeck-effect sensor generating voltage with temperature; RTD — resistance temperature detector, usually platinum; Thermistor — semiconductor with large negative temperature coefficient.
- **Q.** Explain *Data Acquisition Systems* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** DAQ systems condition, convert and record signals. Signal Conditioning — amplification, filtering and isolation; Sample-and-Hold — holds the input steady during conversion; Telemetry — measuring and transmitting data from remote points.
