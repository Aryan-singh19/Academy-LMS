# CE-302 Construction Materials — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Stones, Bricks & Tiles

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Igneous Rock*.  
  **Ans.** formed by cooling of magma, e.g. granite; hard and durable
- **Q.** Define *Sedimentary Rock*.  
  **Ans.** formed by deposition, e.g. sandstone and limestone
- **Q.** Define *Dressing of Stones*.  
  **Ans.** shaping quarried stone to required size and finish
- **Q.** Define *Crushing Strength Test*.  
  **Ans.** compressive strength of a stone cube
- **Q.** Define *Water Absorption Test*.  
  **Ans.** percentage of water absorbed in 24 hours
- **Q.** Define *Attrition Test*.  
  **Ans.** resistance of stone to wear by rubbing
- **Q.** Define *Standard Brick Size*.  
  **Ans.** 190 × 90 × 90 mm (modular) in India
- **Q.** Define *Brick Earth Constituents*.  
  **Ans.** silica, alumina, lime, iron oxide and magnesia
- **Q.** Define *Efflorescence*.  
  **Ans.** white salt deposits caused by soluble salts in bricks
- **Q.** Define *Roofing Tile*.  
  **Ans.** clay tile such as Mangalore pattern
- **Q.** Define *Terracotta*.  
  **Ans.** burnt clay used for ornamental work
- **Q.** Define *Vitrified Tile*.  
  **Ans.** dense low-porosity tile fired at high temperature

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Building Stones* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Natural stones are classified and tested for construction use. Igneous Rock — formed by cooling of magma, e.g. granite; hard and durable; Sedimentary Rock — formed by deposition, e.g. sandstone and limestone; Dressing of Stones — shaping quarried stone to required size and finish.
- **Q.** Explain *Tests on Stones* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Tests decide whether a stone suits a structure. Crushing Strength Test — compressive strength of a stone cube; Water Absorption Test — percentage of water absorbed in 24 hours; Attrition Test — resistance of stone to wear by rubbing.
- **Q.** Explain *Bricks* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Burnt clay bricks are the most common masonry unit. Standard Brick Size — 190 × 90 × 90 mm (modular) in India; Brick Earth Constituents — silica, alumina, lime, iron oxide and magnesia; Efflorescence — white salt deposits caused by soluble salts in bricks.
- **Q.** Explain *Tiles & Clay Products* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Burnt clay products include tiles and pipes. Roofing Tile — clay tile such as Mangalore pattern; Terracotta — burnt clay used for ornamental work; Vitrified Tile — dense low-porosity tile fired at high temperature.

## Unit 2: Lime, Cement & Mortar

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Fat Lime*.  
  **Ans.** high-calcium lime that slakes vigorously
- **Q.** Define *Hydraulic Lime*.  
  **Ans.** sets under water due to clay content
- **Q.** Define *Slaking*.  
  **Ans.** adding water to quicklime to form hydrated lime
- **Q.** Define *Bogue Compounds*.  
  **Ans.** C3S, C2S, C3A and C4AF
- **Q.** Define *C3S*.  
  **Ans.** responsible for early strength
- **Q.** Define *Heat of Hydration*.  
  **Ans.** heat released when cement reacts with water
- **Q.** Define *PPC*.  
  **Ans.** Portland pozzolana cement with fly ash
- **Q.** Define *Rapid Hardening Cement*.  
  **Ans.** gains strength quickly
- **Q.** Define *Vicat Apparatus*.  
  **Ans.** finds initial and final setting time
- **Q.** Define *Cement Mortar*.  
  **Ans.** cement, sand and water
- **Q.** Define *Mix Proportion*.  
  **Ans.** e.g. 1:6 cement to sand for brickwork
- **Q.** Define *Lime Mortar*.  
  **Ans.** lime and sand, slower setting

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Lime* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Lime is a traditional binding material. Fat Lime — high-calcium lime that slakes vigorously; Hydraulic Lime — sets under water due to clay content; Slaking — adding water to quicklime to form hydrated lime.
- **Q.** Explain *Portland Cement* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Cement hydrates to bind aggregates. Bogue Compounds — C3S, C2S, C3A and C4AF; C3S — responsible for early strength; Heat of Hydration — heat released when cement reacts with water.
- **Q.** Explain *Types & Tests of Cement* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Different cements suit different works. PPC — Portland pozzolana cement with fly ash; Rapid Hardening Cement — gains strength quickly; Vicat Apparatus — finds initial and final setting time.
- **Q.** Explain *Mortar* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Mortar binds masonry units. Cement Mortar — cement, sand and water; Mix Proportion — e.g. 1:6 cement to sand for brickwork; Lime Mortar — lime and sand, slower setting.

## Unit 3: Concrete

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Fine Aggregate*.  
  **Ans.** particles passing 4.75 mm sieve
- **Q.** Define *Coarse Aggregate*.  
  **Ans.** particles retained on 4.75 mm sieve
- **Q.** Define *Fineness Modulus*.  
  **Ans.** index of aggregate coarseness from sieve analysis
- **Q.** Define *Workability*.  
  **Ans.** ease of mixing, placing and compacting
- **Q.** Define *Slump Test*.  
  **Ans.** measures consistency of fresh concrete
- **Q.** Define *Segregation*.  
  **Ans.** separation of coarse aggregate from the mix
- **Q.** Define *Water-Cement Ratio Law*.  
  **Ans.** strength falls as w/c ratio rises (Abrams' law)
- **Q.** Define *Curing*.  
  **Ans.** maintaining moisture for hydration
- **Q.** Define *Characteristic Strength*.  
  **Ans.** strength below which only 5% results fall
- **Q.** Define *Plasticiser*.  
  **Ans.** improves workability at same water content
- **Q.** Define *Accelerator*.  
  **Ans.** speeds setting, e.g. calcium chloride
- **Q.** Define *Self-compacting Concrete*.  
  **Ans.** flows and compacts under its own weight

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Constituents of Concrete* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Concrete is cement, aggregates and water. Fine Aggregate — particles passing 4.75 mm sieve; Coarse Aggregate — particles retained on 4.75 mm sieve; Fineness Modulus — index of aggregate coarseness from sieve analysis.
- **Q.** Explain *Fresh Concrete Properties* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Workability governs placing and compaction. Workability — ease of mixing, placing and compacting; Slump Test — measures consistency of fresh concrete; Segregation — separation of coarse aggregate from the mix.
- **Q.** Explain *Hardened Concrete Properties* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Strength and durability of concrete. Water-Cement Ratio Law — strength falls as w/c ratio rises (Abrams' law); Curing — maintaining moisture for hydration; Characteristic Strength — strength below which only 5% results fall.
- **Q.** Explain *Admixtures & Special Concretes* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Admixtures modify concrete properties. Plasticiser — improves workability at same water content; Accelerator — speeds setting, e.g. calcium chloride; Self-compacting Concrete — flows and compacts under its own weight.
