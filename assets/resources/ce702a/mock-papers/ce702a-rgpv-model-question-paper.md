## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Prestressed Concrete: concrete with induced compressive stresses
- **Q1(b):** Pre-tensioning: tendons tensioned before concrete is placed | Hoyer System: long-line pre-tensioning | Bond Transfer: prestress transferred by bond
- **Q2(a):** Kern Distance: limit of eccentricity for no tension
- **Q2(b):** Load Balancing Method: tendon profile balances load | Parabolic Tendon: balances uniformly distributed load | Upward Force: 8Pe/L² for parabolic tendon
- **Q3(a):** Loss Ratio: effective / initial prestress
- **Q3(b):** Overstressing: initial stress above design to offset losses | Restressing: re-tensioning tendons | Low Relaxation Strand: reduces relaxation loss
- **Q4(a):** Composite Section: precast beam with in-situ slab
- **Q4(b):** Limit State of Collapse: ultimate flexural strength | Limit State of Serviceability: stresses and deflection | Type 1 Member: no tensile stress allowed
- **Q5(a):** Bursting Tension: transverse tension behind anchorage
- **Q5(b):** Transmission Length: length for full prestress transfer | Hoyer Effect: wedging action of strand | Development Length: for ultimate strength
- **Q6(a):** Gifford-Udall System: split cone anchorage
- **Q6(b):** Prestressed Concrete: concrete with induced compressive stresses | Advantages: crack-free members and longer spans | IS 1343: Indian code for prestressed concrete
- **Q7(a):** Elastic Shortening: shortening of concrete at transfer
- **Q7(b):** Shrinkage Loss: due to concrete shrinkage | Creep Loss: due to sustained compressive stress | Relaxation Loss: due to steel relaxation
- **Q8(a):** Hoyer Effect: wedging action of strand
- **Q8(b):** Secondary Moments: due to support restraint | Concordant Cable: produces no secondary reactions | Linear Transformation: changes profile without changing resultant
