# CE-702(A) Prestressed Concrete Structures — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Prestressing principles, systems, losses, flexural and shear design and anchorage zones as per IS 1343.

## Unit 1: Introduction to Prestressing

### Concept of Prestressing

Prestress counteracts tensile stresses.

- **Prestressed Concrete:** concrete with induced compressive stresses
- **Advantages:** crack-free members and longer spans
- **IS 1343:** Indian code for prestressed concrete

### Pre-tensioning

Tendons stressed before casting.

- **Pre-tensioning:** tendons tensioned before concrete is placed
- **Hoyer System:** long-line pre-tensioning
- **Bond Transfer:** prestress transferred by bond

### Post-tensioning

Tendons stressed after concrete hardens.

- **Post-tensioning:** tendons stressed after concrete gains strength
- **Duct:** sheath housing tendon
- **Grouting:** filling ducts after stressing

### Prestressing Systems

Anchorages hold tendons.

- **Freyssinet System:** wedge-type anchorage
- **Magnel-Blaton System:** sandwich plate anchorage
- **Gifford-Udall System:** split cone anchorage

## Unit 2: Materials & Analysis

### High-strength Materials

Prestressing needs strong materials.

- **High Tensile Steel:** strands or wires with high strength
- **Relaxation:** loss of stress at constant strain
- **Concrete Grade:** M30 for post-tensioned, M40 for pre-tensioned

### Stress Concept

Stresses are combined linearly.

- **Stress Concept:** σ = P/A ± Pe y/I ± M y/I
- **Kern Distance:** limit of eccentricity for no tension
- **Pressure Line:** line of resultant compression

### Load Balancing

Prestress balances external loads.

- **Load Balancing Method:** tendon profile balances load
- **Parabolic Tendon:** balances uniformly distributed load
- **Upward Force:** 8Pe/L² for parabolic tendon

### Strength Concept

Internal couple resists moment.

- **Strength Concept:** concrete and steel form internal couple
- **Lever Arm:** distance between compression and tension forces
- **Cracking Moment:** moment causing first crack

## Unit 3: Losses of Prestress

### Immediate Losses

Losses occur during transfer.

- **Elastic Shortening:** shortening of concrete at transfer
- **Anchorage Slip:** loss due to wedge seating
- **Friction Loss:** P = P0 e^(−(μα + kx)) in post-tensioning

### Time-dependent Losses

Losses grow with time.

- **Shrinkage Loss:** due to concrete shrinkage
- **Creep Loss:** due to sustained compressive stress
- **Relaxation Loss:** due to steel relaxation

### Total Loss Estimation

Typical losses are budgeted.

- **Total Loss:** about 15-25% of initial prestress
- **Effective Prestress:** initial prestress minus losses
- **Loss Ratio:** effective / initial prestress

### Control of Losses

Techniques reduce losses.

- **Overstressing:** initial stress above design to offset losses
- **Restressing:** re-tensioning tendons
- **Low Relaxation Strand:** reduces relaxation loss

## Unit 4: Design of Members

### Flexural Design

Members are designed for limit states.

- **Limit State of Collapse:** ultimate flexural strength
- **Limit State of Serviceability:** stresses and deflection
- **Type 1 Member:** no tensile stress allowed

### Shear Design

Prestress improves shear capacity.

- **Shear Cracking:** web shear and flexure shear
- **Vertical Component of Prestress:** reduces shear
- **Shear Reinforcement:** stirrups for excess shear

### Deflection

Prestress creates camber.

- **Camber:** upward deflection due to prestress
- **Long-term Deflection:** due to creep and shrinkage
- **Deflection Limits:** span/250 under total load

### Composite Construction

Precast and cast-in-situ act together.

- **Composite Section:** precast beam with in-situ slab
- **Differential Shrinkage:** induces stresses
- **Propped Construction:** supports during casting

## Unit 5: Anchorage Zones & Applications

### End Block Design

Concentrated forces cause bursting stresses.

- **End Block:** anchorage zone of post-tensioned member
- **Bursting Tension:** transverse tension behind anchorage
- **Spalling:** tension near loaded face

### Transmission Length

Pre-tensioned members transfer by bond.

- **Transmission Length:** length for full prestress transfer
- **Hoyer Effect:** wedging action of strand
- **Development Length:** for ultimate strength

### Continuous Members

Prestressing continuous beams.

- **Secondary Moments:** due to support restraint
- **Concordant Cable:** produces no secondary reactions
- **Linear Transformation:** changes profile without changing resultant

### Applications

Prestressing serves many structures.

- **Prestressed Bridges:** long-span girders
- **Prestressed Sleepers:** railway sleepers
- **Circular Prestressing:** water tanks and pipes
