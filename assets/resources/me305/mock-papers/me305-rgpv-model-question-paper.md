## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Pattern: replica of the casting used to make the mould
- **Q1(b):** Permeability: ability of sand to let gases escape | Riser: reservoir feeding molten metal to compensate shrinkage | Sprue: vertical channel through which metal enters
- **Q2(a):** Angle of Bite: maximum angle for metal to enter rolls
- **Q2(b):** Open Die Forging: metal deformed between flat dies | Closed Die Forging: metal fills a shaped die cavity | Upsetting: increasing cross-section by reducing length
- **Q3(a):** Stripper Plate: removes sheet from the punch
- **Q3(b):** Explosive Forming: explosive energy forms sheet into a die | Electromagnetic Forming: magnetic pulse forms conductive parts | Electrohydraulic Forming: spark discharge in liquid creates pressure
- **Q4(a):** Porosity: gas pockets in weld metal
- **Q4(b):** SMAW: shielded metal arc welding with flux-coated electrode | Arc Length: distance between electrode and workpiece | Polarity: DCEN gives deeper penetration
- **Q5(a):** Compaction: pressing powder into a green compact
- **Q5(b):** Sintering: heating below melting point to bond particles | Self-lubricating Bearing: porous sintered bearing filled with oil | Cemented Carbide: tungsten carbide bonded with cobalt
- **Q6(a):** Hot Tear: crack from restrained shrinkage
- **Q6(b):** Pattern: replica of the casting used to make the mould | Pattern Allowance: extra size for shrinkage, machining and draft | Core: insert forming internal cavities
- **Q7(a):** Blanking: punched piece is the product
- **Q7(b):** Spring Back: elastic recovery after bending | Deep Drawing: forming a cup from a blank | Limiting Draw Ratio: maximum blank to punch diameter ratio
- **Q8(a):** Self-lubricating Bearing: porous sintered bearing filled with oil
- **Q8(b):** Injection Moulding: molten plastic injected into a mould | Blow Moulding: air inflates a parison into a hollow shape | Compression Moulding: charge pressed in a heated mould
