# ES-301 Energy & Environmental Engineering — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Energy Science & Resources

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Renewable Energy*.  
  **Ans.** source replenished naturally on a human timescale, e.g. solar, wind, hydro
- **Q.** Define *Non-renewable Energy*.  
  **Ans.** finite stock such as coal, oil, natural gas and nuclear fuel
- **Q.** Define *Primary Energy*.  
  **Ans.** energy in its natural form before any conversion
- **Q.** Define *Photovoltaic Cell*.  
  **Ans.** semiconductor p-n junction that converts sunlight directly into DC electricity
- **Q.** Define *Flat Plate Collector*.  
  **Ans.** absorber plate under glazing that heats water or air for low-temperature use
- **Q.** Define *Solar Constant*.  
  **Ans.** about 1367 W/m² of radiation received outside Earth's atmosphere
- **Q.** Define *Betz Limit*.  
  **Ans.** maximum theoretical wind-turbine efficiency of 59.3%
- **Q.** Define *Hydroelectric Power*.  
  **Ans.** electricity from water head driving a turbine, P = ρgQH·η
- **Q.** Define *Tidal Energy*.  
  **Ans.** power from the rise and fall of sea level due to lunar gravity
- **Q.** Define *Biogas*.  
  **Ans.** methane-rich gas from anaerobic digestion of organic waste
- **Q.** Define *Geothermal Energy*.  
  **Ans.** heat extracted from the Earth's interior via hot water or steam
- **Q.** Define *Fuel Cell*.  
  **Ans.** electrochemical device converting hydrogen and oxygen into electricity and water

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Energy Systems & Resource Classification* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Energy resources are classed by origin and renewability; engineering decisions weigh availability, cost and environmental impact. Renewable Energy — source replenished naturally on a human timescale, e.g. solar, wind, hydro; Non-renewable Energy — finite stock such as coal, oil, natural gas and nuclear fuel; Primary Energy — energy in its natural form before any conversion.
- **Q.** Explain *Solar Energy* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Solar radiation is harvested as heat (collectors) or electricity (photovoltaics). Photovoltaic Cell — semiconductor p-n junction that converts sunlight directly into DC electricity; Flat Plate Collector — absorber plate under glazing that heats water or air for low-temperature use; Solar Constant — about 1367 W/m² of radiation received outside Earth's atmosphere.
- **Q.** Explain *Wind, Hydro & Tidal Power* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Kinetic and potential energy of moving air and water drive turbines. Betz Limit — maximum theoretical wind-turbine efficiency of 59.3%; Hydroelectric Power — electricity from water head driving a turbine, P = ρgQH·η; Tidal Energy — power from the rise and fall of sea level due to lunar gravity.
- **Q.** Explain *Biomass, Geothermal & Fuel Cells* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Alternative sources convert chemical or earth heat energy into usable power. Biogas — methane-rich gas from anaerobic digestion of organic waste; Geothermal Energy — heat extracted from the Earth's interior via hot water or steam; Fuel Cell — electrochemical device converting hydrogen and oxygen into electricity and water.

## Unit 2: Ecosystems

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Producers*.  
  **Ans.** autotrophs such as green plants that fix solar energy by photosynthesis
- **Q.** Define *Consumers*.  
  **Ans.** heterotrophs that feed on producers or other consumers
- **Q.** Define *Decomposers*.  
  **Ans.** bacteria and fungi that break dead matter into nutrients
- **Q.** Define *Food Chain*.  
  **Ans.** linear sequence of who eats whom in an ecosystem
- **Q.** Define *Food Web*.  
  **Ans.** interlocking network of many food chains
- **Q.** Define *10 Percent Law*.  
  **Ans.** only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level
- **Q.** Define *Pyramid of Energy*.  
  **Ans.** always upright because energy is lost at every level
- **Q.** Define *Ecological Succession*.  
  **Ans.** orderly replacement of communities until a climax community forms
- **Q.** Define *Pioneer Species*.  
  **Ans.** first organisms to colonise bare or disturbed land
- **Q.** Define *Carbon Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** exchange of carbon via photosynthesis, respiration, combustion and decay
- **Q.** Define *Nitrogen Fixation*.  
  **Ans.** conversion of atmospheric N₂ into ammonia by bacteria or lightning
- **Q.** Define *Hydrological Cycle*.  
  **Ans.** continuous movement of water by evaporation, condensation and precipitation

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Structure & Function of an Ecosystem* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with its physical environment. Producers — autotrophs such as green plants that fix solar energy by photosynthesis; Consumers — heterotrophs that feed on producers or other consumers; Decomposers — bacteria and fungi that break dead matter into nutrients.
- **Q.** Explain *Energy Flow & Food Chains* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Energy moves one way through trophic levels while nutrients cycle. Food Chain — linear sequence of who eats whom in an ecosystem; Food Web — interlocking network of many food chains; 10 Percent Law — only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level.
- **Q.** Explain *Ecological Pyramids & Succession* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Pyramids summarise number, biomass and energy at each level; succession is gradual community change. Pyramid of Energy — always upright because energy is lost at every level; Ecological Succession — orderly replacement of communities until a climax community forms; Pioneer Species — first organisms to colonise bare or disturbed land.
- **Q.** Explain *Biogeochemical Cycles* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Matter such as carbon, nitrogen and water cycles between living and non-living parts. Carbon Cycle — exchange of carbon via photosynthesis, respiration, combustion and decay; Nitrogen Fixation — conversion of atmospheric N₂ into ammonia by bacteria or lightning; Hydrological Cycle — continuous movement of water by evaporation, condensation and precipitation.

## Unit 3: Biodiversity & Its Conservation

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Genetic Diversity*.  
  **Ans.** variation of genes within a species
- **Q.** Define *Species Diversity*.  
  **Ans.** variety and abundance of species in a region
- **Q.** Define *Ecosystem Diversity*.  
  **Ans.** variety of habitats and ecological processes
- **Q.** Define *Biodiversity Hotspot*.  
  **Ans.** region with at least 1500 endemic plants that has lost 70% of habitat
- **Q.** Define *Endemic Species*.  
  **Ans.** species found naturally only in one area
- **Q.** Define *Mega-diversity Nation*.  
  **Ans.** country holding a large share of world species; India is one of 17
- **Q.** Define *Habitat Fragmentation*.  
  **Ans.** breaking continuous habitat into isolated patches
- **Q.** Define *Poaching*.  
  **Ans.** illegal hunting of wildlife for trade
- **Q.** Define *Man-Wildlife Conflict*.  
  **Ans.** clashes when animals and people compete for space and resources
- **Q.** Define *In-situ Conservation*.  
  **Ans.** protection in natural habitat, e.g. national parks and biosphere reserves
- **Q.** Define *Ex-situ Conservation*.  
  **Ans.** conservation outside habitat, e.g. zoos, seed banks, botanical gardens
- **Q.** Define *Biosphere Reserve*.  
  **Ans.** protected area with core, buffer and transition zones

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Levels & Value of Biodiversity* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Biodiversity is the variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels. Genetic Diversity — variation of genes within a species; Species Diversity — variety and abundance of species in a region; Ecosystem Diversity — variety of habitats and ecological processes.
- **Q.** Explain *Hotspots & India as a Mega-diversity Nation* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Some regions hold exceptional endemic species under high threat. Biodiversity Hotspot — region with at least 1500 endemic plants that has lost 70% of habitat; Endemic Species — species found naturally only in one area; Mega-diversity Nation — country holding a large share of world species; India is one of 17.
- **Q.** Explain *Threats to Biodiversity* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Habitat loss, poaching and invasive species drive extinction. Habitat Fragmentation — breaking continuous habitat into isolated patches; Poaching — illegal hunting of wildlife for trade; Man-Wildlife Conflict — clashes when animals and people compete for space and resources.
- **Q.** Explain *In-situ & Ex-situ Conservation* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Species are protected either in their natural habitat or outside it. In-situ Conservation — protection in natural habitat, e.g. national parks and biosphere reserves; Ex-situ Conservation — conservation outside habitat, e.g. zoos, seed banks, botanical gardens; Biosphere Reserve — protected area with core, buffer and transition zones.

## Unit 4: Environmental Pollution

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Particulate Matter*.  
  **Ans.** suspended solid or liquid particles such as PM2.5 and PM10
- **Q.** Define *Electrostatic Precipitator*.  
  **Ans.** device that charges particles and collects them on plates
- **Q.** Define *Photochemical Smog*.  
  **Ans.** secondary pollution formed when NOx and hydrocarbons react in sunlight
- **Q.** Define *BOD*.  
  **Ans.** biochemical oxygen demand, oxygen needed by microbes to decompose organic matter
- **Q.** Define *Eutrophication*.  
  **Ans.** excessive nutrient enrichment causing algal blooms and oxygen depletion
- **Q.** Define *Activated Sludge Process*.  
  **Ans.** aerobic secondary treatment using microbial floc
- **Q.** Define *Decibel*.  
  **Ans.** logarithmic unit of sound intensity; above about 85 dB risks hearing damage
- **Q.** Define *Thermal Pollution*.  
  **Ans.** degradation of water quality by discharge of heated water
- **Q.** Define *Soil Erosion*.  
  **Ans.** removal of fertile topsoil by wind or water
- **Q.** Define *Sanitary Landfill*.  
  **Ans.** engineered disposal site with liner and leachate collection
- **Q.** Define *Composting*.  
  **Ans.** aerobic decomposition of organic waste into manure
- **Q.** Define *3R Principle*.  
  **Ans.** reduce, reuse and recycle to minimise waste

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Air Pollution* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Gases and particulates from combustion and industry degrade air quality. Particulate Matter — suspended solid or liquid particles such as PM2.5 and PM10; Electrostatic Precipitator — device that charges particles and collects them on plates; Photochemical Smog — secondary pollution formed when NOx and hydrocarbons react in sunlight.
- **Q.** Explain *Water Pollution & Treatment* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Sewage and industrial effluent contaminate water; treatment restores quality. BOD — biochemical oxygen demand, oxygen needed by microbes to decompose organic matter; Eutrophication — excessive nutrient enrichment causing algal blooms and oxygen depletion; Activated Sludge Process — aerobic secondary treatment using microbial floc.
- **Q.** Explain *Soil, Noise & Thermal Pollution* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Other pollution forms affect land, hearing and aquatic life. Decibel — logarithmic unit of sound intensity; above about 85 dB risks hearing damage; Thermal Pollution — degradation of water quality by discharge of heated water; Soil Erosion — removal of fertile topsoil by wind or water.
- **Q.** Explain *Solid Waste & Hazardous Waste Management* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Waste is reduced, segregated, treated and safely disposed. Sanitary Landfill — engineered disposal site with liner and leachate collection; Composting — aerobic decomposition of organic waste into manure; 3R Principle — reduce, reuse and recycle to minimise waste.

## Unit 5: Social Issues & Environmental Policy

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Greenhouse Effect*.  
  **Ans.** trapping of infrared radiation by gases such as CO₂ and CH₄
- **Q.** Define *Ozone Depletion*.  
  **Ans.** thinning of the stratospheric ozone layer by CFCs
- **Q.** Define *Carbon Footprint*.  
  **Ans.** total greenhouse gas emissions caused by an activity
- **Q.** Define *Sustainable Development*.  
  **Ans.** growth that balances economy, society and environment
- **Q.** Define *Rainwater Harvesting*.  
  **Ans.** collecting and storing rainwater for reuse or recharge
- **Q.** Define *Environmental Impact Assessment*.  
  **Ans.** study of a project's likely environmental effects before approval
- **Q.** Define *Environment Protection Act 1986*.  
  **Ans.** umbrella law empowering the Centre to protect the environment
- **Q.** Define *Water Act 1974*.  
  **Ans.** law for prevention and control of water pollution
- **Q.** Define *Air Act 1981*.  
  **Ans.** law for prevention and control of air pollution
- **Q.** Define *Montreal Protocol*.  
  **Ans.** 1987 treaty phasing out ozone-depleting substances
- **Q.** Define *Kyoto Protocol*.  
  **Ans.** 1997 agreement setting binding emission cuts for developed nations
- **Q.** Define *Paris Agreement*.  
  **Ans.** 2015 pact to limit warming well below 2 °C

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Climate Change & Global Warming* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Greenhouse gases trap heat and change the climate system. Greenhouse Effect — trapping of infrared radiation by gases such as CO₂ and CH₄; Ozone Depletion — thinning of the stratospheric ozone layer by CFCs; Carbon Footprint — total greenhouse gas emissions caused by an activity.
- **Q.** Explain *Sustainable Development* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations. Sustainable Development — growth that balances economy, society and environment; Rainwater Harvesting — collecting and storing rainwater for reuse or recharge; Environmental Impact Assessment — study of a project's likely environmental effects before approval.
- **Q.** Explain *Environmental Laws in India* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Legislation gives powers to prevent and control pollution. Environment Protection Act 1986 — umbrella law empowering the Centre to protect the environment; Water Act 1974 — law for prevention and control of water pollution; Air Act 1981 — law for prevention and control of air pollution.
- **Q.** Explain *International Protocols & Public Awareness* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Global agreements coordinate action on shared environmental problems. Montreal Protocol — 1987 treaty phasing out ozone-depleting substances; Kyoto Protocol — 1997 agreement setting binding emission cuts for developed nations; Paris Agreement — 2015 pact to limit warming well below 2 °C.
