# ES-301 Energy & Environmental Engineering — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Energy resources, ecosystems, biodiversity, pollution control and environmental policy.

## Unit 1: Energy Science & Resources

### Energy Systems & Resource Classification

Energy resources are classed by origin and renewability; engineering decisions weigh availability, cost and environmental impact.

- **Renewable Energy:** source replenished naturally on a human timescale, e.g. solar, wind, hydro
- **Non-renewable Energy:** finite stock such as coal, oil, natural gas and nuclear fuel
- **Primary Energy:** energy in its natural form before any conversion

### Solar Energy

Solar radiation is harvested as heat (collectors) or electricity (photovoltaics).

- **Photovoltaic Cell:** semiconductor p-n junction that converts sunlight directly into DC electricity
- **Flat Plate Collector:** absorber plate under glazing that heats water or air for low-temperature use
- **Solar Constant:** about 1367 W/m² of radiation received outside Earth's atmosphere

### Wind, Hydro & Tidal Power

Kinetic and potential energy of moving air and water drive turbines.

- **Betz Limit:** maximum theoretical wind-turbine efficiency of 59.3%
- **Hydroelectric Power:** electricity from water head driving a turbine, P = ρgQH·η
- **Tidal Energy:** power from the rise and fall of sea level due to lunar gravity

### Biomass, Geothermal & Fuel Cells

Alternative sources convert chemical or earth heat energy into usable power.

- **Biogas:** methane-rich gas from anaerobic digestion of organic waste
- **Geothermal Energy:** heat extracted from the Earth's interior via hot water or steam
- **Fuel Cell:** electrochemical device converting hydrogen and oxygen into electricity and water

## Unit 2: Ecosystems

### Structure & Function of an Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with its physical environment.

- **Producers:** autotrophs such as green plants that fix solar energy by photosynthesis
- **Consumers:** heterotrophs that feed on producers or other consumers
- **Decomposers:** bacteria and fungi that break dead matter into nutrients

### Energy Flow & Food Chains

Energy moves one way through trophic levels while nutrients cycle.

- **Food Chain:** linear sequence of who eats whom in an ecosystem
- **Food Web:** interlocking network of many food chains
- **10 Percent Law:** only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level

### Ecological Pyramids & Succession

Pyramids summarise number, biomass and energy at each level; succession is gradual community change.

- **Pyramid of Energy:** always upright because energy is lost at every level
- **Ecological Succession:** orderly replacement of communities until a climax community forms
- **Pioneer Species:** first organisms to colonise bare or disturbed land

### Biogeochemical Cycles

Matter such as carbon, nitrogen and water cycles between living and non-living parts.

- **Carbon Cycle:** exchange of carbon via photosynthesis, respiration, combustion and decay
- **Nitrogen Fixation:** conversion of atmospheric N₂ into ammonia by bacteria or lightning
- **Hydrological Cycle:** continuous movement of water by evaporation, condensation and precipitation

## Unit 3: Biodiversity & Its Conservation

### Levels & Value of Biodiversity

Biodiversity is the variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels.

- **Genetic Diversity:** variation of genes within a species
- **Species Diversity:** variety and abundance of species in a region
- **Ecosystem Diversity:** variety of habitats and ecological processes

### Hotspots & India as a Mega-diversity Nation

Some regions hold exceptional endemic species under high threat.

- **Biodiversity Hotspot:** region with at least 1500 endemic plants that has lost 70% of habitat
- **Endemic Species:** species found naturally only in one area
- **Mega-diversity Nation:** country holding a large share of world species; India is one of 17

### Threats to Biodiversity

Habitat loss, poaching and invasive species drive extinction.

- **Habitat Fragmentation:** breaking continuous habitat into isolated patches
- **Poaching:** illegal hunting of wildlife for trade
- **Man-Wildlife Conflict:** clashes when animals and people compete for space and resources

### In-situ & Ex-situ Conservation

Species are protected either in their natural habitat or outside it.

- **In-situ Conservation:** protection in natural habitat, e.g. national parks and biosphere reserves
- **Ex-situ Conservation:** conservation outside habitat, e.g. zoos, seed banks, botanical gardens
- **Biosphere Reserve:** protected area with core, buffer and transition zones

## Unit 4: Environmental Pollution

### Air Pollution

Gases and particulates from combustion and industry degrade air quality.

- **Particulate Matter:** suspended solid or liquid particles such as PM2.5 and PM10
- **Electrostatic Precipitator:** device that charges particles and collects them on plates
- **Photochemical Smog:** secondary pollution formed when NOx and hydrocarbons react in sunlight

### Water Pollution & Treatment

Sewage and industrial effluent contaminate water; treatment restores quality.

- **BOD:** biochemical oxygen demand, oxygen needed by microbes to decompose organic matter
- **Eutrophication:** excessive nutrient enrichment causing algal blooms and oxygen depletion
- **Activated Sludge Process:** aerobic secondary treatment using microbial floc

### Soil, Noise & Thermal Pollution

Other pollution forms affect land, hearing and aquatic life.

- **Decibel:** logarithmic unit of sound intensity; above about 85 dB risks hearing damage
- **Thermal Pollution:** degradation of water quality by discharge of heated water
- **Soil Erosion:** removal of fertile topsoil by wind or water

### Solid Waste & Hazardous Waste Management

Waste is reduced, segregated, treated and safely disposed.

- **Sanitary Landfill:** engineered disposal site with liner and leachate collection
- **Composting:** aerobic decomposition of organic waste into manure
- **3R Principle:** reduce, reuse and recycle to minimise waste

## Unit 5: Social Issues & Environmental Policy

### Climate Change & Global Warming

Greenhouse gases trap heat and change the climate system.

- **Greenhouse Effect:** trapping of infrared radiation by gases such as CO₂ and CH₄
- **Ozone Depletion:** thinning of the stratospheric ozone layer by CFCs
- **Carbon Footprint:** total greenhouse gas emissions caused by an activity

### Sustainable Development

Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations.

- **Sustainable Development:** growth that balances economy, society and environment
- **Rainwater Harvesting:** collecting and storing rainwater for reuse or recharge
- **Environmental Impact Assessment:** study of a project's likely environmental effects before approval

### Environmental Laws in India

Legislation gives powers to prevent and control pollution.

- **Environment Protection Act 1986:** umbrella law empowering the Centre to protect the environment
- **Water Act 1974:** law for prevention and control of water pollution
- **Air Act 1981:** law for prevention and control of air pollution

### International Protocols & Public Awareness

Global agreements coordinate action on shared environmental problems.

- **Montreal Protocol:** 1987 treaty phasing out ozone-depleting substances
- **Kyoto Protocol:** 1997 agreement setting binding emission cuts for developed nations
- **Paris Agreement:** 2015 pact to limit warming well below 2 °C
