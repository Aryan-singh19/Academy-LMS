## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Absolute Instrument: gives value from constants, e.g. tangent galvanometer
- **Q1(b):** Controlling Torque: spring or gravity torque opposing deflection | Damping Torque: brings pointer to rest quickly | Eddy Current Damping: damping by induced currents
- **Q2(a):** Repulsion Type: two irons repel each other
- **Q2(b):** Electrodynamometer: no iron, accurate for AC | Transfer Instrument: calibrated on DC, used on AC | Wattmeter Application: current and pressure coils
- **Q3(a):** Meter Constant: revolutions per kWh
- **Q3(b):** Static Energy Meter: electronic metering | Smart Meter: two-way communication meter | Maximum Demand Indicator: records peak demand
- **Q4(a):** Polar Potentiometer: reads magnitude and angle
- **Q4(b):** Wheatstone Bridge: medium resistance | Kelvin Double Bridge: low resistance | Megger: insulation resistance tester
- **Q5(a):** Ratio Error: deviation from nominal ratio
- **Q5(b):** Potential Transformer: primary across line | Phase Angle Error: angle between primary and reversed secondary | Burden: load on secondary in VA
- **Q6(a):** Flux Meter: special ballistic galvanometer for flux
- **Q6(b):** Absolute Instrument: gives value from constants, e.g. tangent galvanometer | Secondary Instrument: calibrated against absolute instrument | Deflecting Torque: torque moving the pointer
- **Q7(a):** Dynamometer Wattmeter: current and potential coils
- **Q7(b):** Two Wattmeter Method: P = W1 + W2 for three-wire systems | Power Factor from Two Wattmeters: tan φ = √3(W1 − W2)/(W1 + W2) | Three Wattmeter Method: for four-wire systems
- **Q8(a):** Phase Angle Error: angle between primary and reversed secondary
- **Q8(b):** Digital Voltmeter: ADC-based voltmeter | Digital Frequency Meter: counts cycles in time gate | Resolution: smallest change detectable
