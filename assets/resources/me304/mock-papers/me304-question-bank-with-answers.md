# ME-304 Strength of Materials — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Simple Stress & Strain

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Normal Stress*.  
  **Ans.** σ = P/A
- **Q.** Define *Hooke's Law*.  
  **Ans.** σ = Eε within the elastic limit
- **Q.** Define *Poisson's Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** lateral strain / longitudinal strain
- **Q.** Define *Young's Modulus*.  
  **Ans.** ratio of normal stress to strain
- **Q.** Define *Modulus of Rigidity*.  
  **Ans.** G = shear stress / shear strain
- **Q.** Define *Relation of Constants*.  
  **Ans.** E = 2G(1 + ν) = 3K(1 − 2ν)
- **Q.** Define *Deformation of Bar*.  
  **Ans.** δ = PL/(AE)
- **Q.** Define *Compound Bar*.  
  **Ans.** bars of different materials sharing a load with equal strain
- **Q.** Define *Thermal Stress*.  
  **Ans.** σ = EαΔT when expansion is prevented
- **Q.** Define *Strain Energy*.  
  **Ans.** U = σ²V/(2E)
- **Q.** Define *Resilience*.  
  **Ans.** strain energy stored up to elastic limit
- **Q.** Define *Impact Loading*.  
  **Ans.** suddenly applied load gives twice the static stress

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Stress, Strain & Hooke's Law* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Loads produce internal stress and deformation. Normal Stress — σ = P/A; Hooke's Law — σ = Eε within the elastic limit; Poisson's Ratio — lateral strain / longitudinal strain.
- **Q.** Explain *Elastic Constants* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Material elasticity is described by related constants. Young's Modulus — ratio of normal stress to strain; Modulus of Rigidity — G = shear stress / shear strain; Relation of Constants — E = 2G(1 + ν) = 3K(1 − 2ν).
- **Q.** Explain *Bars of Varying Section & Compound Bars* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Composite and stepped bars share loads. Deformation of Bar — δ = PL/(AE); Compound Bar — bars of different materials sharing a load with equal strain; Thermal Stress — σ = EαΔT when expansion is prevented.
- **Q.** Explain *Strain Energy* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Energy is stored in deformed members. Strain Energy — U = σ²V/(2E); Resilience — strain energy stored up to elastic limit; Impact Loading — suddenly applied load gives twice the static stress.

## Unit 2: Shear Force & Bending Moment

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Cantilever*.  
  **Ans.** beam fixed at one end, free at the other
- **Q.** Define *Simply Supported Beam*.  
  **Ans.** supported on a pin and roller
- **Q.** Define *UDL*.  
  **Ans.** uniformly distributed load
- **Q.** Define *Shear Force*.  
  **Ans.** algebraic sum of forces on one side of a section
- **Q.** Define *Bending Moment*.  
  **Ans.** algebraic sum of moments on one side
- **Q.** Define *Relation*.  
  **Ans.** dM/dx = V
- **Q.** Define *Point of Contraflexure*.

- **Q.** Define *Maximum Bending Moment*.

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *SFD & BMD* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Diagrams show internal force variation. Shear Force — algebraic sum of forces on one side of a section; Bending Moment — algebraic sum of moments on one side; Relation — dM/dx = V.

## Unit 3: Bending & Shear Stresses

  **Ans.** M/I = σ/y = E/R

  **Ans.** layer with zero bending stress
- **Q.** Define *Section Modulus*.  
  **Ans.** Z = I/ymax
- **Q.** Define *Rectangle I*.  
  **Ans.** bd³/12 about centroidal axis
- **Q.** Define *Parallel Axis Theorem*.  
  **Ans.** I = Ig + Ah²
- **Q.** Define *Circular Section I*.

- **Q.** Define *Shear Stress Formula*.  
  **Ans.** τ = VAȳ/(Ib)

- **Q.** Define *Circular Section*.

- **Q.** Define *Eccentric Load*.

- **Q.** Define *Combined Stress*.

- **Q.** Explain *Shear Stress in Beams* in detail with a neat diagram.

## Unit 4: Torsion & Deflection

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Torsion Equation*.  
  **Ans.** T/J = τ/r = Gθ/L
- **Q.** Define *Polar Moment of Inertia*.  
  **Ans.** J = πd⁴/32 for solid shaft
- **Q.** Define *Power Transmitted*.  
  **Ans.** P = 2πNT/60
- **Q.** Define *Hollow Shaft J*.  
  **Ans.** π(D⁴ − d⁴)/32
- **Q.** Define *Equivalent Torque*.  
  **Ans.** Te = √(M² + T²)
- **Q.** Define *Equivalent Bending Moment*.  
  **Ans.** Me = ½(M + √(M² + T²))
- **Q.** Define *Elastic Curve Equation*.  
  **Ans.** EI d²y/dx² = M
- **Q.** Define *Macaulay's Method*.  
  **Ans.** single equation for beams with multiple loads
- **Q.** Define *Cantilever End Load Deflection*.  
  **Ans.** WL³/(3EI)
- **Q.** Define *Moment Area Method*.  
  **Ans.** slope change equals area of M/EI diagram
- **Q.** Define *Close-coiled Helical Spring Stiffness*.  
  **Ans.** k = Gd⁴/(8D³n)
- **Q.** Define *Leaf Spring*.  
  **Ans.** laminated spring used in vehicles

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Torsion of Shafts* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Shafts transmit torque. Torsion Equation — T/J = τ/r = Gθ/L; Polar Moment of Inertia — J = πd⁴/32 for solid shaft; Power Transmitted — P = 2πNT/60.
- **Q.** Explain *Hollow Shafts & Combined Loading* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Hollow shafts are lighter for the same strength. Hollow Shaft J — π(D⁴ − d⁴)/32; Equivalent Torque — Te = √(M² + T²); Equivalent Bending Moment — Me = ½(M + √(M² + T²)).
- **Q.** Explain *Deflection of Beams* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Deflection is found from the elastic curve. Elastic Curve Equation — EI d²y/dx² = M; Macaulay's Method — single equation for beams with multiple loads; Cantilever End Load Deflection — WL³/(3EI).
- **Q.** Explain *Moment Area & Springs* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Other methods for deflection; springs store energy. Moment Area Method — slope change equals area of M/EI diagram; Close-coiled Helical Spring Stiffness — k = Gd⁴/(8D³n); Leaf Spring — laminated spring used in vehicles.

## Unit 5: Columns, Cylinders & Principal Stresses

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Euler's Formula*.  
  **Ans.** Pcr = π²EI/Le²
- **Q.** Define *Effective Length*.  
  **Ans.** depends on end conditions; Le = L for both ends hinged
- **Q.** Define *Slenderness Ratio*.  
  **Ans.** Le/k
- **Q.** Define *Rankine's Formula*.  
  **Ans.** 1/P = 1/Pc + 1/Pe
- **Q.** Define *Crushing Load*.  
  **Ans.** σc·A for short columns
- **Q.** Define *Rankine Constant*.  
  **Ans.** α depends on material
- **Q.** Define *Hoop Stress*.  
  **Ans.** σh = pd/(2t) for thin cylinder
- **Q.** Define *Longitudinal Stress*.  
  **Ans.** σl = pd/(4t)
- **Q.** Define *Lame's Equations*.  
  **Ans.** stresses in thick cylinders
- **Q.** Define *Principal Stress*.  
  **Ans.** normal stress on plane with zero shear
- **Q.** Define *Maximum Shear Stress*.  
  **Ans.** (σ1 − σ2)/2
- **Q.** Define *Mohr's Circle*.  
  **Ans.** graphical method for stress transformation

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Columns & Struts* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Long compression members fail by buckling. Euler's Formula — Pcr = π²EI/Le²; Effective Length — depends on end conditions; Le = L for both ends hinged; Slenderness Ratio — Le/k.
- **Q.** Explain *Rankine's Formula* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Empirical formula covers short and long columns. Rankine's Formula — 1/P = 1/Pc + 1/Pe; Crushing Load — σc·A for short columns; Rankine Constant — α depends on material.
- **Q.** Explain *Thin & Thick Cylinders* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Pressure vessels carry hoop and longitudinal stress. Hoop Stress — σh = pd/(2t) for thin cylinder; Longitudinal Stress — σl = pd/(4t); Lame's Equations — stresses in thick cylinders.
- **Q.** Explain *Principal Stresses & Mohr's Circle* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Stress transformation finds extreme stresses. Principal Stress — normal stress on plane with zero shear; Maximum Shear Stress — (σ1 − σ2)/2; Mohr's Circle — graphical method for stress transformation.
