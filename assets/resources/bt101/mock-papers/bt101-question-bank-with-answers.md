# BT-101 Engineering Chemistry — Question Bank with Answer Hints

## Unit 1: Water Analysis & Treatment

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Temporary Hardness*.  
  **Ans.** due to bicarbonates; removed by boiling
- **Q.** Define *Permanent Hardness*.  
  **Ans.** due to chlorides and sulphates of Ca and Mg
- **Q.** Define *Hardness Unit*.  
  **Ans.** expressed as mg/L (ppm) of CaCO3 equivalent
- **Q.** Define *EDTA Titration*.  
  **Ans.** complexometric method using Eriochrome Black-T indicator
- **Q.** Define *Alkalinity*.  
  **Ans.** due to OH⁻, CO3²⁻ and HCO3⁻ ions
- **Q.** Define *Phenolphthalein Alkalinity*.  
  **Ans.** alkalinity measured up to pH 8.3
- **Q.** Define *Scale and Sludge*.  
  **Ans.** deposits reducing heat transfer
- **Q.** Define *Priming and Foaming*.  
  **Ans.** carry-over of water droplets with steam
- **Q.** Define *Caustic Embrittlement*.  
  **Ans.** boiler metal cracking due to NaOH
- **Q.** Define *Lime-Soda Process*.  
  **Ans.** adds lime and soda to precipitate hardness
- **Q.** Define *Zeolite Process*.  
  **Ans.** sodium zeolite exchanges Na⁺ for Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺
- **Q.** Define *Ion Exchange*.  
  **Ans.** cation and anion resins give demineralised water

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Hardness of Water* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Dissolved calcium and magnesium salts make water hard. Temporary Hardness — due to bicarbonates; removed by boiling; Permanent Hardness — due to chlorides and sulphates of Ca and Mg; Hardness Unit — expressed as mg/L (ppm) of CaCO3 equivalent.
- **Q.** Explain *EDTA Method & Alkalinity* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Titrations measure hardness and alkalinity. EDTA Titration — complexometric method using Eriochrome Black-T indicator; Alkalinity — due to OH⁻, CO3²⁻ and HCO3⁻ ions; Phenolphthalein Alkalinity — alkalinity measured up to pH 8.3.
- **Q.** Explain *Boiler Troubles* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Hard water causes problems in boilers. Scale and Sludge — deposits reducing heat transfer; Priming and Foaming — carry-over of water droplets with steam; Caustic Embrittlement — boiler metal cracking due to NaOH.
- **Q.** Explain *Softening Methods* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Hardness is removed by chemical or ion exchange processes. Lime-Soda Process — adds lime and soda to precipitate hardness; Zeolite Process — sodium zeolite exchanges Na⁺ for Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺; Ion Exchange — cation and anion resins give demineralised water.

## Unit 4: Corrosion & Electrochemistry

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Galvanic Cell*.  
  **Ans.** spontaneous redox reaction produces current
- **Q.** Define *Nernst Equation*.  
  **Ans.** E = E° − (0.0591/n) log Q at 25 °C
- **Q.** Define *Standard Electrode Potential*.  
  **Ans.** potential versus standard hydrogen electrode
- **Q.** Define *Primary Cell*.  
  **Ans.** non-rechargeable, e.g. dry cell
- **Q.** Define *Secondary Cell*.  
  **Ans.** rechargeable, e.g. lead-acid and Li-ion
- **Q.** Define *Hydrogen-Oxygen Fuel Cell*.  
  **Ans.** produces electricity and water
- **Q.** Define *Dry Corrosion*.  
  **Ans.** direct chemical attack by gases
- **Q.** Define *Wet Corrosion*.  
  **Ans.** electrochemical corrosion in moisture
- **Q.** Define *Galvanic Corrosion*.  
  **Ans.** when two dissimilar metals contact in electrolyte
- **Q.** Define *Cathodic Protection*.  
  **Ans.** making the metal a cathode
- **Q.** Define *Galvanising*.  
  **Ans.** coating iron with zinc
- **Q.** Define *Sacrificial Anode*.  
  **Ans.** more active metal corrodes instead

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Electrochemical Cells* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Chemical energy is converted into electricity. Galvanic Cell — spontaneous redox reaction produces current; Nernst Equation — E = E° − (0.0591/n) log Q at 25 °C; Standard Electrode Potential — potential versus standard hydrogen electrode.
- **Q.** Explain *Batteries & Fuel Cells* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Practical electrochemical energy sources. Primary Cell — non-rechargeable, e.g. dry cell; Secondary Cell — rechargeable, e.g. lead-acid and Li-ion; Hydrogen-Oxygen Fuel Cell — produces electricity and water.
- **Q.** Explain *Corrosion Mechanisms* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Metals degrade by reaction with environment. Dry Corrosion — direct chemical attack by gases; Wet Corrosion — electrochemical corrosion in moisture; Galvanic Corrosion — when two dissimilar metals contact in electrolyte.
- **Q.** Explain *Corrosion Prevention* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Corrosion is controlled by design and coatings. Cathodic Protection — making the metal a cathode; Galvanising — coating iron with zinc; Sacrificial Anode — more active metal corrodes instead.
