## Answer Hints (Set A)

- **Q1(a):** Temporary Hardness: due to bicarbonates; removed by boiling
- **Q1(b):** EDTA Titration: complexometric method using Eriochrome Black-T indicator | Alkalinity: due to OH⁻, CO3²⁻ and HCO3⁻ ions | Phenolphthalein Alkalinity: alkalinity measured up to pH 8.3
- **Q2(a):** Ultimate Analysis: C, H, N, S and O percentages
- **Q2(b):** Fractional Distillation: separates crude oil by boiling range | Cracking: breaking large hydrocarbons into smaller ones | Octane Number: knock resistance of petrol
- **Q3(a):** Thermosetting Polymer: sets permanently on heating, e.g. bakelite
- **Q3(b):** Vulcanisation: cross-linking rubber with sulphur | Conducting Polymer: polymer that conducts electricity, e.g. polyaniline | Biodegradable Polymer: decomposes naturally
- **Q4(a):** Cathodic Protection: making the metal a cathode
- **Q4(b):** Galvanic Cell: spontaneous redox reaction produces current | Nernst Equation: E = E° − (0.0591/n) log Q at 25 °C | Standard Electrode Potential: potential versus standard hydrogen electrode
- **Q5(a):** UV-Visible Spectroscopy: electronic transitions in molecules
- **Q5(b):** Chromatography: separation between mobile and stationary phases | Rf Value: distance by solute / distance by solvent | Gas Chromatography: separation of volatile compounds
- **Q6(a):** Ion Exchange: cation and anion resins give demineralised water
- **Q6(b):** Temporary Hardness: due to bicarbonates; removed by boiling | Permanent Hardness: due to chlorides and sulphates of Ca and Mg | Hardness Unit: expressed as mg/L (ppm) of CaCO3 equivalent
- **Q7(a):** Fluid Film Lubrication: thick film separates surfaces
- **Q7(b):** Viscosity Index: change of viscosity with temperature | Flash and Fire Point: temperatures where vapours ignite | Cloud and Pour Point: low-temperature flow properties
- **Q8(a):** Rf Value: distance by solute / distance by solvent
- **Q8(b):** TGA: thermogravimetric analysis measures mass change | DTA: differential thermal analysis | DSC: differential scanning calorimetry
