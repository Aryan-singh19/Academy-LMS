# ME-404 Fluid Mechanics — Unit-wise Study Notes

> Fluid properties, statics, kinematics, dynamics, flow measurement, viscous flow, boundary layer and dimensional analysis.

## Unit 1: Fluid Properties & Statics

### Properties of Fluids

Fluids deform continuously under shear.

- **Viscosity:** resistance to shear; τ = μ du/dy
- **Newtonian Fluid:** shear stress proportional to velocity gradient
- **Surface Tension:** force per unit length at a liquid surface

### Pressure & Its Measurement

Pressure acts equally in all directions at a point.

- **Pascal's Law:** pressure at a point is equal in all directions
- **Hydrostatic Law:** dp/dz = −ρg
- **U-tube Manometer:** measures pressure using liquid columns

### Hydrostatic Forces

Submerged surfaces experience pressure forces.

- **Total Pressure Force:** F = ρgAh̄
- **Centre of Pressure:** depth h* = h̄ + I/(Ah̄)
- **Curved Surface Force:** horizontal and vertical components found separately

### Buoyancy & Stability

Floating bodies obey Archimedes' principle.

- **Archimedes' Principle:** buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid
- **Metacentric Height:** GM; positive for stable floating
- **Metacentre:** point about which a floating body oscillates

## Unit 2: Fluid Kinematics

### Types of Flow

Flows are classified by variation and nature.

- **Steady Flow:** properties at a point do not change with time
- **Uniform Flow:** velocity same at every point
- **Laminar Flow:** fluid moves in smooth layers

### Streamlines & Continuity

Streamlines visualise flow; continuity conserves mass.

- **Streamline:** curve tangent to velocity everywhere
- **Continuity Equation:** A1V1 = A2V2 for incompressible flow
- **Stream Tube:** bundle of streamlines

### Velocity Potential & Stream Function

Potential flow uses φ and ψ.

- **Velocity Potential:** φ with u = −∂φ/∂x
- **Stream Function:** ψ, constant along streamlines
- **Flow Net:** grid of streamlines and equipotential lines

### Rotation & Vorticity

Fluid elements may rotate.

- **Vorticity:** twice the angular velocity
- **Irrotational Flow:** zero vorticity
- **Circulation:** line integral of velocity around a closed curve

## Unit 3: Fluid Dynamics

### Euler's & Bernoulli's Equations

Energy along a streamline is conserved.

- **Euler's Equation:** dp/ρ + V dV + g dz = 0
- **Bernoulli's Equation:** p/ρg + V²/2g + z = constant
- **Assumptions:** steady, incompressible, inviscid flow along a streamline

### Flow Measurement Devices

Bernoulli explains flow meters.

- **Venturimeter:** measures discharge using pressure difference
- **Pitot Tube:** measures velocity from stagnation pressure
- **Orifice Meter:** simple plate meter with higher losses

### Momentum Equation

Momentum principle gives forces from flow.

- **Impulse-Momentum Equation:** F = ρQ(V2 − V1)
- **Force on Pipe Bend:** found from momentum change
- **Jet Impact on Plate:** F = ρaV² on a fixed flat plate

### Notches & Weirs

Open-channel flow is measured by notches.

- **Rectangular Notch:** Q = (2/3)Cd·L·√(2g)·H^(3/2)
- **V-notch:** Q = (8/15)Cd·tan(θ/2)√(2g)H^(5/2)
- **Weir:** notch on a large scale

## Unit 4: Viscous Flow & Pipe Flow

### Laminar Flow in Pipes

Viscous flow obeys Hagen-Poiseuille law.

- **Hagen-Poiseuille Equation:** Δp = 32μVL/D²
- **Reynolds Number:** Re = ρVD/μ; laminar below about 2000
- **Velocity Profile:** parabolic in laminar pipe flow

### Turbulent Flow & Friction

Turbulence increases losses.

- **Darcy-Weisbach Equation:** hf = fLV²/(2gD)
- **Moody Chart:** friction factor versus Re and relative roughness
- **Turbulent Velocity Profile:** flatter than laminar profile

### Minor Losses & Pipe Networks

Fittings add losses; pipes form networks.

- **Sudden Expansion Loss:** (V1 − V2)²/2g
- **Pipes in Series:** same flow, losses add
- **Pipes in Parallel:** same head loss, flows add

### Water Hammer

Sudden valve closure causes pressure surges.

- **Water Hammer:** pressure rise from sudden stopping of flow
- **Pressure Wave Speed:** depends on fluid bulk modulus and pipe elasticity
- **Surge Tank:** absorbs pressure surges in pipelines

## Unit 5: Boundary Layer & Dimensional Analysis

### Boundary Layer Concept

Viscous effects are confined near surfaces.

- **Boundary Layer:** thin layer where velocity rises from zero to free stream
- **Boundary Layer Thickness:** distance where u = 0.99U
- **Displacement Thickness:** shift of streamlines due to the boundary layer

### Flow Separation & Drag

Adverse pressure gradients cause separation.

- **Flow Separation:** boundary layer detaches from surface
- **Drag Force:** FD = CD·½ρV²A
- **Lift Force:** force perpendicular to flow direction

### Dimensional Analysis

Dimensionless groups reduce variables.

- **Buckingham π Theorem:** n variables and m dimensions give n − m π groups
- **Rayleigh's Method:** finds relation using exponents of variables
- **Dimensional Homogeneity:** each term has same dimensions

### Model Analysis & Similitude

Models predict prototype behaviour.

- **Geometric Similarity:** model and prototype shapes in fixed ratio
- **Froude Number:** V/√(gL), used for free-surface flows
- **Reynolds Model Law:** equal Re for viscous-dominated flows
