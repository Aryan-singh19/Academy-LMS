## Unit 1: Fluid Properties & Statics

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Viscosity*.  
  **Ans.** resistance to shear; τ = μ du/dy
- **Q.** Define *Newtonian Fluid*.  
  **Ans.** shear stress proportional to velocity gradient
- **Q.** Define *Surface Tension*.  
  **Ans.** force per unit length at a liquid surface
- **Q.** Define *Pascal's Law*.  
  **Ans.** pressure at a point is equal in all directions
- **Q.** Define *Hydrostatic Law*.  
  **Ans.** dp/dz = −ρg
- **Q.** Define *U-tube Manometer*.  
  **Ans.** measures pressure using liquid columns
- **Q.** Define *Total Pressure Force*.  
  **Ans.** F = ρgAh̄
- **Q.** Define *Centre of Pressure*.  
  **Ans.** depth h* = h̄ + I/(Ah̄)
- **Q.** Define *Curved Surface Force*.  
  **Ans.** horizontal and vertical components found separately
- **Q.** Define *Archimedes' Principle*.  
  **Ans.** buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid
- **Q.** Define *Metacentric Height*.  
  **Ans.** GM; positive for stable floating
- **Q.** Define *Metacentre*.  
  **Ans.** point about which a floating body oscillates

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Properties of Fluids* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Fluids deform continuously under shear. Viscosity — resistance to shear; τ = μ du/dy; Newtonian Fluid — shear stress proportional to velocity gradient; Surface Tension — force per unit length at a liquid surface.
- **Q.** Explain *Pressure & Its Measurement* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Pressure acts equally in all directions at a point. Pascal's Law — pressure at a point is equal in all directions; Hydrostatic Law — dp/dz = −ρg; U-tube Manometer — measures pressure using liquid columns.
- **Q.** Explain *Hydrostatic Forces* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Submerged surfaces experience pressure forces. Total Pressure Force — F = ρgAh̄; Centre of Pressure — depth h* = h̄ + I/(Ah̄); Curved Surface Force — horizontal and vertical components found separately.
- **Q.** Explain *Buoyancy & Stability* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Floating bodies obey Archimedes' principle. Archimedes' Principle — buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid; Metacentric Height — GM; positive for stable floating; Metacentre — point about which a floating body oscillates.

## Unit 2: Fluid Kinematics

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Steady Flow*.  
  **Ans.** properties at a point do not change with time
- **Q.** Define *Uniform Flow*.  
  **Ans.** velocity same at every point
- **Q.** Define *Laminar Flow*.  
  **Ans.** fluid moves in smooth layers
- **Q.** Define *Streamline*.  
  **Ans.** curve tangent to velocity everywhere
- **Q.** Define *Continuity Equation*.  
  **Ans.** A1V1 = A2V2 for incompressible flow
- **Q.** Define *Stream Tube*.  
  **Ans.** bundle of streamlines
- **Q.** Define *Velocity Potential*.  
  **Ans.** φ with u = −∂φ/∂x
- **Q.** Define *Stream Function*.  
  **Ans.** ψ, constant along streamlines
- **Q.** Define *Flow Net*.  
  **Ans.** grid of streamlines and equipotential lines
- **Q.** Define *Vorticity*.  
  **Ans.** twice the angular velocity
- **Q.** Define *Irrotational Flow*.  
  **Ans.** zero vorticity
- **Q.** Define *Circulation*.  
  **Ans.** line integral of velocity around a closed curve

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Types of Flow* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Flows are classified by variation and nature. Steady Flow — properties at a point do not change with time; Uniform Flow — velocity same at every point; Laminar Flow — fluid moves in smooth layers.
- **Q.** Explain *Streamlines & Continuity* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Streamlines visualise flow; continuity conserves mass. Streamline — curve tangent to velocity everywhere; Continuity Equation — A1V1 = A2V2 for incompressible flow; Stream Tube — bundle of streamlines.
- **Q.** Explain *Velocity Potential & Stream Function* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Potential flow uses φ and ψ. Velocity Potential — φ with u = −∂φ/∂x; Stream Function — ψ, constant along streamlines; Flow Net — grid of streamlines and equipotential lines.
- **Q.** Explain *Rotation & Vorticity* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Fluid elements may rotate. Vorticity — twice the angular velocity; Irrotational Flow — zero vorticity; Circulation — line integral of velocity around a closed curve.

- **Q.** Define *Bernoulli's Equation*.

- **Q.** Define *Assumptions*.  
  **Ans.** steady, incompressible, inviscid flow along a streamline
- **Q.** Define *Venturimeter*.  
  **Ans.** measures discharge using pressure difference
- **Q.** Define *Pitot Tube*.  
  **Ans.** measures velocity from stagnation pressure
- **Q.** Define *Orifice Meter*.  
  **Ans.** simple plate meter with higher losses
- **Q.** Define *Impulse-Momentum Equation*.

- **Q.** Define *Force on Pipe Bend*.

- **Q.** Define *Jet Impact on Plate*.

- **Q.** Define *Rectangular Notch*.

- **Q.** Define *V-notch*.

- **Q.** Explain *Flow Measurement Devices* in detail with a neat diagram.

- **Q.** Explain *Momentum Equation* in detail with a neat diagram.

- **Q.** Explain *Notches & Weirs* in detail with a neat diagram.

## Unit 4: Viscous Flow & Pipe Flow

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Hagen-Poiseuille Equation*.  
  **Ans.** Δp = 32μVL/D²
- **Q.** Define *Reynolds Number*.  
  **Ans.** Re = ρVD/μ; laminar below about 2000
- **Q.** Define *Velocity Profile*.  
  **Ans.** parabolic in laminar pipe flow
- **Q.** Define *Darcy-Weisbach Equation*.  
  **Ans.** hf = fLV²/(2gD)
- **Q.** Define *Moody Chart*.  
  **Ans.** friction factor versus Re and relative roughness
- **Q.** Define *Turbulent Velocity Profile*.  
  **Ans.** flatter than laminar profile
- **Q.** Define *Sudden Expansion Loss*.  
  **Ans.** (V1 − V2)²/2g
- **Q.** Define *Pipes in Series*.  
  **Ans.** same flow, losses add
- **Q.** Define *Pipes in Parallel*.  
  **Ans.** same head loss, flows add
- **Q.** Define *Water Hammer*.  
  **Ans.** pressure rise from sudden stopping of flow
- **Q.** Define *Pressure Wave Speed*.  
  **Ans.** depends on fluid bulk modulus and pipe elasticity
- **Q.** Define *Surge Tank*.  
  **Ans.** absorbs pressure surges in pipelines

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Laminar Flow in Pipes* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Viscous flow obeys Hagen-Poiseuille law. Hagen-Poiseuille Equation — Δp = 32μVL/D²; Reynolds Number — Re = ρVD/μ; laminar below about 2000; Velocity Profile — parabolic in laminar pipe flow.
- **Q.** Explain *Turbulent Flow & Friction* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Turbulence increases losses. Darcy-Weisbach Equation — hf = fLV²/(2gD); Moody Chart — friction factor versus Re and relative roughness; Turbulent Velocity Profile — flatter than laminar profile.
- **Q.** Explain *Minor Losses & Pipe Networks* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Fittings add losses; pipes form networks. Sudden Expansion Loss — (V1 − V2)²/2g; Pipes in Series — same flow, losses add; Pipes in Parallel — same head loss, flows add.
- **Q.** Explain *Water Hammer* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Sudden valve closure causes pressure surges. Water Hammer — pressure rise from sudden stopping of flow; Pressure Wave Speed — depends on fluid bulk modulus and pipe elasticity; Surge Tank — absorbs pressure surges in pipelines.

## Unit 5: Boundary Layer & Dimensional Analysis

### Short answer (2-3 marks)

- **Q.** Define *Boundary Layer*.  
  **Ans.** thin layer where velocity rises from zero to free stream
- **Q.** Define *Boundary Layer Thickness*.  
  **Ans.** distance where u = 0.99U
- **Q.** Define *Displacement Thickness*.  
  **Ans.** shift of streamlines due to the boundary layer
- **Q.** Define *Flow Separation*.  
  **Ans.** boundary layer detaches from surface
- **Q.** Define *Drag Force*.  
  **Ans.** FD = CD·½ρV²A
- **Q.** Define *Lift Force*.  
  **Ans.** force perpendicular to flow direction
- **Q.** Define *Buckingham π Theorem*.  
  **Ans.** n variables and m dimensions give n − m π groups
- **Q.** Define *Rayleigh's Method*.  
  **Ans.** finds relation using exponents of variables
- **Q.** Define *Dimensional Homogeneity*.  
  **Ans.** each term has same dimensions
- **Q.** Define *Geometric Similarity*.  
  **Ans.** model and prototype shapes in fixed ratio
- **Q.** Define *Froude Number*.  
  **Ans.** V/√(gL), used for free-surface flows
- **Q.** Define *Reynolds Model Law*.  
  **Ans.** equal Re for viscous-dominated flows

### Long answer (7 marks)

- **Q.** Explain *Boundary Layer Concept* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Viscous effects are confined near surfaces. Boundary Layer — thin layer where velocity rises from zero to free stream; Boundary Layer Thickness — distance where u = 0.99U; Displacement Thickness — shift of streamlines due to the boundary layer.
- **Q.** Explain *Flow Separation & Drag* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Adverse pressure gradients cause separation. Flow Separation — boundary layer detaches from surface; Drag Force — FD = CD·½ρV²A; Lift Force — force perpendicular to flow direction.
- **Q.** Explain *Dimensional Analysis* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Dimensionless groups reduce variables. Buckingham π Theorem — n variables and m dimensions give n − m π groups; Rayleigh's Method — finds relation using exponents of variables; Dimensional Homogeneity — each term has same dimensions.
- **Q.** Explain *Model Analysis & Similitude* in detail with a neat diagram.  
  **Key points:** Models predict prototype behaviour. Geometric Similarity — model and prototype shapes in fixed ratio; Froude Number — V/√(gL), used for free-surface flows; Reynolds Model Law — equal Re for viscous-dominated flows.
